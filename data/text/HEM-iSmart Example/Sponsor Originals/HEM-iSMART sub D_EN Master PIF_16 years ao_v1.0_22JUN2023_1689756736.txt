1.  What is the purpose of the trial?

This trial consists of two phases: Phase 1 and Phase 2. Your doctor will be able to tell you which phase the trial is in currently. The purpose of Phase 1 is to examine which dose of the study medication, trametinib, combined with dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine, is safe in patients whose symptoms did not properly respond to previous courses of treatment (i.e. who have refractory symptoms) or who have experienced a relapse (return of the disease). In Phase 1 of this trial, we will investigate two different doses of the medication. We will provide more information on this in Chapter 4 of this letter. Your doctor will be able to tell you which dose is currently being tested.

Trametinib has previously been administered to patients suffering from various types of cancer: a certain type of lung cancer, skin cancer and thyroid cancer. So far, nearly 2,000 people have been administered this medication. Trametinib has not yet been approved for use in children, but has previously been tested in children with other forms of cancer, and in adults with leukaemia and other types of cancer. Those studies showed that trametinib can be administered in combination with chemotherapy, and also that it can be safely administered to children.

Dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine are medications that are used in the standard treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) and lymphoblastic lymphoma (LL). You have probably been administered these medications before. However, this particular combination of these medications has never been investigated before, in children or adult patients.

If it is found that the combination of these medications is safe, the investigators will proceed to Phase 2, in which they will focus on whether the combination is effective in treating the disease.

3.  Background information on the trial

Leukaemia or lymphoma cells may be subject to changes (mutations) that cause cancer cells to develop more quickly and keep growing afterwards. Known changes that have such an effect are mutations in different genes known as the ‘RAS signalling pathway’. In the rest of this letter, we will refer to these mutations as ‘RAS mutations’. Patients who have been diagnosed with a RAS mutation tend to be harder to treat than those without such mutations.

Trametinib is a medication that blocks these RAS mutations and, as a result, may slow down the growth of cancer cells. RAS mutations have been found in your leukaemia cells. We hope that trametinib will fight off your disease to such an extent that you will be able to undergo a stem cell transplant (if you are eligible for one). The doctor will discuss this with you. Dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine are medications that may break down leukaemia and lymphoma cells. Laboratory trials in ALL cells have shown that the combination of trametinib and dexamethasone is more effective than either trametinib or dexamethasone alone, since the two medications enhance each other’s effect.

3.  Course of the trial

How long will the trial last?

Are you taking part in the trial? If so, depending on the number of cycles you will undergo, the course of treatment with trametinib will last between 1 and 6 months. If your leukaemia responds well to the course of treatment, the therapy may be extended at the end of the trial. Once the course of treatment has been completed, we would like to keep monitoring you, until 1 year after the start of the therapy.

Step 1: are you a suitable candidate for this trial?

The first thing we wish to determine is whether you are a suitable candidate for the trial. We will do so during the screening period. The investigator will perform several examinations to determine your eligibility. For instance, a physical examination, a thorough look at your medical history, a blood test, a bone marrow biopsy, a lumbar puncture, a urinalysis, an X-ray (or a CT scan) of the lungs, an electrocardiogram, an echocardiogram, a pregnancy test where relevant, a questionnaire and an eye examination. Furthermore, we will discuss any other medication(s) you may be taking.

In Appendix C you will find a list of all the examinations to be performed. Most of these tests are also performed in the standard therapy, and we will be collecting the results of these tests as part of this trial.

Some of the tests are not part of the standard therapy and are specifically performed as part of the trial. Tests in this category are marked as such in the overview of tests to be performed presented in Appendix C.

Once all these examinations have been performed, the investigator will discuss the results with you and tell you whether you are eligible for the trametinib trial. You will only be allowed to take part in the trial if a RAS mutation is found and if he/she meets all the inclusion criteria. If the screening results are such that you will not be allowed to take part, the treating physician will discuss the available alternatives with you.

Step 2: the study treatment

In this trial, you will be administered trametinib combined with standard chemotherapy. You will receive this combination for about 1 to 6 months. Trametinib comes as a tablet formulation. At the start of each new cycle, we will have another look at the right dose for you, based on your age and body weight. You will have to take this medication once daily. Twenty-four hours must pass between each administration of the medication. More information on taking the medication can be found in the medication diary, in which you will record daily when you take the medication.

The amount and/or frequency of the dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine to be given to you will depend on the dosage being investigated. There will be two different dosages: Dose Level 1 and Dose Level -1. We will start the trial by administering the highest dose level (Dose Level 1). We will use the same dose of trametinib in both dose levels. If Dose Level 1 is not well tolerated, we will reduce the amount of dexamethasone administered in Dose Level -1.

At the end of the first 28-day cycle, we will evaluate whether the course of treatment is sufficiently effective with regard to your disease and whether you are fit enough to undergo another cycle. If this is the case, you will be started on Cycle 2.

Dose Level 1: In Cycle 1, you will be administered trametinib once daily for 28 days, in combination with dexamethasone and standard chemotherapy. In both cycles, you will receive dexamethasone for the initial five days. Dexamethasone is ingested in tablet form or administered intravenously. In other words, it can either be taken at home or be administered in hospital.

In Cycle 1, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 3, and cytarabine on Days 5-8 and on Days 12-15. In Cycle 2, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 1, and cytarabine on Days 3-6 and on Days 10-13. If you feel good enough, the medication can be given as a day treatment, not requiring an overnight stay. If not, you will be admitted to hospital for the administration of the medication. At the start of each cycle, you will probably need to spend a few days in hospital.

Step 3: examinations and tests

As part of this trial, you will have to visit the hospital every week if he/she is not actually staying overnight at the hospital. Each session will last about 3 hours.

We will perform the following examinations:

- Physical examination. For instance, the investigator will listen to your heart and lungs and measure your blood pressure.

- Blood test. Your blood will be collected for testing purposes. Two 2ml tubes of blood will be taken each time.

- Urinalysis

  On Day 14 of Cycle 1, blood and cerebrospinal fluid will be collected several times to determine the medicine blood level. This is called a pharmacokinetic assay. When we perform a pharmacokinetic assay, we analyse the amount of medication in the body at various points after administration of the medication. In this way, we seek to determine whether we are administering the right dose. We will collect blood once before trametinib dosing, and then three more times, at 4, 6 and 8 hours after trametinib dosing. On Day 14 of Cycle 1, we will also collect some cerebrospinal fluid (once).

Cerebrospinal fluid can be found around the brain and spinal cord. You will be given a sedative and local anaesthesia when we draw cerebrospinal fluid. A hollow needle will be inserted between two vertebrae into the space just behind them, which is filled with cerebrospinal fluid. The cerebrospinal fluid will be collected in a tube.

At the end of each 28-day trametinib cycle, additional tests will be performed to see if the treatment has been effective.

- You will be asked to complete a questionnaire (only at the start and end of Cycle 1). The questions will be about your quality of life.

  Bone marrow examination

- A scan (e.g. a CT scan), only if the leukaemia or lymphoma has spread to other parts of the body

- Cerebrospinal fluid analysis

- Blood test

- Urinalysis

- Electrocardiogram (ECG) and echocardiogram

- Eye examination (if necessary)

Bone marrow can be found in the innermost part of our bones. This is where blood is produced. You will be sedated when the bone marrow biopsy is performed, so you will not feel a thing. You will receive information on the examination and the sedative beforehand. A tiny needle is used to suction a small piece of bone marrow from the pelvis. Sometimes a small piece of bone will be collected, as well. The bone marrow and the piece of bone will be examined in a laboratory. A plaster will be affixed to the spot where the biopsy is performed.

Step 4: follow-up

We would like to perform one last check-up 30 days after the final dose of study medication is administered. We would also like to keep monitoring you, until 1 year after the start of the course of treatment. As part of the monitoring procedure, you will have to come to hospital about four times a year. The monitoring appointments will coincide with regular follow-up appointments. During these appointments, we will physically examine you and check how you are doing.

3.  What kinds of side effects, adverse effects or discomforts may you experience?

The study medications (trametinib, dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine) may cause side effects or adverse effects.
Since you have leukaemia or lymphoma, you probably do not have many immune cells – a problem with which you will be familiar from previous courses of treatment.

Contact the investigator at once if you experience any of the following adverse effects:

- fever

- feeling poorly or extremely fatigued

- nausea or vomiting

- impaired vision (blurred vision)

The following side effects are very common in people who take trametinib (observed in 1 or more in 10 people):

- High blood pressure

- Bleeding

- Coughing

- Chest tightness

- Diarrhoea

- Nausea

- Vomiting

- Constipation

- Abdominal pain

- Dry mouth

- Skin rash

- A type of acne (pimples)

- Dry skin

- Itching

- Hair loss

- Fatigue

- Swelling of hands or feet

- Fever

- Abnormal blood liver results

The study medication may also have side effects we are currently unaware of. For this reason, you are kindly requested to record any symptoms you may experience in the diary. Please call the investigator or clinical research nurse if you experience any symptoms that are new or serious, or if you have any concerns.

For a more complete overview of known side effects, refer to Appendix D.

3.  What are the benefits and risks of taking part in the trial?

Taking part in the trial may be both beneficial and detrimental to your health. We will list the benefits and risks below. We advise you to carefully consider them and discuss them with others.

Trametinib may reduce your symptoms, but this is by no means guaranteed. You may grow worse at any point during the course of the trial.

By participating, you are assisting us in the search for better therapies against relapsed leukaemia or lymphoma. In the best-case scenario, the chemotherapy will prove to be effective, meaning that you will be able to undergo a stem cell transplant. The investigator will be able to tell you more about your prospects of recovery.

Participation in the trial may come with the following risks:

- You may experience the side effects or adverse effects associated with trametinib as specified in Section 6 and Appendix D. The other medications to be administered during this trial also come with side effects, with which you will be familiar from your previous course of treatment.

  You may experience some discomfort due to the tests performed during the trial. For instance, the collection of blood may be a little painful. In most children, we will use the PAC or Hickman line for blood sampling – a method you are already used to. We will also combine blood sampling for trial purposes with regular blood sampling where possible.

- Taking part in the trial will require extra time from you.

- If you are not admitted to hospital for overnight stays, you will have to come to hospital every week.

- You will have to honour the arrangements you must commit to as part of the trial.

What if you don't wish to take part?

Whether or not you will take part in the trial is entirely up to you. If you’d rather did not to take part, the doctor will discuss the alternative treatment options available to you. He/she will also discuss the benefits and risks associated with each treatment option.

3.  When will the trial end?

If any new information about the trial becomes available that might be important for you to know, the investigator will let you know. After informing you, the investigator will ask you if you want to continue taking part.

You will stop participating in the trial in any of the following situations:

- Once all the scheduled examinations have been performed.

- In the event that you want to stop taking part in the trial. Or in the event that you want to stop taking part in the trial. You can decide to withdraw at any time. If you decide to withdraw, please notify the investigator at once. You do not have to explain why you are withdrawing from the trial. If you withdraw, your doctor will discuss the alternatives with you. For the sake of your safety, the doctor may schedule one or more follow-up check-ups.

- Or in the event that the doctor believes that it would be better for you to withdraw from the trial. If this is the case, the investigator will still invite you to attend a follow-up check-up.

- Or in the event that any of the following authorities decides that the trial must be terminated:

  - the sponsor, for safety reasons or if the medication is found to be insufficiently effective, or

  - a government agency, or

  - the medical ethics review committee that evaluated the trial.

What happens if you withdraw from the trial?

The investigators will use the data and bodily materials (blood, cerebrospinal fluid and bone marrow) that were collected in the period leading up to the withdrawal. If you wish, the bodily materials collected can be destroyed. If this is what you want, please notify the investigator accordingly.

The trial in its entirety will be completed once all the participants have completed their course of treatment.

3.  What happens after the trial?

Can you continue to take these medications?

You are eligible for a one-to-six-month supply of the study medications. If your leukaemia responds well to the medications, the therapy may be continued afterwards. The investigator will discuss with you whether the therapy can be extended or what other types of therapy are available to you.

Will you receive the results of the trial?

The key results of the trial will be known approximately one year after the final patient has commenced the trial. You can receive this information from the investigator upon request.

Appendix D: Overview of known side effects

Potential side effects, adverse effects and discomforts associated with trametinib

Very common (at least 1 in 10 patients may experience these side effects):

- High blood pressure

- Bleeding (external bleeding, pain, swelling, nausea and vomiting)

- Coughing

- Chest tightness

- Diarrhoea

- Nausea

- Vomiting

- Constipation

- Abdominal pain

- Dry mouth

- Skin rash

- Some type of acne

- Dry skin

- Itching

- Hair loss

- Fatigue

- Swelling of hands or feet

- Fever

  Abnormal liver function, which may mean that the liver is not functioning properly; such abnormalities tend to be mild and reversible but can be severe or life-threatening. Your liver function will be checked regularly during the course of the trial. Please notify your treating physician or clinical research nurse if you notice any of the following changes: itching, skin turning yellow, whites of the eyes turning yellow, dark urine, pain or an uncomfortable sensation in the upper right abdomen.

Common (1 in 100 up to 1 in 10 patients may experience these side effects):

- Inflammation of the hair follicles

- Inflammation of the cuticles

  Skin effects: thickening of the skin, skin fissures, warts, small and benign skin growths, small wounds, flaky or scaly patches of skin that may be an early sign of skin cancer, redness, small wounds oozing pus, and skin rash, including an acne-like rash.

- Inflammation of the subcutaneous fat tissue, with red and painful bumps in the skin

- Cellulitis

  Anaemia

- Hypersensitivity (immune system abnormalities)

- Dehydration

- Blurred vision

- Fluid retention around the eyes or eyelids

- Impaired vision

- Impaired cardiac function

- Reduced heart rate

- Pneumonia

- Stomatitis (inflammation of the oral mucosa)

- Redness

- Skin rash in the palm of the hand and sole of the foot

- Skin fissures

- Cracked skin

- Facial swelling

- Mucosal inflammation

  Feeling weak, or lack of energy

  Abnormal muscle protein values (elevated blood creatine phosphokinase levels)

Infrequent side effects (fewer than 1 in 100 patients may experience these side effects):

- Eye-related problems, including an occluded or bleeding retinal vein, inflammation of the eye, swelling around the eyes, retinal detachment and other retinal disorders, which may cause the patient to experience blurred vision. In many cases, these vision-related problems will pass, but in some cases they will not, and may result in blindness.

- Heart failure, causing the heart to be unable to pump blood around the body properly. This may result in fatigue, swelling and shortness of breath.

  - Retinal detachment in the eye

  - Lymphoedema

  - Pneumonia

  - Intestinal perforation (stomach ache, nausea, vomiting and fever)

  - Colitis (diarrhoea, often involving blood and slime, sometimes involving pus, stomach ache, stomach cramps and abdominal swelling).

  - Excessive muscle atrophy (pain, swelling, haphalgesia (pain upon physical contact) or sensitivity at the level of the affected area, loss of consciousness, confusion and dehydration, possibly with fever, paralysis or dysaesthesia of the limbs, dark urine, very little to no urine production).

Appendix E: Approved contraceptives and pregnancy

If you are a girl and of childbearing age, it is vital that you do not fall pregnant during the course of the trial and for at least 6 months after the last dose of chemotherapy. In addition, you should not have any egg cells stored (either for your own use or for donation) while she is taking part in the trial and for 6 months after the last dose of chemotherapy. You must not breastfeed any infants during the trial and for at least 35 days after the last dose of chemotherapy.

If you are a boy and taking part in the trial, you must not cause a pregnancy or donate sperm during the trial and for 6 months after the last dose of chemotherapy.

If you are capable of becoming pregnant and are heterosexually active, or if you have a partner who may become pregnant, the treating physician or nurse will explain the different types of contraceptives that you must use during and after the trial. Approved forms of contraception include:

- Hormonal contraceptives that are taken by mouth, inserted into the vagina, applied to the skin in the form of a skin patch, injected or implanted under the skin

- A coil (IUD) (some IUDs contain hormones)

- Refraining from having sex (complete sexual abstinence)

If you become pregnant during this trial, or if your partner becomes pregnant during this trial, you must notify the investigator or the members of the study team at once. If you fall pregnant, the treating physician will discuss with you how this will impact your course of treatment. The investigator will inform the sponsor and the Medical Ethics Review Committee that monitors this trial of the pregnancy and the outcome of the pregnancy.
