Dear,

You are invited to participate in a clinical study to evaluate how safe and effective the medicines Trametinib + Dexamethasone + Cyclophosphamide and Cytarabine are for the treatment of your disease. A clinical study is a research study that helps us to find new treatment options. You and your parents or guardians may decide together if you want to participate in this study. You don't have to decide right away, you get time to think about it and to ask questions. This letter contains more information about the study and can help you make a decision. It is important that you understand everything in this letter. Your parents/guardians have received a similar information letter and we will also ask their permission for you to participate in the study.

Do you want to take part? Then please sign this form. We also need a signature from your parents/guardian. Once everyone has signed for taking part, you will receive a copy of this document.

What is the purpose of this study?

You are receiving this letter because you are being treated for a form of paediatric cancer, called leukaemia or lymphoma. Unfortunately, the disease has returned. This is known as relapse. Or the disease has not responded (sufficiently) to the medications you were administered. This is known as refractory. Your leukaemia or lymphoma cells continue to grow and they are less likely to die. This is due to changes in proteins (mutations) and changes in the structure of the hereditary material (DNA).

As your treating physician has informed you, there is no standard therapy available for your disease. We want to ask you whether you would like to take part in a clinical study to examine whether we can improve the treatment of children and adolescents with refractory or relapsed leukemia or lymphoma, just like you.

In this study, we want to find out how safe and effective the medicine trametinib is for children and adolescents with leukaemia or lymphoma. We hope that the combination of trametinib and standard chemotherapy can beat the disease so that we can then do a stem cell transplant (SCT). A SCT is a procedure in which a patient receives healthy stem cells from a donor to replace its own damaged stem cells (stem cells are produced in the bone marrow and can become any type of blood cell the body needs).

Trametinib has previously been administered to patients suffering from various types of cancer: a certain type of lung cancer, skin cancer and thyroid cancer. So far, nearly 2,000 people have been administered this medication. Trametinib has not yet been approved for use in children, but has previously been tested in children with other forms of cancer, and in adults with leukaemia and other types of cancer. Those studies showed that trametinib can be administered in combination with chemotherapy, and also that it can be safely administered to children.

Dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine are medications that are used in the standard treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) and lymphoblastic lymphoma (LL). You have probably been administered these medications before. However, this particular combination of these medications has never been investigated before, in children or adult patients. Laboratory trials in ALL cells have shown that the combination of trametinib and dexamethasone is more effective than either trametinib or dexamethasone alone, since the two medications enhance each other’s effect.

If you participate in this study, we want to record information about your disease, the treatment you will receive, the outcome of your disease, side effects after treatment and your quality of life. Your information will be stored in a digital study file, called a database, and will always be kept confidential. You can read more about that later in this letter and in the letter your parents received.

The sponsor of this study is Princess Máxima Center, Heidelberglaan 25, 3584 CS Utrecht, The Netherlands.

what does participation in the study include for you?

As part of your participation in the study and taking into account your medical situation, part of the examinations that we describe will be part of the standard care in our hospital while others are necessary for the study.

Treatment with Trametinib in combination with standard chemotherapy (Dexamethasone + Cyclophosphamide and Cytarabine) takes between 1 to 6 months and will be administered in one or more cycles. Each cycle takes 28 days.

During the first cycle, you will be administered trametinib once daily for 28 days, in combination with dexamethasone and standard chemotherapy. Trametinib comes as a tablet formulation. The dose of medication that you will need is based on your age and body weight. At the start of each cycle, you will probably need to spend a few days in hospital.

In all cycles, you will receive dexamethasone for the first five days. Dexamethasone is ingested in tablet form or in powder form. In other words, it can either be taken at home or be administered intravenously in hospital.

In Cycle 1, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 3, and cytarabine on Days 5-8 and on Days 12-15. In Cycle 2, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 1, and cytarabine on Days 3-6 and on Days 10-13. If you feel well, you can return home between visits. If you feel sick, you will have to stay in hospital.

How many participants will be included in this study?

A total of 69 children/young adults will participate in the study worldwide. 4 patients will be included in Belgium, of which 1 or 2 will receive your specific treatment.

WHAT IS THE DURATION OF THIS STUDY?

The expected total duration of the study for you can vary between 1 to 6 months. At the end of each cycle (28 days), a tests will be done to see if the treatment has worked for you. If this is the case, and you are healthy enough, you can start the second cycle.

If your leukaemia or lymphoma responds well to the course of treatment, the therapy may be extended at the end of the trial. Once the course of treatment has been completed, we would like to keep monitoring you, until 1 year after the start of the therapy.

As part of this trial, you will have to visit the hospital every week if you are not actually staying overnight at the hospital.

What is expected from you?

For the study to run smoothly, it is important that you fully cooperate with the doctor and that you follow his/her instructions carefully.

In addition, it is important that you and your parents/guardians respect the following items:

- Report all side effects, injuries, symptoms or complaints to your doctor, even if they do not seem important, including any unplanned hospital admissions

- Do not take part in another study without approval from sponsor is requested by your doctor

- You will be given a card with phone numbers and information about the study. You or your parents must carry this card with you/them at all times.

- Are you taking medication? Please tell the investigator about this.

Pregnancy

Some drugs you will be given can be harmful for the baby if a girl is pregnant. For this reason, you must not be pregnant when you take part in this study and for a while after the study has ended. We will therefore do regular pregnancy tests during your participation to be sure you aren’t pregnant.

Your doctor will speak to you about a reliable way to prevent a pregnancy.

It is important that you carefully consider the potential advantages and disadvantages before deciding to take part:

- The treatment may make you less ill, but there are no guarantees. At any time during the study, you may become more ill.

- By participating, you are helping in the search for better treatment of relapsed leukaemia or lymphoma.

- Taking part in the study will require extra time because you will need to visit the hospital more often or because the visits take longer.

Study progress:

If you decide to participate in the study, you will need to undergo the following tests and investigations:

Screening before treatment

We will first check whether you can take part in this study. Most of these tests are also part of your standard treatment. There will only be a few additional tests done specifically for this study:

- We will ask you to complete a questionnaire, it will ask you how you feel and how you are doing. This will take about 10 minutes.

- We will also do a pregnancy test on girls who can become pregnant.

Tests and measurements during treatment

You will have to visit the hospital every week if you are not actually staying overnight at the hospital.

We will perform the following examinations, most of which would also be done if you would not take part in the trial:

- Physical examination. For instance, the investigator will listen to your heart and
  lungs and measure your blood pressure.

- Blood tests. Your blood will be collected for testing purposes. Two 2 ml tubes of blood will be taken.

- Review of your patient diary, which you have to fill in daily.

- Urinalysis.

- On Day 14 of Cycle 1, blood will be collected several times to determine the level of medicines in your blood. This is called a pharmacokinetic assay. We will collect blood once before trametinibdosing, and then three more times, at 4, 6 and 8 hours after trametinib dosing.

- On Day 14 of Cycle 1, we will also collect some cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) once. This is the fluid around your brain and spinal cord. This collection will be done by means of a needle that goes directly in the fluid around your spine, which is called a lumbar puncture. Your doctor will give you more information about this procedure. These samples are needed to measure the levels of trametinib in your CSF.

- At the end of each 28-day cycle, additional tests will be performed to determine if the treatment has worked for you.

  - You will be asked to complete a questionnaire, the questions will ask about how you feel and how you are doing.

  - Pregnancy test for girls who can become pregnant.

  - Bone marrow examination. Bone marrow can be found in the innermost part of our bones. This is where blood is produced. You will be sedated when the bone marrow aspirate is performed, so you will not feel a thing. You will receive information on the examination and the sedative beforehand. A tiny needle is used to suction a small piece of bone marrow from the pelvis. Sometimes a small piece of bone will be collected, as well (= biopsy). The bone marrow aspirate and the piece of bone will be examined in a laboratory. A plaster will be affixed to the spot where the biopsy is performed.

  - A scan (e.g. a CT scan), only if the leukaemia or lymphoma has spread to other parts of your body

  - Cerebrospinal fluid analysis

  - Blood tests

  - Urinalysis

  - Electrocardiogram (ECG) and echocardiogram. An ECG records the electrical signal from the heart. Special pads are placed on your chest to record the heart’s electrical signals, which cause the heart to beat. The signals are shown as waves on an attached computer monitor or printer and the doctor can read this to see if your heart is working as it should. An echocardiogram (echo) of the heart is a simple test which uses sound waves (ultrasound) to take a moving picture of your heart. It allows your doctor to “see” how your heart muscle is working.

  - Eye examination

The letter that we send to your parents/guardian states how often and how much blood and cerebrospinal fluid we will draw. Appendix A of that information letter provides a summary of exactly what will happen, when it will happen and what will be examined in additional cycle(s).

Follow-up

We would like to perform one last check-up 30 days after the final dose of study medication is administered. We would also like to keep monitoring you, until 1 year after the start of the course of treatment. To do this, you will have to come to hospital about four times. These visits will be on days where you have scheduled a regular visit with your doctor already. During these visits, we will physically examine you and check how you are doing.

Collection of biological samples:

Your samples that are collected and analyzed in the context of this study will be pseudonymized (also referred to as “coded”) after collection. You remain the "owner" of your human body material. This means that you can always ask that your stored samples are destroyed by the biobank. In that case you should contact the responsible doctor of the study at UZ Ghent, who will then ensure that the stored body material is destroyed.

It may happen that the results of the analysis of your biological samples by chance (and over and above the objectives of the study), reveal information that may be important to your health or that of your blood relatives. These data are called "accidental findings". You can choose whether you wish to be informed about this or not, and you can give your consent to this by ticking the box on the consent form on the last pages of this information letter.

The sponsor shall use the biological samples within the context of the trial as described above. You may also ask for a return of the remainders of your biological samples, if they could still be usable for your benefit. Please contact your investigator or the trial staff for this.

If you agree, the sponsor would like to retain the remainders of your biological samples for 25 years. The sponsor will use them for future research, outside the trial that you will participate in, to better understand the disease, its treatment and the responses to this treatment, and trametinib, dexamethasone, Cyclophosphamide and Cytarabine. Such a new study must always be submitted and approved by the ethics committee. The objectives of the future study should correspond to the objectives of this study. The retention of the remainders of your samples goes together with the retention of the accompanying data.

What are the possible side effects of the treatment?

It is possible that you will experience side effects from the medicines. The treatment might cause unpleasant effects, but we aren’t sure about this yet.

Trametinib may cause fever, feeling ill or extremely tired, nausea and impaired vision. This is quite common.

The appendix of the information letter for your parents/guardians provides more information about the side effects. If you feel very sick, tell your parents/guardians immediately so they can call the research staff.

Would you like to know more about side effects? You can ask the doctor or the study nurse.

In your patient diary, you must write down every day (alone or with the help of your parents) the time that you took your medication and whether you experienced any discomfort.

All drugs in your treatment may involve risks to the embryo, foetus and the unborn or nursing child. As a result, if you are old enough to have children, you may not become pregnant, breastfeed or get someone pregnant while you are participating in the study.

WHAT ARE YOUR RIGHTS and what happens if you do not participate in this study?

Your decision to participate in this study is entirely voluntary. If for any reason and at any time you decide not to participate, you can tell your doctor. We will still use the information that has already been collected for the study.

Your doctor and the nurses won't mind if you don't want to participate. The doctor will then discuss with you how your illness can be treated. You can also refuse to have information collected about you.

If, during the course of the study, your doctor feels that this study is not the best treatment, the doctor may remove you from the study and suggest a possibly better treatment.

The doctor conducting this study will inform your general practitioner/pediatrician about your participation in this study.

This study was evaluated by an independent Ethics Committee In Belgium. The study is thus carried out in accordance with national laws. Your data and samples will be treated confidentially and given a code (pseudonymized) in order to respect your privacy. This means that other people cannot directly see that it belongs to you. Only the investigator will know which code belongs to which person. Other people will only see the code, so they won’t know your name. This coded data can then be shared with other researchers for future studies in the context of the same or similar disease or treatment. These studies may take place in Belgium or abroad. Such new studies must always first be approved by the ethics committee. If you do not want your data to be used for future research, you can let your parents or the researchers know.

The doctor will also ensure that insurance is taken out to protect and/or treat you in the event of damage and/or injury resulting from your participation in this study (). Do you want to know more about this? You can ask your parents about this. More information is provided in the information letter for your parents. You can also ask the doctor or the study nurse about this.

You can ask questions about the study at any time.
