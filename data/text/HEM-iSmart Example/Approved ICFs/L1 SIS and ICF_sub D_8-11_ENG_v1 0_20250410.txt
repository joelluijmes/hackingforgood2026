Title of the study: HEM-iSMART sub-protocol D: Trametinib + Dexamethasone + Cyclophosphamide and Cytarabine in pediatric patients with relapsed or refractory hematological malignancies

EU number: 2021-003398-79

Sponsor of the clinical study: Princess Máxima Center, Heidelberglaan 25, 3584 CS Utrecht, The Netherlands

Hello,

You are invited to take part in a clinical study. This is scientific research that is done to find out what effect a new combination of medication has on your disease. You and your parents or guardian can decide together if you want to participate in this study. If you do not understand something or want to know more about the study, you can always ask your doctor or other persons of your choice.

Why am i invited to take part in this study?

We invite you to participate in this study because you have a cancer called leukaemia or lymphoma. Unfortunately, the disease has returned. This is known as relapse. Or the disease has not responded (sufficiently) to the medications you were administered. This is known as refractory.

There is no standard therapy available for your disease. We want to ask you whether you would like to take part in this clinical study to examine whether we can improve the treatment of children and adolescents with refractory or relapsed leukemia or lymphoma, just like you.

If you participate in this study, we want to record information about your disease, the treatment you will receive, the outcome of your disease, side effects after treatment and we want to know how you feel during and after the treatment.

You can decide yourself if you want to take part in this study, you don’t have to do it. If for any reason and at any time you decide not to participate, you can tell your doctor. The doctor will then discuss with you how your illness can be treated in other ways.

WHAT TREATMENT WILL I receive IN THIS STUDY?

In this study, we want to find out how well the medicines (Trametinib, Dexamethasone , Cyclophosphamide and Cytarabine ) treat your disease and how safe they are for children and adolescents with your disease.

Treatment with these medicines takes between 1 to 6 months and will be administered in one or more cycles. Each cycle takes 28 days.

During the first cycle, you will be administered trametinib once daily for 28 days, in combination with dexamethasone and standard chemotherapy. Trametinib comes as a tablet formulation. The way in which you will be expected to take the medication will be determined by your age and body weight. In all cycles, you will receive dexamethasone for the first five days. Dexamethasone is ingested in tablet form or in powder form. In other words, it can either be taken at home or be administered intravenously in hospital.

In Cycle 1, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 3, and cytarabine on Days 5-8 and on Days 12-15. In Cycle 2, you will be intravenously administered cyclophosphamide on Day 1, and cytarabine on Days 3-6 and on Days 10-13. If you feel well, you can return home between visits. If you feel sick, you will have to stay in hospital.

Which tests will be done for the study?

Most of the tests that are needed for this study are also part of the standard care in our hospital, this means that this would also be tested if you would not be taking part in this study. Your participation in the study includes no extra visits compared to treatment without participation in the study, but there will be some extra tests done. You will always receive the standard care, even if you decide not to participate in the study.

We will first check whether you can take part in this study. There will only be a few extra tests done compared to the tests that you would be receiving if not taking part in the study:

- We will ask you to complete a questionnaire, in which we ask you how you feel and how you are doing. This will take about 10 minutes.

- We will also do a pregnancy test on girls who can become pregnant.

During the study there will be tests on your

- Blood

- Urine

- Bone marrow

- The fluid around your brain and spinal cord

Via these samples the doctors want to test how well the medicines treat your disease and how safe they are for you. The doctors will also test how your heart is doing. We think it’s important to understand how you feel as well, so we will ask you to complete a questionnaire to find out. We might also take a photo of your body (a CT-scan).

All data and samples we collect about you will be given a code so they do not mention your name, so no one outside the study team will know your name. The data we collect about you may also be used for later research on your disease.

WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU DO NOT WANT TO PARTICIPATE IN THE STUDY?

If you do not want to participate in the study or if your parents do not want you to participate in the study, you can say this to your doctor. Your doctor and the nurses will not mind if you don’t want to take part. The doctor will then look with you how your disease will be further treated according to the current standard.

You can always ask questions about the study at any time.
