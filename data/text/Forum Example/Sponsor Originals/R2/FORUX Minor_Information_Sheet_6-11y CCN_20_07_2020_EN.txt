What is this clinical study for?[]

This study is for children who have a disease called acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (we call it aGvHD), which can happen after they get special cells (called a stem cell transplant) from another person. We want to find out if using a medicine called ruxolitinib together with another medicine called corticosteroids can help treat this illness better than using corticosteroids alone.

This medicine has already been tested in other children, and we want to learn more about how well it works when we start it earlier, right when the illness begins.

What we expect from the study:

- Help the illness get better more quickly

- Make it easier to stop using corticosteroids sooner

- Lower the chance of the illness coming back or getting worse

Doctors believe that this combination might work better and have fewer problems than using corticosteroids by themselves. But we need to study it more to be sure.

What will happen to me during the study?

If you join the study:

- []First, doctors will do some tests to make sure it’s safe for you to join. This includes things like blood tests or a check-up.

- Then, you’ll be given medicine. You’ll be placed in one of two groups:

  - One group will take ruxolitinib + corticosteroids

  - The other group will take corticosteroids only

- You won’t get to choose your group. A computer will decide, kind of like flipping a coin.

How long will the study last?[]

The study will last about 6 months. During that time, you’ll keep seeing your doctors and nurses regularly. After that, you’ll have check-ins every few months for up to 2 more years to make sure everything is okay.

Will I have any benefit from participating in the study?

[]Maybe!

The treatment might help your illness get better more quickly or help you feel better with fewer problems. Even if the treatment doesn’t help you directly, what we learn from your participation could help other children with the same illness in the future.

Am I taking risks in participating in the study?

[]All medicines, even those we already use, can sometimes cause side effects. Some children who take ruxolitinib may feel tired, have low blood cells, feel dizzy, or get infections more easily. These things can usually be treated by the doctors, but it’s very important to tell your parents or your doctor if you feel anything strange or uncomfortable.

You’ll be watched very carefully during the study. The doctors and nurses will work hard to keep you safe and help with any problems.
