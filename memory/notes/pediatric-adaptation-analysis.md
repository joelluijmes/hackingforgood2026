# How adult ICFs are adapted for children (EN, 4 study families)

Source: approved ICFs under `memory/text/` (Forum R1 + S1, Biomede 2.0, HEM-iSmart sub D). Metrics from a python3 -I script (scratchpad `an.py`). Readability = Flesch-Kincaid grade proxy on flattened text (rough; tables and boilerplate inflate it).

## 1. Version set per study
Each study has 4 EN docs (+NL/FR twins; Forum also has v1.1 clean pediatric files, adult/parents are v1.0):
- Adult: "INFORMATION SHEET AND INFORMED CONSENT FORM FOR ADULT PATIENTS" (Chapter I info + Chapter II consent).
- Parents/guardians: "INFORMATION SHEET AND INFORMED CONSENT FORM FOR PARENTS/GUARDIANS" (adult text re-voiced about "your child").
- 12-17: "Information form for PARTICIPANTS AGED 12-17 years" + "ASSENT FORM FOR ADolescents aged 12-17 years".
- 8-11: "Information form for Children 8-11 years" + (Forum: assent block inline at end) / "ASSENT FORM FOR CHILDREN (8-11 YEARS)" (HEM).
Key insight: the pipeline has TWO different transformations. (a) adult -> parents is a near-mechanical pronoun/voice rewrite (same structure, ~same length). (b) adult -> 12-17 / 8-11 is a true condensation + rewrite.

Rules of thumb for length: 12-17 = 30-38% of adult (typically 1,900-3,800 words); 8-11 = 9-17% (about 900-1,250 words, ~2 pages + assent; the same ~900-1000 words in every Forum sub-study regardless of adult length). Parents = 104-107% of adult.
Readability: FK drops only ~0.5-1.5 grade for 12-17 (still ~9.5-10.5: it is a shortened adult text, not a simplified one) and ~2.5-3 grades for 8-11 (~8 by this proxy; true target is grade 3-5, the proxy is inflated by long proper nouns like "Lymphoblastic", drug names, "Randomisation"). Shorter sentences matter less than vocabulary: avg word length / long-word share (9+ letters) goes 15% (adult) -> 11-14% (12-17) -> 8.5-9.6% (8-11). Expect to beat the existing 12-17 docs on simplicity; the approved precedent is moderate.

## 3. Structure: what is kept / dropped
Adult (Forum R1) has ~23 numbered Q-style headings, e.g. "Who can I contact in case of questions?", "Why am I being asked to take part?", "Do I have to take part in a trial?", "What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?", "What If something goes wrong within the trial?", "Can my participation in the trial end prematurely?", "Will my participation in the trial involve extra costs for me?", "Which data are collected about me during the trial and what will happen with them?" (sub 1-4, plus "Who, other than the Investigator ... has access", "How long will my data be kept?"), "Who has reviewed and approved the trial documents?", then consent checklist ("Prerequisites for your participation in the trial"), signature blocks (participant, impartial witness/interpreter, investigator), GLOSSARY, REFERENCES. Document is Chapter I (info) + Chapter II (consent).

12-17 keeps (condensed ~35%): short intro, purpose, what happens/randomisation, benefits, number of participants, duration, "What is expected from you?" (bulleted: keep appointments, tell doctor about medications, vaccination, side effects, no other trial, birth control if sexually active), side effects (short/long-term, still medical terms with parentheses), pregnancy/contraception (kept, addressed to girls/boys), rights/voluntariness, ethics committee, a 1-2 sentence insurance mention, coded data, whom to contact, then assent form with checklist + YES/NO.
12-17 DROPS: contact table with ombudsperson, "What if something goes wrong" detail (reduced to one insurance sentence), extra costs section, 4-subsection GDPR block (controller, DPO, rights of access/rectification, transfers outside EU, retention 25 y), "Who has access to my personal data", results publication, other-purposes secondary use detail (reduced to one sentence "coded data can be shared with other researchers for future studies ..."), glossary, references, impartial-witness block (kept in HEM/Biomede 12-17: "Witness/Interpreter (if present)").
8-11 keeps only 6-8 Q headings, e.g. Forum: "1. What is the FORUM2 R1 sub-study?", "2. What will happen if I participate ...?", "3. Who knows that I am participating in this study?", "4. Has anyone checked that this study is okay?", "5. Do I have to participate in the SUB-study?", "6. TO WHOM CAN YOU ASK QUESTIONS?". Biomede 8-11: "WhAT IS THE AIM OF THE STUDY?", "WHAT TREATMENT WILL I RECEIVE IN THIS STUDY?", "Which tests will be done for the study?", "WhO KNOWS I'm taking part", "Has anybody checked that the study is OK to do?", "WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU DO NOT WANT TO PARTICIPATE", "To whom can you ask questions?".
8-11 DROPS everything else: insurance (zero mentions in R1/S1/Bio 8-11), GDPR/legal terms (replaced by one sentence: "Your information we collect for the study will receive a code without mentioning your name. The data we collect about you may also be used for later research on your disease."), side effect lists (replaced by "If you ever feel something is wrong, like a headache, tummy ache, or feel sick, tell your parents right away. The doctors will take care of you and help you feel better."), contraception (kept only in HEM 8-11 as "pregnancy test on girls who can become pregnant"), costs, withdrawal procedure details, number of participants (Biomede keeps it), randomisation percentages, regulatory (FAMHP) names, glossary.
Parents doc: same headings as adult, in "my child" form: "Does my child have to take part in a trial?", "Which biological samples are collected from my child ...", Chapter I title "DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN your child is PARTICIPATING"; adds "You should not under any circumstances take their approval as an incentive for your child to take part in the trial."

## 4. Tone / voice
- Adult: "you/your" + formal register ("The investigator will ...", "You decide to withdraw your consent"). "Investigator" 48-77x.
- Parents: same text with "your child" (306x Biomede, 337x HEM, 129x R1); mixes "you/your child" ("you and your child agree"). Consent checklist flips to first person parent: "I understand that data about my child will be collected".
- 12-17: direct address to the teen ("Dear,", "We ask you to participate because you have been diagnosed with ..."), still formal ("This document will inform you clearly and in detail about the nature and purposes of the trial"). Keeps technical words but adds parenthetical glosses: "conditioning treatment (a kind of preparation treatment)", "immune system (your body's defence system that recognises and fights off viruses...)", "Cataracts (clouding of the lens in the eye)". "Investigator" almost replaced by "doctor" (doctor 18-25x vs investigator 5-8x). HEM 12-17 adds glossary-in-table: "Personal data = information about who you are, for example your date of birth and where you live." and "Do you want to know more about this? You can ask your parents/doctor".
- 8-11: warm greeting "Hello,"; "you" 9-13 per 100 words (2x adult); "doctor" 13-14x, "investigator" 0-1x; "x-rays (called radiation)", "a computer will decide which group you will be in. Neither you, nor your parents, nor your doctor can choose the treatment.", "a device that takes photos of your tumour" (MRI), "a group of people who look very carefully at the study ... We call this an Ethics Committee". Uses "we hope"/"we want to see". Questions in first person ("What will happen if I participate?"). Decision framed jointly: "You and your parents or guardian can decide together if you want to participate. You can decide to stop at any time."
- Emotional safety: 8-11 adds reassurance sentences ("The doctors will take care of you", "Your doctor and the nurses will not mind if you don't want to take part").

## 5. Before / after examples (adult R1 -> children)
- Purpose. Adult: "Although treatment so far has led to complete remission ... there remains a high risk that the leukaemia could come back. ... allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT)". 12-17 (kept near-verbatim with glosses). 8-11: "You have a disease in your blood called leukemia. To help you get better, the doctors want to give you healthy cells from another person, a so called stem cell transplantation. To make some space ... the sick cells must be removed with x-rays (called radiation) and strong medicines."
- Randomisation. Adult: "randomly assigned ... 12 Gy or 8 Gy". 8-11: "Group 1 'less radiation than usual during treatment' or Group 2 'as much radiation as usual'" (jargon (Gy, TBI, Etoposide) fully removed; numbers dropped).
- Voluntariness. Adult: "Your participation in a trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion ... without giving a reason". 8-11: "It is important to know that you are not obliged to participate. You and your parents can decide this. ... If you do not want to participate, this will have no effect on how the doctors will follow you up". 12-17: "Your doctor and the nurses won't mind if you don't want to participate."
- Data/GDPR. Adult: 4 subsections (controller, DPO, access/rectification, transfers outside EU, 25 y retention). 12-17: "Your data will be treated confidentially and given a code (pseudonymized) ... Only the investigator will know which code belongs to which person." 8-11: "will receive a code without mentioning your name".
- Insurance. Adult: 16 mentions. 12-17: "The sponsor will also ensure that insurance (protection against unexpected costs) is taken out to protect and/or treat you in the event of damage". 8-11: omitted.
- Side effects. Adult: full list with frequencies. 12-17: bullets of short-term ("Fatigue, nausea, vomiting ...") and long-term (endocrine, growth, infertility, cataracts, secondary malignancies "rare but serious") effects, no frequencies. 8-11: single "tell your parents right away" sentence.
- Pregnancy. Adult: full contraception duration rules. 12-17: "If you are a women, you must not become pregnant during the trial and for at least 12 months..." "Your doctor will speak to you about a reliable way to prevent a pregnancy." 8-11: omitted (HEM: pregnancy test for girls who can become pregnant).

## 6. Consent vs assent wording, signatures
- Adult: "Prerequisites for your participation in the trial" (17 "I declare/understand" bullets: read, time to think, contraception, insurance, costs, data access, ...). Signatures: participant (name/date/time/signature), impartial witness/interpreter (tick box, qualification), investigator (attestation of no pressure, Helsinki, GCP, Belgian law). Original kept by investigator 25 years, copy to participant.
- Parents: same bullets in parental voice ("I consent for my child to take part ...", "I understand the importance of contraception and will inform my child adequately"); signatures: FIRST parent/guardian AND SECOND parent/guardian (name, date DD/MMM/YYYY, time, signature) + witness/interpreter + investigator. "Chapter II - Informed consent FOR PARENTS/GUARDIANS".
- 12-17: titled "ASSENT FORM FOR ADolescents aged 12-17 years" (the child "assents"; text says "you will be asked to sign an assent form. Your parents will be asked to sign a consent form as well"). Items: "I have read and understood the document 'Information for participants aged 12-17 years' and I received a copy." "I understand that my participation ... is voluntary" "I agree to fully cooperate with the doctor-investigator" "I agree to participate in this R1 sub-study: YES / NO". Biomede/HEM 12-17 use numbered YES/NO items per component (e.g. "9) I agree that data obtained from this trial can be used for other research ... approved by a recognized Ethics Committee"; "I agree to have my data and samples collected for the study"). Signers: participant (name, signature, date), doctor-investigator (tick by investigator), witness/interpreter if present. "2 copies must be completed."
- 8-11: Forum: inline "I agree to participate in this R1 sub-study" + child name/date + investigator name/date (NO child signature line, only name + date; no parent line on this page since parents sign their own form). HEM: "ASSENT FORM FOR CHILDREN (8-11 YEARS)" with 3 simple YES/NO items ("1) I will let the doctor know if I notice unexpected symptoms.", "3) I agree that my samples are examined and kept for further study."), participant name/date, doctor name/signature/date, investigator attestation retained ("no pressure has been exerted").
- Sentence in info: "If you want to participate, please tell your parents and your doctor. In that case, we ask you to enter your name, first name and date below."

## 7. Questions/answers format
All adult+parents headings are Q-form or caps statements. 8-11 uses numbered first-person Q headings (what is, what will happen if I, who knows that I, has anyone checked, do I have to, to whom can you ask). 12-17 uses unnumbered mixed-case/CAPS headings ("What is the purpose of the SUB-study?", "WHAT WILL HAPPEN IF YOU TAKE PART?", "What is expected from you?", "WHO CAN YOU CONTACT IN CASE OF PROBLEMS OR QUESTIONS"). Case is inconsistent in originals (artefact of Word styles); regenerate with consistent casing.

## 8. Images / pictograms
Only 8-11 versions (and Biomede/HEM 12-17 to a minor degree) use images: Forum 8-11 has ~5 inline pictures (placeholders in text: `[Afbeelding met schermopname, ontwerp ...]`, `[MC900134551[1]]` = Office clipart header, drawing of hand with pen, clipart near the question heading, doll/toy at the signature). Biomede 8-11 has 4 `[]` image placeholders (treatment flow, radiotherapy, tumour, ethics committee). 12-17 Forum: one table/figure "Clinical design" `[]`. Adult/parents: images only for study design diagrams. Images are decorative/explanatory (illustrate randomisation, x-ray machine, cartoon figures), not required by law; leave placeholder markers "[IMAGE: ...]" for human designer. NB: alt text is machine generated and in other languages (Dutch/Italian) - strip it.

## 9. Rule-set for an LLM prompt: adult ICF -> 12-17 / 8-11
General:
1. Input = approved/nationally-compliant adult ICF (Chapter I). Output separate info letter + assent form; parents get the parents version (not the child version). Keep study identifiers (title, EU number, sponsor) from the adult doc in the header; add "Information form for PARTICIPANTS AGED 12-17 years" / "Information form for Children 8-11 years" title.
2. Never invent facts: every statement must trace to a sentence in the adult ICF. Numbers (doses, durations, visit counts) may be dropped for 8-11 but must be unchanged in 12-17.
3. Keep Belgian mandatory elements in 12-17: voluntary participation + right to withdraw without reason and without effect on care; what is expected; risks/side effects (summarised); pregnancy/contraception for sexually active teens; ethics committee review; insurance (one sentence); coded data (one sentence, term "pseudonymized"); who to contact; assent form + parents' consent mention.
4. For 8-11 keep only: purpose, what happens/what groups, who knows (confidentiality), ethics check, voluntary/no consequences, who to ask, assent. Add one simple "tell your parents/doctor if you feel something" sentence.
5. Drop (both): GDPR legal detail (controller, DPO, transfers, retention years), costs, compensation, regulatory authority names (FAMHP), glossary, references, ombudsperson table, impartial witness text (keep only a minimal "witness/interpreter (if present)" line in 12-17).
12-17 voice: 2nd person "you", warm but still explicit; max ~17 words/sentence; keep medical terms only with a bracketed gloss; replace "investigator/trial staff" with "your doctor/the study doctor" (once define "study doctor"); active voice; address both sexes ("If you are a girl ... If you are a boy ..."); target 30-38% of adult word count; end with assent form saying "I understand ... I can stop at any time ... YES / NO", signature participant + investigator, "2 copies".
8-11 voice: ~900-1,000 words; avg sentence <=15 words (aim <=12); "you" ~10-13 per 100 words; every heading a first-person question; no drug names unless essential (describe by function: "strong medicines", "x-rays (called radiation)"); concrete analogies, e.g. MRI = "a device that takes photos of your tumour"; no numbers except small ones; no passive; reassure ("The doctors will take care of you"). Mention parents explicitly in decision: "You and your parents can decide together"; no legalese ("coercion", "pseudonymized"); 6-8 headings; sprinkle image placeholders (5 max) at purpose, groups/randomisation, tests, ethics, contact. Assent: "I agree to participate in this sub-study" + child name/date + doctor name/date (HEM adds 2-3 simple YES/NO items).
Parent version (separate task, mechanical): swap "you/your" -> "your child/you and your child" for patient-referring clauses; Chapter II consent bullets 3rd person about child; two parent signature blocks (first + second parent/guardian, name/date/time/signature); add "approval ... incentive for your child" sentence; keep GDPR, insurance, costs in full.
Language levels: NL/FR same rules; FR/NL twins exist for every Forum/Biomede pediatric version (compare word counts: NL 8-11 ~940-985, FR 8-11 ~943-1011).

## 10. Caveats for pipeline
- Adult vs parents diff is large at the token level (single-word pronoun changes everywhere): do not diff; regenerate.
- 12-17 files are the ones where sponsors reuse text verbatim from the adult doc (typos/grammar errors carried over: "will been given", "If you are you in group 2", "you chose") - do an editing pass.
- Table flattening (pandoc `+---+` grid tables) pollutes headings counts; heading counts above are approximate.
- Pediatric docs lack GLOSSARY/REFERENCES and the contact table; adult docs have it. A generator should look at the adult doc and emit only the retained sections list in section 3.
