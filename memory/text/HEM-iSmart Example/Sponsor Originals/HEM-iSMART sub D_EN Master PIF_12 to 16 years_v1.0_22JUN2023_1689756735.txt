About the study

This study will be performed in <insert name hospital> and in 14 other countries. Approximately 26 children and adolescents will take part. The study has been checked and approved. The name of the committee that evaluated the study is: <insert name>.

Why this study?

You are receiving treatment for a form of paediatric cancer. You were given information about this at the start of the treatment. Unfortunately, the disease has returned. This is known as relapse. Or the disease has not responded (sufficiently) to the medications you were administered. This is known as refractory.

In this study, we want to find out how safe and effective the medicine trametinib is in combination with dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine for children with leukaemia or lymphoma.

The medicine trametinib has previously been tested in children with other forms of cancer. These studies showed that trametinib is suitable to be given in combination with chemotherapy.

The combination of trametinib with dexamethasone, cyclophosphamide and cytarabine has not previously been tested in children or adults.

Background

There may be changes (mutations) in leukaemia or lymphoma cells. These cause cancer cells to be produced faster and continue to grow. Below, this is referred to as ‘RAS mutations’. Patients with an RAS mutation are often more difficult to treat than those without the mutation.

In your case, these RAS mutations have been found. We hope that trametinib can beat the disease so that we can then do a stem cell transplant.

How does participation work?

Screening before treatment
We will first check whether you can take part in this study. This will take about half a day. Most of these tests are also part of the standard treatment. We collect the results of the tests for this study. We will perform the following tests for this study:

- Skin examination

- Eye examination

- Completing a questionnaire. We will ask you to complete a questionnaire about how you feel and how you are doing. This will take about 10 minutes.

Visits and treatment
Treatment with trametinib takes about 6 months. In the first cycle, you will be administered trametinib once a day for 28 days in combination with dexamethasone and standard chemotherapy.

At the end of this cycle, additional tests will be done to see if the treatment has worked. If this is the case, and you are healthy enough, you can start the second cycle. In this cycle, you will again be given trametinib for 28 days.

You will visit the hospital every week. If you feel well, you can return home between visits. If you feel sick, you will have to stay in hospital.

During the visits, we will do the following additional examinations:

- On day 14 of the first cycle, we will take cerebrospinal fluid and blood samples to measure the levels of trametinib in them. This is called pharmacokinetics (PK). If you have a PAC/central line, we use this to draw blood. This day may take longer than usual. The letter we sent to your parents states how often and how much blood and cerebrospinal fluid we will draw.

- Skin examination (every week)

  We will have a look at the patient diary you have to fill in daily.

  At the end of the first cycle, you must complete another questionnaire. This is about how you feel and how you are doing.

Cerebrospinal fluid can be found around the brain and spinal cord. You will be given an anaesthetic when we draw cerebrospinal fluid. A hollow needle will be inserted between two vertebrae into the cerebrospinal fluid space just behind them. The cerebrospinal fluid is collected in a tube.

Appendix C of the information letter for your parents provides a summary of exactly what will happen and when.

About the treatment

Risks
It is possible that you will experience side effects from the medicines. The treatment might cause unpleasant effects, but we aren’t sure about this yet.

Side effects
Trametinib can cause you to suffer from skin rashes, high blood pressure, bleeding, diarrhoea and coughing. This is quite common.

The appendix of the information letter for your parents provides more information about the side effects. If you feel very sick, or if you start having trouble seeing, tell your parents/guardians immediately so they can call the research staff.

Would you like to know more about side effects? You can ask the doctor or the study nurse.

Discomforts and different than normal
The eye and skin examinations are extra examinations for this study.

In your patient diary, you must write down every day (alone or with the help of your parents) whether you have taken your medication and whether you have experienced any discomfort.

We will draw additional tubes of blood and cerebrospinal fluid as described above.

The visits to the hospital may take longer, because we have to take more measurements.

Important to know:

- You may not take part in another study while you are participating in this study.

- You will be given a card with phone numbers and information about the study. You or your parents must carry this card with you/them at all times.

- You may not participate in this study if you are pregnant. We may do a test beforehand to make sure.

Advantages and disadvantages:

It is important that you carefully consider the potential advantages and disadvantages before deciding to take part.

- The treatment may make you less ill, but there are no guarantees. At any time during the study, you may become more ill.

- By participating, you are helping in the search for better treatment of relapsed leukaemia or lymphoma.

  Taking part in the study will require extra time because you will need to visit the hospital more often or because the visits take longer.

Payment

You will not receive payment for taking part. We do, however, pay your travel expenses.

Your rights

Do you have to take part?

No, it is up to you to decide whether you want to take part. If you don’t want to take part, then you don’t need to, even if your parents would like you to take part. If you want to take part, please sign the form. Even after you have signed the form, you can always decide to stop if you don’t want to continue. Please tell the investigator about this. You do not have to say why you are stopping.

If you choose not to participate, your doctor will discuss treatment options with your parents/guardians. And about their advantages and disadvantages.

Withdrawing consent

Please tell the doctor or the study nurse if you want to stop taking part. This is called: withdrawing your consent. You may do this at any time. We will still use the information that has already been collected for the study.
