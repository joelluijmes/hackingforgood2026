CHAPTER I – DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN PARTICIPATING

Dear,

We would like to invite you to participate in a clinical trial to evaluate a treatment for your disease. Please take the time to read this information carefully and discuss it with the investigator, the medical team, or others before you decide to participate in this trial. Please feel free to ask questions if anything is unclear or if you would like additional information. We call this process “informed consent.” If you decide to participate in this study, we will ask you to sign the consent form at the back of this information sheet.

Why is this study being conducted?

An infiltrating brainstem glioma or diffuse midline tumour is a very aggressive malignant brain tumour. There is currently no treatment that is effective enough to cure this disease in most cases.

Because of its location in the brain, this tumour might not be operable. Conventional chemotherapy has shown not to be effective in this disease; only radiotherapy is effective, but most often only temporarily.

Your doctor is proposing to add a new drug to the standard radiotherapy treatment to increase its effectiveness, as part of a clinical study called "BIOMEDE 2.0". This treatment will be chosen at random from two possible treatments.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the efficacy of ONC201 compared to everolimus in combination with radiotherapy. Everolimus is the best of the three drugs tested in the previous trial (BIOMEDE 1.0). This is why we will use this drug as a comparator in this new trial. ONC201 is the new drug to be tested. The efficacy will be assessed based on the duration without disease progression from the beginning of the treatment and on clinical signs and medical imaging results.

Everolimus and ONC201 are so called “targeted therapies/drugs”. These are drugs that specifically target a protein or mechanism involved in tumour development. These drugs are therefore theoretically without effect on healthy cells. As a result, they have fewer side effects than conventional anti-tumour chemotherapy. Anti-tumour chemotherapy act on all cells in the body that divide, whether they are cancerous or not.

Other objectives of this study are:

- Evaluate the side effects of these treatments given at the same time as standard radiotherapy and then during the total duration of treatment.

- Evaluate the duration of the response to the treatment.

- Evaluate the differences in efficacy between the two treatments studied (ONC201 and everolimus) according to tumour site and known prognostic biomarkers. Biomarkers are molecules or proteins that are overexpressed or abnormally absent in certain types of tumours. These markers can predict whether the targeted treatment is effective. Biomarker research helps physicians to guide their therapeutic choices, monitor the effectiveness of treatments and can help to individualize treatments.

- To evaluate the possibility for patients to be treated at the time of disease progression with a targeted therapy according to their molecular profile performed at diagnosis.

Additional objectives of this study include:

- Assess the predictive and prognostic value of biomarkers. (the extent to which a biomarker is capable of predicting the future course of a disease or condition).

- Describe the changes observed on imaging during treatment.

Why am I being asked to take part?

We are inviting you to take part in this clinical trial because you have been diagnosed with a tumour that is an infiltrating brainstem glioma or diffuse midline tumour. You and your medical team are considering participation in the Biomede 2.0 study. A new drug (ONC201 or everolimus) will be added to the standard radiotherapy treatment to increase its effectiveness. Which new drug will be added will be randomly chosen.

The investigator or trial staff will discuss with you the requirements to be allowed to enter the trial. It is not guaranteed that your participation in this trial will cure your brain tumour, improve your quality of life, or will extend your life.

Do I have to take part in a trial?

Your participation in a trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion. This means that you have the right not to take part in the trial or to withdraw at any time without giving a reason, even if you previously agreed to take part. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the investigator or your treating physician nor will it affect the quality of your future medical care.

If other treatments are available for your brain tumour, the investigator or his/her delegate will discuss these treatments with you. If you will not participate in the trial, you will receive the standard of care treatment with radiotherapy without the addition of everolimus of ONC201or another standard of care treatment.

What will happen during the trial? 

A total of 368 children/(young) adults will participate in this study across Europe. An estimated number of 8 participants per year will be included in Belgium.

In this study, you will receive one of the following treatments in combination with standard of care radiation therapy:

 ONC201 (company Chimerix)

 Everolimus (company Novartis)

Whether you will receive either ONC201 or Everolimus will be determined by randomization. This randomization will be done by a computer that will randomly determine which of the two potentially effective treatments will be given.

Most of the examinations/tests that are needed for this study below are also part of the standard care. Thus your participation in the study includes no extra visits compared to treatment without participation in the study.

This study is divided into 3 phases:

- Screening period

- Treatment phase

- Follow-up phase

Screening period

During the screening period you will have to undergo tests before the treatment is administered (see details in the table below). At this stage of the study, we are checking and assessing whether your participation is scientifically justified.

During the screening period the following procedures will be done:

Tumour Biopsy:

The tumour biopsy will first be analysed in the local laboratory of the hospital where you had the biopsy. This biopsy is not study specific. If you agree to participate to this study, a part of the tumour biopsy already performed will be sent to France (Villejuif) and will be examined into more detail to:

- confirm the diagnosis of invasive brainstem glioma or diffuse midline tumour,

- check the presence of biological indicators (biomarkers),

- complete the biological analysis of the tumour (by genetic sequencing) with the aim of possibly finding new biomarkers and new treatment options (= translational research).

A search for biomarkers (biological indicators) will be done to decide which is the most optimal treatment. In very rare cases, biomarkers found in the tumour may contraindicate the administration of one of the treatments proposed in this study. If this is the case, you will be informed.

Demographic data, medical and surgical history:

The investigating physician will collect the following information: your age (month and year of birth only), medical and surgical history (past and current illnesses).

He or she will also ask you about any medications you are taking, such as corticosteroids.

Clinical Examination:

The physician-investigator will check your general health, and determine your performance status (assessment of ability to perform daily activities, well-being). He or she will perform a complete clinical and neurological examination, measure your vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate by pulse measurement, take your temperature), and record your height and weight. You will also have an electrocardiogram (ECG).

Imaging tests:

Imaging tests will be done to measure and record the size of the tumour, and to check your general condition. The different imaging tests that will be done are an MRI (scan of your tumor by Magnetic Resonance Imaging) and a lung x-ray. This MRI will include intra-tumour perfusion and will therefore be a little longer than the usual MRIs (about 30 minutes). This is a study specific functional MRI. Specifically for the study, the images will be uploaded to a platform for central review by experts.

Blood tests:

- For a biological workup (haematological, biochemical, coagulation)

- For hepatitis B screening (serology)

- For a pregnancy test: If you are a woman of childbearing age, you will need to do a blood (or urine) pregnancy test. The result of this test must be negative to participate in the study.

All the above procedures during the screening period are standard of care procedures that you will also have if you are not participating in the study except for the functional MRI imaging.

Only after all of these tests have been completed, and if you meet all of the inclusion criteria and none of the non-inclusion criteria, we can confirm your participation in this study. If you cannot participate, you will be offered the standard radiation treatment. No experimental treatment will be added to the radiation therapy in this case.

Treatment phase

On Day 1 (D1) of the treatment phase, you will start:

- Radiation therapy: you will receive the standard dose of radiation (54 Grays) over 6 weeks, which is a fraction of 1.8 Gray per day, on 5 consecutive days per week (no weekend radiation therapy).

- One of the following treatments:

- Either everolimus: you will take one or more tablets (defined according to your weight) orally, daily, once a day;

- Or ONC201: you will take one or more capsules (defined according to your weight) orally, only 2 consecutive days per week (D1-D2), once a day.

During this treatment phase, a clinical examination (with performance evaluation) will be performed once a week for the first 2 months of treatment, then once a month. During these visits, a blood test will also be done to check if you are doing fine and if the treatment is well tolerated. The physician-investigator will ask you if you have (had) any side effect that will be treated if necessary.

Once a month, you will also have a neurological examination, and we will collect your weight and height. These are all examinations you will also have if you are not participating to the study.

In addition, it is important that you report all treatments you are receiving (including corticosteroids) to your doctor, including alternative medicine treatments. Depending on the trial medication you are taking, the doctor will check for drug interactions with these other treatments.

Finally, an initial follow-up MRI will be done right after the radiation treatment ends and then every 2 months. These first follow-up MRI will include a study specific functional MRI of intra-tumour perfusion and will therefore be a little longer than the usual MRIs (about 30 minutes). All other follow-up MRIs will be done as usual without the study specific functional MRI.

If your disease progresses or returns, your treatment will be stopped. If possible, your doctor can suggest that you are switched to the other study treatment (ONC201 or everolimus), as described above.

Prior to this 2nd treatment period, a new pregnancy test and a new hepatitis B screening will be performed, if indicated.

During this 2nd treatment period, you will have the same examinations as during the 1st treatment period:

- Once a month: clinical examination, biological check-up

- Every 2 months: MRI

Treatment with ONC201 or everolimus will be continued as long as its efficacy and tolerance allow. The duration of treatment is therefore variable from one person to another.

Follow-up phase

You will have a visit 28 days after the treatment was stopped.

After that, you will continue to be monitored every 3-4 months for the first year and then every 6 months until the fifth year after starting treatment. At these standard of care follow-up visits, you will have a clinical examination (looking for late side effects), a neurological examination and an MRI. Your doctor will note at each visit whether you have taken any other treatments, and whether you have had any side effects.

The total duration of your participation in this study will therefore be until 5 years after start of treatment.

The follow-up phase will be done according the standard of care so no study specific procedures will be performed.

At the end of your participation in the study, if you have an unresolved side effect, the investigating physician may contact you until the side effect is resolved to ensure that you receive appropriate medical follow-up. He/she may also contact you if you report a side effect after the end of the study.

Or

-1ml Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF, fluid around the brain and spinal cord)

Cerebrospinal fluid is collected during a lumbar puncture. This is a procedure in which a doctor uses a thin needle to extract a small amount of cerebrospinal fluid from your lower back. This is not specific to the study and is carried out as part of the standard treatment.

Will I benefit from the trial? 

We have selected the medicines to be included in Biomede 2.0 trial as they have shown promising results and so we hope they will be of benefit to children, adolescents, and young people an infiltrating brainstem glioma or diffuse midline tumour. However, we cannot promise that you will benefit from taking part in this trial.

The information obtained during a trial may contribute to a better understanding of your disease and the use of the investigational medicinal product (referred to as “IMP”) or to the development of a new medicinal product for the treatment of yourself or future patients.

The treatment schedule may or may not be beneficial in treating your infiltrating brainstem glioma or diffuse midline tumour or relieving your symptoms. Even if it is beneficial to you, a potential return or worsening of symptoms, illness or disease is still possible.

What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?

1.  What are the possible side effects of standard radiotherapy and everolimus/ONC201 treatment?

Participation in a trial involves some risk. Everolimus, ONC201 and the standard radiotherapy can have side effects. Some are more common than others, and any one patient may not experience all of them.

Everolimus

Common side effects include (may affect more than 1 in 10 patients):

  - Reduction in number of red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets

  - Loss of appetite

  - Anorexia, weight loss

  - Nausea and vomiting

  - Diarrhea

  - Skin rash, acne

  - Inflammation of the oral mucosa with ulcerations (stomatitis, aphta)

  - Fatigue

  - Headache

Less common side effects include (may affect up to 1 in 10 patients):

- Renal failure

  - Abdominal pain

  - Bleeding

ONC201

Uncommon but potentially serious adverse events (may affect up to 1 in 100 patients):

- Upper respiratory infections, generalized infections (sepsis)

- Neurological disorders associated with brain damage (encephalopathy),
  cognitive impairment

- Difficulty breathing (dyspnea, respiratory distress)

- Abnormal blood flow to the skull, lungs and liver (intracranial bleeding,
  pulmonary bleeding, hepatic bleeding)

- Obstruction of an artery in the lungs (pulmonary embolism)

- Complications related to the massive destruction of cancer cells (tumour lysis
  syndrome).

Standard radiotherapy

- Nausea and vomiting

- Headaches

- Drowsiness

- Transient neurological worsening sometimes corresponding to pseudoprogression (exaggeration of the effects of radiation therapy by the targeted drug)

- Delayed hearing loss.

You will also experience these side effects of radiotherapy if you will not participate in this study.

You will receive regular blood tests during and after treatment and will be checked frequently. Seek urgent medical attention if you have any concerns during or after treatment.

Because this treatment combination is still under investigation, other currently unknown risks and discomforts could occur. Therefore, it is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to the investigator, regardless of whether or not you think it has to do with the trial, and even when it is already described in this document. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with the investigator beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial and present your emergency card. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

1.  What are the possible risks or discomforts of the examinations during the trial?

There are no known risks of the examinations during the trial. All examinations are performed for the standard treatment of your disease.

Only at screening and the end of radiotherapy a study specific functional MRI will be done together with the standard MRI. No possible risks are expected but this examination will take a little longer (30min longer).

2.  Can I take other medicines during the trial?

Do not hesitate to ask your investigator for more explanation about the use of other medicines and food supplements (including aromatherapy, phytotherapy, homeopathy..) during the trial.

3.  Will my participation to the trial have an impact on my daily activities?

We want to ensure that the trial runs smoothly. That is why we will make the following agreements with you:

• You will make sure you take all your drugs in the way that the investigator
has explained.

• During this trial, you won’t participate in another medical scientific trial,
unless sponsor approval was obtained prior to participation

• You will attend all your appointments.

• Please carry your participant card with you. For example, in your wallet.
This states that you are taking part in this trial and who to notify in case of
an emergency. Show this card when you visit another doctor.

• Please contact the investigator or research nurse in the following situations:

  o You will start to take other drugs. This includes homeopathic drugs,
  natural remedies, vitamins or over-the-counter drugs.

  o You need to be admitted to the hospital or receive treatment there.

  o You suddenly develop problems with your health.

During the course of the trial, important new information might become available, possibly affecting your decision to (further) participate.  Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial and what will happen with them? 

1.  Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial?

Biological samples are samples of human body material (for example blood, tissue, urine, faecal stool, ….). In this trial, the tumour biopsy is done according to the standard of care (not study specific). A part of the tumour biopsy already performed will be sent to France (Villejuif) and will be examined for this trial into more detail to confirm the diagnosis, a genetic analysis and to check for biomarkers.

In the exceptional case where a so-called "liquid" biopsy (blood or cerebrospinal fluid) has been performed instead of a tumour biopsy, new biomarkers may be tested in the laboratory of the sponsor in France on this sample. You are free to accept or refuse to have this sample analysed to search for new biomarkers at the end of this form.

The procedure to encode your biological samples is the same as that used for your personal data in accordance with Belgian legislation on the use of human body material (see I § 12.2). Samples sent to the sponsor or to organisations working in collaboration with the sponsor, will only be labelled with your trial identification code. The sponsor ensures that the location of your biological samples is always tracked. The use of your biological samples is linked to the processing of the associated coded personal data.

Your biological samples are deemed to be a “donation”. You will not receive any financial benefit associated with the development of new therapies derived from the use of your biological samples, and which may have commercial value.

2.  What will happen to the collected biological samples?

The tumour biopsy will be analysed for the objectives of the trial.

Since scientific progress in this area is constant, the sponsor would like to, with your consent retain the remainders of your biological samples for 10 years. The sponsor will use them for additional research that remains within the context of the current clinical trial and thus with the same disease or the same treatment/drug as in this study.

You agree or disagree to the retention of the remainder of your biological samples for additional research within the context of the current clinical trial by ticking the appropriate checkbox in Chapter II. The material will be used for future research, outside the trial that you will participate in, to better understand the disease, its treatment and the responses to this treatment, and everolimus/ONC201.

If you agree, this additional research may only be conducted according to the legislation on the use of human tissue material and with the approval of a recognized Ethics Committee.

Genetic analyses will also be performed on your biological samples. Genetic analyses are studies of DNA. For example, DNA determines the colour of our hair and eyes. DNA can also explain why some people respond to certain drugs and others do not. It can also explain why some people get certain diseases and others do not.

These genetic analyses will deliver essential information for the trial. If you do not want these analyses to be conducted, you will not be allowed to participate in the trial.
