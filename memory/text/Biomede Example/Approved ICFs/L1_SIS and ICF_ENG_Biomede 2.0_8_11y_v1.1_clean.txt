BIOMEDE 2.0 study:

Treatment of diffuse midline glioma

[MC900134551[1]]

Hello,

Your doctor told you that you have a brain tumour. It is possible that this cannot not be removed by surgery. The usual treatment is radiotherapy but it is not always effective enough. Radiotherapy is a way doctors treat some illnesses by using special invisible rays to damage bad cells so they stop growing.

Your doctor suggests that you take a tablet or capsule of a new medicine together with the radiotherapy to increase the effectiveness of radiotherapy, as part of a clinical study called "BIOMEDE 2.0".

You and your parents or guardian can decide together if you want to participate in this study. You can decide to stop taking part in the research at any time. If you do not understand something or want to know more about the study, you can always ask your doctor or other persons of your choice.

WhAT IS THE AIM OF THE STUDY?

The purpose of this study is to compare a new treatment (ONC201) with an existing treatment (Everolimus) in combination with radiotherapy. . We hope to further improve the treatment.

A total of 368 children/(young) adults will participate in this study across Europe. An estimated number of 8 participants per year will be included in Belgium.

WHAT TREATMENT WILL I RECEIVE IN THIS STUDY? 

We know of two treatments that might be helpful, but we don't know which is better. To know which is the best treatment, it is necessary to compare these two treatments on two groups of patients. A computer will decide which group you will be in. Neither you, nor your parents, nor your doctor can choose the treatment. At the end of this study, the results of the two groups will be compared.

[]

Before starting the treatment, your doctor will make sure that you can receive the study treatments by performing a few tests, including a blood test and an analysis of your tumour.

Once the treatment has been chosen, you will take the medication (ONC201 or Everolimus) from the first day of radiotherapy. Everolimus tablets or ONC201 capsules will be taken by mouth as a single dose.

[]

You will be monitored regularly by your doctor with an examination and blood tests. During the first six weeks of treatment, you will also have radiotherapy sessions, one session a day, five days a week but not on weekends.

[]

If your disease progresses or comes back, your treatment will be stopped. Then, if possible, you will take the other study treatment (Everolimus or ONC201).

Throughout this period of treatment, your doctor will monitor you regularly with examinations, blood tests and MRIs (a device that takes photos of your tumour).

Which tests will be done for the study?

During the study sometimes images will be taken from your tumour. Also some tests will be done on your blood to check if your treatment works. These tests will also be done if you don’t participate in this study and if you would receive another treatment for your disease. You will regularly visit your doctor. Information about your treatment, your exams and how you feel will be collected.

Your information we collect for the study will receive a code without mentioning your name. The data we collect about you may also be used for later research on your disease. Your participation in the study will take until 5 years after the start of the treatment. During your participation data on your disease, your treatment, how you feel and eventual further treatments you will receive will be collected..

For the study we will investigate your tumour. The images taken from your tumour will be reviewed by experts of the study. No extra procedures are needed for this study. All samples taken for the study will be given a code so they do not mention your name. This way they can be stored and later be used for further research into your disease if you agree. You can ask to have the collected samples destroyed at any time without having to give a reason for this.

WhO KNOWS I’m taking part in this study

The only people who will know that you are taking part in this study will be the doctors, nurses and researchers looking after you, and your family doctor. All of the information about you will only be shared with people involved in the research.

Has anybody checked that the study is OK to do?

Before any research is allowed to happen, it has to be checked by a group of people called a Research Ethics Committee. They make sure that the research is fair.

WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU DO NOT WANT TO PARTICIPATE IN THE STUDY?

If you do not want to participate in the study or if your parents do not want you to participate in the study, you can say this to your doctor. Your doctor and the nurses will not mind if you don’t want to take part. If you do not wish to participate in this study, you will be offered the usual radiotherapy treatment. No other new treatment will be added to radiotherapy in this case. You can always ask questions about the study at any time.
