Dear ..............................................,

The doctors have talked to your parents to let you take part in a medical study. This text will help you understand what this study is about. Your parents will decide, but it’s also important that you know what’s going on and what you think about it.We have given your parents an information paper similar to this one, only more difficult.

We have thought about the questions you might have, and wrote down answers that we hope will clear up your doubts. You can ask the doctor any other questions to have a better understanding – there are no silly questions!

After you and your parents have learnt everything about this study and the doctor has answered all your questions, your parents sign an important document, the so-called “declaration of informed consent” - it means they agree that you can take part.

What is this clinical study for?

[]

You have a sickness in your blood called leukemia.

To help you get better, the doctors want to give you healthy cells from another person, a so called stem cell transplantation. To make room in your body for the new healthy cells, the sick cells must be removed with x-rays and strong medicine (called conditioning). In this study, we want to see whether even a small amount of x-rays can help you stay healthy for a long time.

What we expect from the study:

[Immagine che contiene clipart Descrizione generata automaticamente]Since the x-rays are also harmful to your body, we hope that less x-rays are better for you and that you will stay healthy for a long time even with less x-rays.

What will happen to me during the study?

[]

First of all, you will be assigned to a group:

Group 1 ‘less radiation than usual during treatment’

OR

Group 2 ‘as much radiation as usual during treatment’

Are you group 1, you will receive less x-rays than you would normally receive during radiotherapy.

Are you group 2, you will receive as much x-rays as you would normally receive during radiotherapy.

After that, everything stays the same: Your treatment and your care will stay the same – nothing will change.

At regular intervals, you will undergo examinations and check-ups during which the doctor will ask you how you feel.

How long will the study last?

[]

You will be part of the study for up to 5 years, which is the normal time doctors keep checking on patients after a stem cell transplantation.

Why is this study good for me?

[]

  Doctors think that this new treatment can be safer for you than the old treatment. But at the moment we are not 100% sure. However, we as doctors, will learn from this study more about this new treatment, and this information could help other children with the same disease as you have in the future.

Do I take any risks if I join the study?

All treatments from doctors can have some risks, even the ones you already know.

[]If you take part in this study, there is a chance that the smaller amount of the special x-rays (called radiation) might not be strong enough to keep you healthy for a long time.

But don’t worry — the doctors will always check how you’re doing, just like they do now.

If you ever feel something is wrong, like a headache, tummy ache, or feel sick, tell your parents right away.

The doctors will take care of you and help you feel better.

Yes! You can ask the doctors anything you don’t understand. They will try their best to explain everything in a way that’s easy for you.

We hope that what you’ve read and heard has helped you understand what a clinical trial is.
Now it's a good idea to talk about it with your parents, so you can decide together what’s best for you.
