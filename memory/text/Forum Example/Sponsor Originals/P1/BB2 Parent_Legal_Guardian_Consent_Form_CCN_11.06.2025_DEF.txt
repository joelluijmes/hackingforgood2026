B.  INFORMATION SECTION.

SUMMARY OF THE TRIAL: KEY INFORMATION

Your child/the minor has been diagnosed with CD19+ B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, needing a HSCT as curative treatment. Given the young age of your child, he/she cannot receive total body irradiation (TBI, the gold standard for this kind of disease) as part of the conditioning regimen in order not to cause potential severe side effects. For this reason, as demonstrated in the previous FORUM study, the incidence of relapse of patients not receiving TBI is higher than that of those receiving radiation. Additionally, the rate of relapse after HSCT in children with B-ALL below the age of 2 years is particularly high as compared to older children.

For all these reasons, in order to try to reduce the risk of relapse we want to administer blinatumomab, a kind of immunotherapy authorized to treat some high-risk CD19+ B-cell ALL, as prophylaxis of relapse after HSCT. Blinatumomab, a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) targeting CD19, redirects T cells to eliminate CD19-positive cells, including leukemia cells, with a favorable toxicity profile when compared to traditional chemotherapy. The BiTE has demonstrated effectiveness both in pediatric and adult populations. In infants with ALL, blinatumomab was recently shown to be safe and effective, with an excellent safety profile.

Generally, well tolerated, particularly in the setting of low-burden disease, blinatumomab represents an ideal drug to be used in the post-HSCT setting. Indeed, consolidation strategies after allogeneic HSCT to prevent relapse in patients with CD19-positive B-ALL are under investigation, and blinatumomab has been shown to be feasible, with effectiveness possibly depending on the patient's immunity at the time of treatment

The ForInfantBB2 (Group P1) is part of a larger international research project (FORUM2) aimed at improving the outcomes of stem cell transplantation in children and young adults with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), and it is specifically focused on pediatric patients (< 2 years) with CD19+ B-ALL.

We are asking you to give your consent to participate in this clinical trial because we want to verify if the use of blinatumomab after HSCT in children <2 years of age is safe and effective in reducing the incidence of leukemia relapse. C.  INFORMATION SECTION. FURTHER INFORMATION

1.  What is the purpose of the trial?

The purpose of this clinical trial is to improve the outcome of children below 2 years of age and affected by CD19+ B-cell ALL by testing whether using blinatumomab after HSCT leads to a reduction of leukemia relapse incidence.

The rationale of this study lies on the observation that i) the rate of relapse after HSCT in children with B-ALL below the age of 2 years is particularly high and ii) these patients are unable to benefit from the relapse-reducing effect of TBI as part of the conditioning regimen due to their age, since use of TBI during the conditioning regimen in very young children is associated with an excessive risk of severe long-term toxicities, in particular on growth and neurocognitive development.

2.  What are the patient groups compared? What is the intervention being tested?

This is a Phase II, open-label clinical trial. Eligible patients are males or females aged 3 months to 2 years of age, transplanted at FORUM2 centers following a chemotherapy-based conditioning regimen.

Enrolled patients will receive the investigational treatment blinatumomab, a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) targeting CD19, redirects T cells to eliminate CD19-positive cells, including leukemia cells, with a favorable toxicity profile.

Treatment will consist of up to four 28-days cycle of blinatumomab infused intravenously, separated by a 2-month break (see image below). In patients with signs of possible disease recurrence (i.e., positive minimal residual disease) at any time-point after HSCT, this interval period can be reduced to 1 month. Blinatumomab treatment will begin on day 60 +/- 10, after HSCT.

[]

Blinatumomab will be given at the dose of 15 mcg/m2/day as a 24-hour continuous i.v. infusion over a 28 days course. The drug will be administered though specific injectors that will be provided to you/your child. The first 5 subjects enrolled will be treated as inpatient for the first 7 days of blinatumomab treatment. Following review by a specific safety commission (“Data Monitoring Committee”), subsequently enrolled subjects will be treated as inpatient for the first 3 days. The remaining treatment can continue in an outpatient setting. The hospitalization time depends on investigator’s judgment, as well as safety and tolerability of blinatumomab. The drug administration should not be interrupted, if possible. In case of infusion interruption due to technical or logistical interruption (e.g., diagnostic measurement), the interruption should be as short as possible and the infusion continued at the earliest time possible. If the interruption is longer than 4 hours, re-start of the infusion should be performed in the hospital, under the supervision of the investigator.

Specific criteria to start a new cycle are the same as for inclusion/exclusion criteria at time of study enrollment (i.e., ANC >0.5 x 10^9/L or self-sustained platelet count >30 x 10^9/L).

The planned treatment period in this study is 10 months. Your child’s/the minor’s treatment will begin on Study Day 1 and continue unless one of the following occurs earlier:

- A leukemia relapse occurs

- Serious side effects occur and do not resolve after reduction/interruption of the treatment

- Your child/the minor withdraws from the study or passes away

During the treatment period, your child’s/the minor’s care will be adjusted based on how well they respond to the therapy. The response (i.e., maintenance of remission) will be formally assessed through bone marrow at the end of the first cycle and before each subsequent cycle.

In order to understand if blinatumomab is effective as compared to standard of care (i.e., no additional treatments), the results of treated patients will be compared to those of patients enrolled in the previous FORUM 1 study (historical cohort).

4.  What risks can I our child/the minor face if I/we authorize participation in the trial?

Participation in this clinical trial may involve risks related to the study drug (blinatumomab) and procedures such as blood sampling. Side effects may vary from person to person. This document includes both common side effects and rare but serious side effects known to be associated with blinatumomab. You are encouraged to discuss any concerns with the study doctor, including possible side effects not listed here.

Many side effects resolve shortly after stopping or adjusting the medication. However, some side effects may be serious, long-lasting, or permanent, and in rare cases, could be life-threatening.

Side effects of blinatumomab

In patients treated for lymphoid malignancies, outside the setting of HSCT, very common side effects (occurring in more than 1 in 10 people) include:

- Infections (viral or bacterial)

- Low white blood cell count (neutropenia), which may increase the risk of infection

- Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia), which may lead to easy bruising or bleeding

- Febrile neutropenia

- Cytokine release syndrome

- Insomnia, headache, tremors

- Tachycardia, hypertension, hypotension

- Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain

- Back pain, pain in extremity

- Hepatic enzyme increased, decreased immunoglobulins

In the vast majority of cases (not including infections), these side effects are reversible and can be managed by temporarily reducing or stopping blinatumomab. Your child/the minor will be monitored regularly through clinical examination, vital sign measurements and blood tests, and if clinical conditions do not improve, the drug dose may be reduced or even temporary stopped and later restarted at a lower dose.

Common side effects (occurring in 1 to 10 in 100 patients) included:

- Severe infections (including sepsis and pneumonia)

- Tumor lysis syndrome (which is not expected in this setting due to the complete remission state required for enrollment)

- Confusional state, disorientation, encephalopathy, paraesthesia, seizure, dizziness, somnolence, cranial nerve disorder, ataxia, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS)

- Dyspnoea, wheezing

In rare cases (less than 1 in 100), capillary leak syndrome, acute respiratory failure, histiocytosis haematophagic and pancreatitis have been reported. Symptoms may include weight gain, severe cough and dyspnea, high-grade fever, severe abdominal pain. Inform the study doctor immediately if your child/the minor shows any of these symptoms.

Clinical experience of blinatumomab in pediatric patients with B-ALL

In 2 recent clinical trials, children with high-risk B-ALL were treated with one or two courses of blinatumomab before HSCT (and the results compared to that of standard chemotherapy, since these were randomized controlled trials):

- All patients experienced at least one adverse event (AE)

- Treatment-related AEs, were most commonly CRS (22%) and encephalopathy (11%)

- In the 2 studies, 57% and 76% experienced a severe AE (grade ≥3): Thrombocytopenia (18.5%), stomatitis (18.5%), neutropenia (16.7%), anemia (14.8%), fever 5.6%

- Serious AEs occurred in 24.1% of patients

- Only 3.7% of patients had an AE resulting into discontinuation of blinatumomab

In these studies, treatment with blinatumomab was associated with improved outcome (including survival after HSCT) and less side effects as compared to standard chemotherapy.

These results clearly indicate that blinatumomab offers clinically meaningful benefits with a manageable safety profile outside the HSCT setting.

As part of the study, your child/the minor will undergo approximately 34 blood draws. The volume of blood collected will depend on their age and the specific visit. Risks include fainting, discomfort, bruising, and rarely, infection or blood clots at the site of collection. Blood pressure cuffs may cause temporary discomfort or bruising.

During treatment with blinatumomab, you must immediately inform the study doctor if your child/the minor:

- Develops unusual bruising, bleeding, extreme fatigue, shortness of breath

- Has a persistent cough and/or fever

- Experiences confusion, disorientation, encephalopathy, seizure, dizziness, somnolence.
