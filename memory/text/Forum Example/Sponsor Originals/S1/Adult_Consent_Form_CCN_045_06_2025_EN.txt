B.  INFORMATION SECTION.

SUMMARY OF THE TRIAL: KEY INFORMATION (no more than 1-2 pages)

This section aims to briefly present the key aspects of the trial which we propose that you join. The following sections will provide more details in order to give you the opportunity to express or not a fully informed consent to your participation in the trial.

- Why am I being asked to participate in this trial?

We are asking you to participate in a clinical trial funded by ................ because you are a candidate to receive an allogeneic stem cell transplantation from a mismatched related donor (haplo-HSCT). In this type of transplant, T cell depletion is required to in order to reduce the risk of Graft-versus-Host Disease (GvHD). The two most used methods for T-cell depletion are based on the administration of Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy) and on the removal of a specific subtype of T lymphocyte (αβ T-cells) and B lymphocyte from the graft. To date, there are not comparative study in order to clarify which is the best strategy between the aforementioned two. With this trial we want to verify if there are some differences in terms of outcomes between these two different T-cell depletion platforms.

You are being asked to participate in this trial because you show some clinical characteristics that will be better specified in section C.

- What are the aims of the trial? How many centers and patients will participate?

The trial is performed to answer this question:

Are there any differences in survival or severe complications rates between patients who received a haplo-HSCT based on the administration of cyclophosphamide after the transplant, or on the depletion of αβ/CD19+ cells from the graft?

The trial is expected to take place in approximately 133 centers in 26 countries and 150 patients will be included.

- What is the routine care approach for treating the disease?

Both αβ T-cell depletion and PTCY are increasingly used to reduce the risk of GvHD in the context of haplo-HSCT in patient with acute lymphoblastic leukemia lacking a matched donor.

- Is it my free choice to decide whether to participate or not?

You can freely choose whether or not to participate in the trial. Even after accepting, you can change your mind at any time.

- If I decide not to give my consent to participate in the trial, what choices do I have?

If you decide not to join the trial, you will be followed by the clinicians that are treating you. The T cell depletion platform for the haplo-HSCT will be chosen by your Center as their standard clinical practice, and the data regarding your transplant outcome will not be utilized for a comparative study.

In addition, you can participate in another trial that may be in progress.

- What happens if I decide to participate in the trial?

If you decide to participate in the trial, the T cell depletion platform for the haplo-HSCT will be chosen by your treating phisicians between one of these two options:

- PTCY: administration of Cyclophosphamide at the dose of 50 mg/kg of body weight on day +3 and +4 after HSCT. On day +5, immune suppressive prophylaxis with CSA (5mg/kg) and mofetil mycophenolate (MMF) 30 mg/kg divided in 2 doses per day will be started. These drugs will be administered first intravenously and then orally- If no GVHD is present, MMF will be progressively reduced starting from day +30 to day+40 and eventually discontinued.

- αβ T-Cell depl: the leukapheresis product collected from the donor will undergo depletion of TCR αβ T cells and B cells following the standardized protocol operating under Center’s standard procedures. The product of the depletion will be infused to the patient on day 0.

The follow-up duration required by the study is 5 years from HSCT date.

Before taking part in the trial, your doctor asked you to perform some tests and checked if you have the characteristics required to join it:

- Medical History

- Routine blood analyis

- Microbiological analysis

- Radiological exams

- Bone marrow aspirate: cytology, cytometry, and MRD

- Lumbar puncture in case of CNS involvement

During the trial, invasive procedures (e.g. biopsies, bone marrow sampling, etc.) are planned, which are better detailed below.

The entire program of tests and examinations provided for during the trial is reported in the next section "What examinations, tests and procedures are provided for in the trial?"

- What are the risks and benefits if I participate in the trial?

Participation in this trial may result in both risks and benefits. It is important to evaluate them carefully before making a decision.

Expected benefits

By joining the trial, you will undergo the type of T depletion required for haplo-HSCT chosen for you by your treating physicians between the two aforementioned options. The transplant outcome data will be merged with those of other patients participating in the protocol, and will be analized for a comparative study. By joining the trial you will not have a direct immediate benefit, but your participation to the study will help the clinicians to discriminate whether there are differences in terms of transplant success between the two T - cell depletion platforms. In the future you could benefit yourself as well as other patients undergoing transplant from an haploidentical donor.

DESCRIBE THE BENEFITS ACCORDING TO THE TRIAL STAGE.

Clinical studies conducted in patients with acute leukaemia who received T-cell depleted haplo-HSCT have shown that this approache is effective in treating your disease. Both transplant platform are equally effective in treating childhood acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, based on retrospective studies. A large prospective study comparing the outcomes of PT-Cy haplo-HSCT and TCR αβ/CD19+ depleted HSCT has not yet been attempted. This study will focus on comparing the outcome of patients treated with these two transplant strategies. The choice of the T-cell depletion strategy will be based on center expertise.

Potential risks

We want to make sure that you immediately understand some possible risks: additional information can be found in the next section “What risks can I face if I participate in this trial”?

The risks you may face by participating in this trial are the generic risks associated with the allogeneic haploidentical donor transplant procedure.

Conditioning chemotherapy before a transplant leads to immune deficiency, making you susceptible to infections from pathogens such as bacteria, viruses and fungi. Some of these infections may be incurable or even fatal. In T-cell depleted transplant, immune recovery is often delayed and this may increase risk of infections. To minimise this risk, you will be given drugs such as antibiotics, as well as drugs against viruses and fungi.

The conditioning therapy has similar side effects to the initial treatment, including nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, mumps, hair loss, impaired kidney and liver function, bladder inflammation, heart failure and lung problems. Other drugs may be given to treat these side effects. Production of red blood cells (needed for oxygen transport) and platelets (needed for blood clotting) is also temporarily affected. These cells must be replaced by blood transfusions.

Historically, haplo-HCT has been associated with higher graft failure rates, largely due to intense bidirectional alloreactive reactions between donor and recipient. Both aforementioned strategies are employed to overcome this, though they only reduce, rather than eliminate, this risk.

Graft versus Host Disease, which consists of donor T cells recognising as foreign host body cells and attacking them, may occur after transplantation. Organs affected by GvHD include the skin (e.g. rash), liver (e.g. reduced function) and intestines (e.g. watery stools). To diagnose GvHD, a biopsy of the skin or intestines may be necessary. GvHD also reduces the immune system's ability to fight infection. It is difficult to predict whether GvHD will occur, and sometimes viral infections themselves trigger this complication. To prevent this reaction, patients receiving haplo-HSCT will be given cyclophosphamide after transplant, alongside mycophenolate mofetil and cyclosporine, or will be infused with a previously manipulated αβ+ T/B cell depleted graft, depending on the choice made from your treating phisicians.

Unfortunately, even years later, leukemia can still recur, so it is important to have regular follow-up visit in order to detect any complications as soon as possible.

Years after conditioning, late effects of chemotherapy and irradiation may occur. The most common late effects are growth retardation, pulmonary dysfunction, and infertility.

C.  INFORMATION SECTION. FURTHER INFORMATION

1.  What is the purpose of the trial?

The purpose of this trial is to compare two different methods of eliminating T cells in Haplo-HSCT in children and young adults: administering Cyclophosphamyde after transplant (PTCY) or removing αβ T cells from the graft (αβ T-Cell Depletion).

In the PTCY group, the T-cell depletion is realized through the administration of Cyclophosphamide, which is able to selectively deplete alloreactive T cells, mainly involved in inducing GvHD, while sparing non-alloreactive T cells, which could protect the patient from infection. Cyclophosphamide will be administered along with CSA and mofetil mycophenolate (MMF) for GvHD prophilaxis.

In the αβ T-Cell Depl group, the leukapheresis product collected from the donor will undergo depletion of TCR αβ T cells and B cell following the standardized protocol operating under Center’s standard procedures.

2.  What are the patient groups compared? What is the intervention being tested?

  The following platforms are the only ones permitted:

- Haplo-HSCT following αβTCR/CD19 depletion;

- Unmanipulated haplo-HSCT followed by post-transplant cyclophosphamide administration in addition to CSA and MMF

The eligibility for the study will be confirmed by checking for inclusion and exclusion criteria. Pediatric and young adult patients undergoing haplo-HSCT for ALL, and without major organ damage or uncontrolled infections will be included in the study. Transplantation will have to be performed in one of the centres that are part of the FORUM 2 study.

Clinical trial design

Patients diagnosed with ALL and aged between 0 and 25 years, who lack an HLA-identical sibling donor or a matched donor, will be stratified between GvHD prophylaxis with PT-Cy or αβ T cell and B cell depletion.

The selection of the approach and the relative stratification will be determined by the treating investigator. This decision will be based on the patient's characteristics, in addition to center-specific experience and availability.[Immagine che contiene testo, schermata, software Descrizione generata automaticamente]

3.  What examinations, tests and procedures are provided for if I participate in the trial?

During the screening procedure and post-transplant visits, patients will undergo a physical, laboratory and radiological examinations, depending on their clinical condition, with the addition of a visit at +30,+ 60, +120, +180, +270 and +360 days, and then yearly until the end of the post-transplant period.

The evaluations will include:

- General examination (with measurement of blood pressure and heart rate, etc.) and record of the medications you are taking;

- Blood samples: routine analysis; immune reconstitution and chimerism analysis at the aforementioned timepoints;

- Urine collection;

- Radiological examinations are performed, depending on clinical assessments and during long-term follow-ups

- PEDsQLTM questionnaire and PedsQL™ Stem Cell Transplant Module

- Long-term follow-up visits including endocrinological, pneumological and cardiological assessment yearly

  A bone marrow evaluation will be performed at +30, +60 and +100 days, as well as at +6, + 9,+ 12, +18 and +24 months after transplantation.

4.  What risks can I face if I participate in the trial? (section no longer than 4 pages)

The risks associated with participating in this stratification study are those related to the transplant procedure, as detailed above.

In particular:

1.  Transplant related mortality (TRM), due to severe infections, Transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy, toxicities, GvHD, etc;

2.  Graft failure;

3.  Acute and chronic GvHD;

4.  Secondary malignancies;

5.  Reduction or abolition of fertility;

6.  Possible risks to the fetus in case of pregnancy.

10. Who will have access to my health data, including personal data, and how will they be treated during the trial?

Your data, in particular personal and health data, will be processed only to the extent that they are indispensable to the objective of the trial and for pharmacovigilance purposes, in compliance with EU Regulation 2016/679, known as GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation) and with Legislative Decree 10 August 2018, no. 101. In practical terms, the documents relating to the participant will be kept in a safe place and will not report his/her name in clear text, only known to the researchers, but an identification code.

The data, made anonymous, may be subject to control by regulatory bodies and used for scientific publications (journals, conferences).

Your clinical data collected for the purposes of the trial, as well as the results of the tests carried out, will be stored for the time required by the regulations and subsequently destroyed. They will not be destroyed only if a) it is no longer possible to trace them back to your identity, because they have been anonymised during the trial itself; b) in the presence of your specific informed consent.

If the personal data are transferred to a third country or to an international organization, all the guarantees will be adopted which are provided for by Article 46 of the GDPR 679/2016.

Further information is included in the attached data processing authorization form.

11. How will my biological samples taken for trial purposes be treated and who will have access to them?

As for your health data, also your biological samples will be used for the purposes of the trial in a pseudonymised way (technique allowing to change and mask personal and sensitive data of a natural person, in order not to make them directly and easily attributable to him/her).

Once the trial is over, your samples will be destroyed. They will not be destroyed only if: a) it is no longer possible to trace them back to your identity, because they have been anonymised during the trial itself, or b) in the presence of your specific informed consent and agreement with the biobank for sample storage.

The law provides for the possibility that participants have access to the results of the trial. Therefore, you can ask the investigating physician to communicate the general results of the trial .
