What is this clinical study for?[]

we want to compare the efficacy and safety of the two types of half-identical (haploidentical) family donor transplantation most frequently employed to treat your disease. Studies performed so far have shown that both types works safely in children with your disease, however more studies are needed to better define if they are equivalent.

We want to check if there are any differences in efficacy, tolerability and side effects between patients who received a haploidentical transplantation based on the administration of a drug called cyclophosphamide after the transplant, or based on the elimination of specific cells from the graft.

What will happen to me during the study?

During the study, you will receive a haploidentical transplant as chosen from your physician between these two alternative:

1)  Administration of the drug cyclophosphamide intravenously on days +3 and +4 after transplant, alongside the drugs cyclosporine and mycophenolate, during the early period after transplant. These drugs will be administered first intravenously, and then by mouth.

2)  The graft that will be collected from your donor will be free of two cell populations: alpha/beta T cells and B cells. We'll get rid of the cells in the lab before we do the transplant.[]

First of all, you will undergo some tests to verify that you can really take part in the study.

[]

At regular intervals you will undergo examinations and check-ups during which the doctor will ask you to give him indications on how you feel.

How long will the study last?[]

The study we propose will last 5 years, during which you will be in constant contact with the center where the clinical study is being performed.

Will I have any benefit from participating in the study?

[]

  Doctors are interested in understanding which type of haploidentical donor transplantation is optimal to treat your disease. To answer this question, they decided to compare patients treated with the two most frequently used types of haploidentical transplantation. Your participation in this study will not produce a benefit for you, but it is important for the doctors who treat you, who in this way will be able to better treat other patients with your disease. This information could help children with your own disease in the future.

Am I taking risks in participating in the study?

[]All medical practices involve risks, even those you are already doing.

In particular, in this case the risks will be those of transplantation.

However, you will always be under control, as you already are now. For your part, immediately report to your parents any discomfort you may have, such as headaches, stomach pains, nausea. Doctors will help you get better.

Can you explain it better?

You may get infections, especially in the first few months after transplantation.

You may develop itching, skin rash, diarrhea, nausea or vomiting, or changes in liver function. These symptoms may be related to the effect of the transplant cells on your body.

In some cases the cells in your body may attack the transplant cells.

Years after the transplant your growth may be slowed.
