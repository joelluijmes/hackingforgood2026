CHAPTER I – DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN PARTICIPATING

Why is this sub-study being conducted?

This sub-study (R2 FORUX study) is part of the larger FORUM2 platform trial, which aims to optimize the way stem cell transplantation is performed for children and young people with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

For patients with a diagnosis of high-risk or relapsed ALL, an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT) is one of the recommended treatments. This means that you will receive healthy stem cells from a suitable donor. This approach is part of routine care for this kind of ALL and has been shown to improve long-term survival.

A possible complication that can occur after receiving a stem cell transplant from a donor is acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (aGvHD). Acute GvHD occurs when immune cells from the donor (contained in the graft) attack your body, mistakenly recognizing it as foreign. This immune response causes inflammation and damage to tissues, and it can seriously affect the success of the transplant.

Preventive treatments (called prophylaxis) can reduce the risk of developing aGvHD, but this complication still affects 30% to 50% of patients receiving a transplant, this variability depending on individual risk factors.

The standard first treatment for aGvHD is corticosteroids. Unfortunately, these only lead to full recovery in about 40% to 50% of patients. For those patients who don’t respond well to steroids (a condition known as steroid-refractory aGvHD, or SR-aGvHD), the outcome is often poor. If corticosteroids don’t work well enough, doctors may use second-line treatments.

A new medication, Ruxolitinib, has shown better results than other second-line options in clinical studies conducted in patients with SR-aGvHD. Ruxolitinib is a targeted drug taken by mouth that blocks specific proteins (JAK1 and JAK2) involved in the immune response responsible for GvHD development. It is now approved for use in both children and adults who have either acute or chronic GvHD and who haven’t responded well to steroids or other systemic treatments.

The purpose of this sub-study is to improve treatment for children and adolescents with newly diagnosed acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (aGvHD) by testing whether adding ruxolitinib to standard corticosteroid therapy as first treatment leads to better outcomes than using corticosteroids alone.

This sub-study aims to find out if starting ruxolitinib early, together with corticosteroids, can:

- Increase the likelihood of treatment success (overall response rate)

- Prevent progression to more severe forms of aGvHD

- Reduce the need for long-term steroid or other immunosuppressive treatments

- Improve survival, recovery of the immune system, and overall quality of life

By participating in this sub-study, researchers hope to offer a more effective and safer first-line treatment option for children with aGvHD, and reduce the long-term risks associated with current long-term steroid use such as infections, high blood sugar, bone loss, or growth delays.

The FORUX (R2) sub-study, is a Phase III clinical trial, meaning it is designed to rigorously compare the new combination treatment against the current standard of care in a larger group of pediatric patients. If the results are positive, this combination could become the new standard first-line treatment for aGvHD in children and adolescents.

The sub-study is expected to take place in approximately 90 centers across 20 European countries and 192 children and adolescents aged 3 months to 18 years, will be included. All participating centres are experienced transplant centres following strict international protocols. We estimate that 1 Belgian patient per year will be included in this sub-study.

Why am I being asked to take part?

You have been diagnosed with acute Graft-versus-Host Disease (aGvHD), a possible complication that can occur after receiving a stem cell transplant from a donor (called an allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant, or HSCT).

We are asking you to give your consent to participate in this R2 FORUX sub-study because we want to verify if the combination of Ruxolitinib and Corticosteroids is safe and more effective when compared to Corticoids alone for the treatment of newly diagnosed aGvHD that has not received any systemic therapy.

Do I have to take part in a trial?

Your participation in a trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion. This means that you have the right not to take part in the trial or to withdraw at any time without giving a reason, even if you previously agreed to take part. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the investigator or your treating physician nor will it affect the quality of your future medical care.

If other treatments are available for HSCT for ALL, the investigator or his/her delegate will discuss these treatments with you.

What will happen during the sub-study? 

If you agree to take part, the first step will be the screening period, which is the time between signing the informed consent form and the start of treatment. This period is designed to confirm that you meet the eligibility criteria to take part in the study.

The screening period will begin when you are diagnosed with treatment-naïve grade II aGvHD and will last no more than 7 days. During this time, several medical assessments will be performed including:

- Review of your medical history and current health status

- Physical examination

- Blood tests

- Other clinical evaluations to rule out other possible diagnoses and confirm aGvHD

All the procedures during the screening procedure are performed in the regular care as usual and are not study specific. Some procedures will have been done before the screening period. No study-specific procedures will be done before the signature of the ICF.

Once you are confirmed as eligible, treatment will begin promptly, ideally within 48 hours after randomization, to reduce the risk of disease progression and avoid unnecessary delays. With the randomization , you will be randomly assigned to one of two treatment groups, either (1) ruxolitinib in combination with corticosteroids, or (2) corticosteroids alone, which is the current standard treatment:

- Group 1

You will receive RUXOLITINIB in combination with corticosteroids (methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg/day or equivalent prednisone), along with calcineurine (either cyclosporine A or tacrolimus).

- Ruxolitinib is an oral medication that is taken by mouth in tablets or liquid formulation.

- The exact dosage and schedule of ruxolitinib will be determined based on your age and weight and will be administered daily alongside corticosteroids.

- Group 2 – Standard Treatment (Control Group):
  You will receive corticosteroids alone (methylprednisolone 2 mg/kg/day or equivalent prednisone), along with calcineurine (either cyclosporine A or tacrolimus).

  - This represents the current standard of care used in clinical practice.

The planned treatment period for aGvHD in this study is 24 weeks (approximately 6 months). You will regularly visit the hospital as usual. These visits will occur weekly during the first 8 weeks, and then once every 4 weeks until the end of the treatment period, which should last approximately 6 months (24 weeks) from treatment initiation.

Treatment will begin on study day 1 and continue unless one of the following occurs earlier:

• Treatment failure (no response or worsening of GvHD)

• Mixed response (partial improvement with some worsening)

• Relapse (return of symptoms)

• Occurrence of serious adverse events

• Withdrawal from the study or death

During the treatment period, care will be adapted based on how well the patient responds to therapy. Response will be formally assessed for the first time on study day 28.

- If improvement is shown (partial or complete response) by day 28:

• Investigational group (ruxolitinib + corticosteroids): Ruxolitinib will continue at the prescribed dose until at least day 42, while corticosteroids will be gradually tapered (slowly reduced) after achieving complete response. If stable, the physician may begin tapering ruxolitinib after day 42 based on clinical judgment. Tapering should ideally be completed by week 24, unless medically indicated to continue longer.

• Corticosteroids-only group: Corticosteroids may be tapered following standard guidelines even before day 28 after achieving complete response, depending on clinical condition.

- If no improvement is shown by day 28:

• Investigational group: Treatment will be considered a failure. The study physician may prescribe additional systemic therapies based on medical needs. The patient will then enter the long-term follow-up phase while remaining carefully monitored within the clinical trial.

• Corticosteroids-only group: The physician may start ruxolitinib at any time during the treatment period if criteria for steroid-refractory aGvHD are met. If started before day 28, this will be recorded as treatment failure for analysis purposes. If started after day 28, it will be considered a formal "cross-over" to investigational treatment.

Patients who cross over to ruxolitinib after starting in the corticosteroids-only group will follow the same treatment and tapering plan as those who started with ruxolitinib. They will be monitored weekly for 8 weeks after ruxolitinib initiation and then monthly until tapering completion.

In all cases, patients receiving ruxolitinib will continue to take corticosteroids and calcineurine (cyclosporine A or tacrolimus) per standard practice, unless the physician identifies specific contraindications. Tapering of these drugs will be based on treatment response.

Approximately 30 days after taking the last dose of the study drug, the study physician will contact you (by phone or to schedule a visit) for a safety follow-up assessment to check your general health status.

After the treatment period, you will be asked to return to the hospital or study center for long-term follow-up visits at 9, 12, 18, and 24 months after treatment initiation. These visits are important for continued health monitoring and are done within the standard of care.

Depending on treatment response or if GvHD symptoms reappear, the study physician may decide to continue or gradually taper treatment for up to an additional 24 weeks (approximately 6 months). In such cases, study visits will continue every 4 weeks as described above.

The "end of treatment" visit will be scheduled upon completion of ruxolitinib intake—at week 24, week 48, or earlier depending on the study physician's clinical decision. If treatment ends before week 24, follow-up visits and assessments will continue according to the planned schedule.

All visits and examinations are performed within the standard of care treatment (no study specific visits or examinations). The following is study specific in this sub-study:

-completion of questionnaires to evaluate quality of life – see below

-blood samples – see below

Standard of care procedures or examinations for your condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security). If you need more details or if you are not affiliated with a mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security), please contact the trial staff.

QOL questionnaires

During this sub-study, quality of life (QOL) will be assessed through questionnaires that need to be completed at the following timepoints:

- before start of your ruxolitinib treatment

- day 28 after treatment start

- day 56 after treatment start

- day 100 after treatment start

- day 168 after treatment start

- day 30 after last dose of medication

Specifically for the sub-study, the FACT-BNT and Eq-5D questionnaire will need to be completed by patients from 18 years onwards. For patients between 2 and 18 years, a PEDsQL and PEDsQL Stem cell transplant module questionnaire needs to be completed. Per timepoint, this will take approximately 20 minutes to complete. For this sub-study, there will in total be an extra time investment of approximately 2 hours for the completion of these questionnaires.

Blood samples

In addition to routine laboratory tests, study specific blood samples will be collected for:

• Biomarker analysis

• Pharmacokinetic (pK) analysis—to understand how ruxolitinib is absorbed, distributed, metabolized, and eliminated by the body

Blood samples will be taken at standard of care timepoints (no additional study visits needed) and will be combined with blood samples that are taken within the standard of care. It is possible that an additional puncture is needed for pharmacokinetic blood samples as this requires also blood samples taken after ruxolitinib is administered.

Will I benefit from the sub-study? 

Ruxolitinib may or may not be beneficial in treating your ALL or relieving your symptoms. Even if it is beneficial to you, a potential return or worsening of symptoms, illness or disease is still possible.

The information obtained during a trial may contribute to a better understanding of the use of the investigational medicinal product ruxolitinib and may improve care and outcomes of yourself and future patients with ALL undergoing a stem cell transplantation.

What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?

1.  What are the possible side effects

Participation in a trial involves some risk.

If you decide to participate in this sub-study, it is important to understand that the investigational treatment, namely the combination of ruxolitinib and corticosteroids, may not be more effective than the current standard treatment of corticosteroids alone. In some cases, it may be less effective or could potentially be associated with an increased risk of leukemia relapse, although current evidence does not conclusively indicate this.

If you do not fully respond to treatment or if aGvHD symptoms worsen, additional therapies will be provided based on clinical need. Regardless of which treatment group you are assigned to, you will continue to be followed and monitored closely throughout the study.

There is also a risk that the investigational treatment may cause more frequent or more severe side effects than the standard therapy. For example, combining ruxolitinib with corticosteroids may lead to:

- An increased risk of infections.

- A decrease in blood cell counts, such as red cells (anemia), white cells (leukopenia), neutrophils (neutropenia) and platelets (thrombocytopenia).

In previous studies involving pediatric patients with aGvHD, the most common treatment-related side effects included:

- Anemia

- Low white blood cell counts

- Low neutrophil counts

Some patients experienced serious side effects, such as:

- Infections

- Fever (pyrexia)

- Kidney problems (acute kidney injury)

- Sepsis (a life-threatening infection)

Participation in this sub-study may involve risks related to ruxolitinib and procedures such as blood sampling. Side effects may vary from person to person. This document includes both common side effects and rare but serious side effects known to be associated with ruxolitinib. You are encouraged to discuss any concerns with the study doctor, including possible side effects not listed here.

Many side effects resolve shortly after stopping or adjusting the medication. However, some side effects may be serious, long-lasting, or permanent, and in rare cases, could be life-threatening.

Side effects of ruxolitinib

In patients treated for other conditions (such as myelofibrosis or polycythemia vera), very common side effects (occurring in more than 1 in 10 people) included:

- Low red blood cell count (anemia), which may cause fatigue, pale skin, or shortness of breath

- Low white blood cell count (neutropenia), which may increase the risk of infection

- Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia), which may lead to easy bruising or bleeding

- Urinary tract infections

- Increased cholesterol or triglyceride levels

- Headache, dizziness, weight gain

- Constipation, high blood pressure, altered liver test results

- Shingles (herpes zoster), which may cause painful rash or blisters

In most cases, changes in blood cell counts are reversible and can be managed by temporarily reducing or stopping ruxolitinib. You will be monitored regularly through blood tests, and if your cell counts do not recover quickly, ruxolitinib may be paused longer or restarted at a lower dose.

Common side effects (occurring in 1 to 10 out of 100 patients):

- Excessive intestinal gas (flatulence)

- Reduction in all three types of blood cells (pancytopenia)

In rare cases (less than 1 in 100), tuberculosis has been reported. Symptoms may include chronic cough, coughing up blood, fever, night sweats, and weight loss. Immediately inform the study physician if you experience any of these symptoms.

The effect of ruxolitinib on viral replication in people with chronic hepatitis B is not known. If you have ever had viral hepatitis, this must be discussed with the study doctor. Children with active viral infection will not be treated with ruxolitinib.

There have been rare reports of progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML), a serious brain infection, among patients who underwent allogeneic stem cell transplantation, including some receiving ruxolitinib. Although it is unclear whether ruxolitinib caused PML, symptoms such as confusion, speech difficulties, weakness on one side of the body, or vision problems must be reported immediately.

Cases of non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSC), including basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and the rare Merkel cell carcinoma, have been reported in patients treated with ruxolitinib. These may also be related to prior treatments or underlying disease. Inform the physician if you notice new or unusual skin lesions.

In animal studies, delayed bone growth and fractures were observed in very young animals treated with high doses of ruxolitinib. Although these findings occurred at doses higher than those used in this sub-study, the greatest concern applies to children under 2 years old, particularly those under 1 year. Bone growth will be monitored, and potential effects are expected to be reversible, although full growth recovery may not always occur.

Suddenly stopping ruxolitinib may also lead to health risks. In patients with aGvHD or myelofibrosis, abrupt discontinuation has been associated with a return of symptoms or inflammatory reactions, including anxiety, insomnia, weakness, or worsening disease. If treatment needs to be stopped, it will be done under close medical supervision.

Therefore, it is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to the investigator, regardless of whether or not you think it has to do with the sub-study, and even when it is already described in this document. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with the investigator beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

During treatment with ruxolitinib, you must immediately inform the study doctor if you:

- Develop unusual bruising, bleeding, extreme fatigue, shortness of breath, or frequent infections

- Show signs of shingles (painful skin rash with blisters)

- Have a persistent cough with blood, fever, night sweats, or weight loss (possible signs of tuberculosis)

- Experience confusion, weakness, visual disturbances, or trouble speaking or walking (possible signs of PML)

- Have any changes in prescribed medications or develop any new medical issues

- Develop new or changing skin lesions, which may require further examination

- Become pregnant or, if male, are known or suspected to have fathered a pregnancy during the study

  1.  What are the possible risks or discomforts of the examinations during the trial?

All examinations are performed within the standard of care. The taking of blood for the study specific blood samples will be combined with routine blood punctures as much as possible. Taking of blood may cause pain, bleeding, bruising or infection localised around the injection site. Similarly, some participants may feel dizzy or even faint during the procedure. The trial staff who take the blood will do all they can to minimize these discomforts.

Do not hesitate to ask your investigator for more explanation about the use of medicines or and food supplements (including aromatherapy, phytotherapy, homeopathy..).

We want to ensure that the trial runs smoothly. That is why we will make the following agreements with you:

• You will make sure you take all your drugs in the way needed

• During this trial, you won’t participate in another medical scientific trial,
unless sponsor approval was obtained prior to participation

  natural remedies, vitamins or over-the-counter drugs.

  o You need to be admitted to the hospital or receive treatment there.

  o You suddenly develop problems with your health.

  o You no longer want to take part in the trial.

  o Your phone number, address or e-mail address has changed.

4.  Can I get pregnant, conceive a child, or can I breastfeed during the trial?

Animal studies suggest ruxolitinib may harm a fetus or newborn. If pregnancy occurs or is suspected during the study or within 30 days after completing treatment, you must immediately inform the study physician and stop taking the study drug. Treatment continuation during pregnancy is not permitted.

Loss of fertility is expected in both males and females after HSCT, though individual risk depends on age, dose, and sex. Your treating physician will discuss in detail possible interventions for fertility preservation.

If you are a women, you must not become pregnant during the trial and for at least 12 months after completing treatment. In addition, you must not have egg cells stored for your own use or donation after the start of the treatment and for at least 12 months after the last treatment. You must not breastfeed during treatment.

If you are a men, you must not cause a pregnancy or donate sperm during the trial and for at least 6 months after completing treatment.

If you can become pregnant and are heterosexually active, or if you have a partner who can become pregnant, then the treating physician or nurse will explain the different types of contraceptives that you should use during and after the trial.

Your investigator will discuss with you the suitable forms of contraception. If you decide that you will participate in this trial, you or your female partner of the participant that can become pregnant, must use one of the recognised contraceptive methods (to prevent you or the female partner from becoming pregnant). If you/your partner is using oral hormonal contraception, you/your partner will need to use a barrier method such as condoms too.

If you or your partner become pregnant during this trial, you must tell the investigator or the trial staff immediately. For safety reasons, the investigator wants to monitor the pregnancy and its outcome and may be required to share this data with the sponsor to comply with regulations on monitoring and reporting safety information. You/your partner will receive a separate information letter regarding this, and a separate consent will have to be granted.

Also if you don’t participate in this study, you will receive HSCT and it is also important not to become pregnant or not to cause a pregnancy outside this study.

Which data are collected about me during the trial and what will happen with them?

Ethnic origin data maybe collected to ensure that research data are processed in the broadest possible population.

The investigator (and the trial staff) is bound by professional secrecy or by a confidentiality agreement about the data collected.

This means that he/she will never reveal your identity, including in a scientific publication or a lecture and that he/she will encode your data (Ref. Therefore, the investigator and the trial staff under the responsibility of the investigator, will be the only ones able to establish a link between your identity and the data transmitted during the trial, with the exceptions listed under section 12.5.

The data transmitted to the sponsor will not allow the sponsor to identify you.

3.  How will my data be handled?

Your trial data will be processed in accordance with the current European and Belgian data protection legislation.

The reason that your personal data may be processed for the purposes of the study is that it is processed for the conduct of scientific research and for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

 Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial and what will happen with them? 

1.  Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial?

Biological samples are samples of human body material. In this sub-study, the following biological sample(s) will be taken: blood samples - see Chapter I, section 4.

The procedure to encode your biological samples is the same as that used for your personal data in accordance with Belgian legislation on the use of human body material (see I § 12.2). Samples sent to the sponsor or to organisations working in collaboration with the sponsor, will only be labelled with your trial identification code. The sponsor ensures that the location of your biological samples is always tracked. The use of your biological samples is linked to the processing of the associated coded personal data.

Your biological samples are deemed to be a “donation”. You will not receive any financial benefit associated with the development of new therapies derived from the use of your biological samples, and which may have commercial value.

2.  What will happen to the collected biological samples?

These biological samples will be analysed for the objectives of the trial. Study-specific blood samples will be sent to the promotor of the study (Bambino Gesù Pediatric Hospital in Rome-Italy).

Genetic analyses will also be performed on your biological samples. Genetic analyses are studies of DNA. For example, DNA determines the colour of our hair and eyes. DNA can also explain why some people respond to certain drugs and others do not. It can also explain why some people get certain diseases and others do not. The purpose of these genetic analyses is to learn for example about the levels of ruxolitinib that enter the blood stream and how quickly it is broken down in the body (pharmacokinetics).

These genetic analyses will deliver essential information for the trial. If you do not want these analyses to be conducted, you will not be allowed to participate in the trial.

Since scientific progress in this area is constant, the sponsor would like to, with your consent retain the remainders of your biological samples for 25 years. The sponsor will use them for additional research that remains within the context of the current clinical trial and thus within the same disease or the same treatment/drug as in this study.

These samples may be used in the future for research related to this study, such as:

• Understanding aGvHD biology

• Improving methods to diagnose, monitor, or treat the disease

• Comparing data with other clinical studies or developing new clinical endpoints

There are two types of permitted future use of remaining samples:

1. Use by the same research group for purposes strictly related to this study

2. Use by different research groups, but only within joint scientific projects aligned with the study's objectives, within the same disease or the same treatment/drug.

At the end of this form, you agree or disagree to the use of the remainders of your samples for future research within the context of this trial by ticking the appropriate checkbox in Chapter II. Additional research outside of the trial must be approved by a recognized Ethics Committee.

You may revoke your consent at any time without affecting medical care. If you choose to revoke consent for future sample use, you may request:

• That all remaining samples be permanently anonymized (so they can no longer be linked to you), OR

• That the samples and associated data be destroyed
