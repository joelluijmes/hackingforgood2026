CHAPTER I – DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN PARTICIPATING

Why is this study being conducted?

You are invited to participate in the FORUM 2 platform trial, which aims to optimize the way stem cell transplantation is performed for children and young people with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

This information sheet and consent form refers only to the participation in the main study of the FORUM 2 platform trial and the associated data collection. You are not consenting to any treatment change, experimental intervention, or clinical sub-study at this point. Every further study or intervention will be explained in detail in a separate information and consent form, and your explicit written consent will be requested again before participation.

Why am I being asked to take part?

You have been diagnosed with ALL. Although treatment so far has led to complete remission (meaning that no leukaemia cells are currently detectable), there remains a significant risk that the leukaemia could come back based on the characteristics of your disease. To give you the best chance of a long-term cure, an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT) is recommended, in which you will receive healthy stem cells from a suitable donor.

The FORUM 2 platform trial is set up by the Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù (IRCCS) in Rome (Italy). This trial provides a shared infrastructure to address multiple scientific questions over time, within a single framework.

The platform trial consists of a main study and several predefined substudies that address different clinical questions. These include, for example:

• Group R1: comparing two different radiation doses used in conditioning therapy prior to HSCT

• Group R2: evaluating treatments for acute graft-versus-host disease (aGvHD)

• Group S1: assessing different immune suppression strategies for transplants from mismatched family donors

• Group P1: a pilot study on post-transplant maintenance therapy in young children

Each of these sub-studies above has its own specific eligibility criteria, timelines and a separate consent will be asked before your participation.

This information and consent form is only about the main study of the FORUM 2 platform trial, which focuses on the collection of medical data related to your standard of care HSCT for ALL. By agreeing to take part, you consent to collection of your clinical data during the standard of care treatment that you will receive.

Do I have to take part in a trial?

Your participation in this main study of the platform trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion. This means that you have the right not to take part in the trial or to withdraw at any time without giving a reason, even if you previously agreed to take part. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the investigator or your treating physician nor will it affect the quality of your future medical care.

If other treatments are available for HSCT for ALL, the investigator or his/her delegate will discuss these treatments with you.

If you agree to participate, your investigator will inform your treating physicians. You then cannot participate in another interventional clinical trial without informing the investigator or trial staff. You also need to communicate all relevant information related to the state of your health, other medication taken or the symptoms experienced.

What will happen during the trial? 

If you choose to participate in this main study of the FORUM2 platform trial, you will receive the standard of care HSCT and follow-up as usual. This main study does not involve any additional tests, medical procedures, or treatments beyond those that are part of your standard clinical care. You are not consenting to any treatment change, experimental intervention, or clinical sub-studies.

In this main study, we ask if we can collect clinical information from your standard of care treatment such as routine examinations and treatments before, during, and after your stem cell transplantation. This includes for example, information on laboratory results, transplantation characteristics, complications, and follow-up data. These data will be collected from your medical records and stored in a secure, pseudonymized database.

All visits and examinations are performed within the standard of care treatment (no study specific visits or examinations). The following is study specific

-completion of questionnaires to evaluate quality of life (mandatory)

-blood samples (optional)

QOL questionnaires

During this study, quality of life (QOL) will be assessed through questionnaires that need to be completed at the following timepoints:

- Before your stem cell transplantation

- After your stem cell transplantation at:

  - 1 month

  - 3 months

  - 6 months

  - 12 months

  - 18 months

  - 24 months

- In case you develop GvHD (graft versus host disease, a disease in which the donor cells attack the recipient's healthy tissues after a stem cell transplantation)

  - At onset GvHD

  - 9 months after stem cell transplantation only if you develop chronic GvHD

Specifically for the study, the FACT-BNT and Eq-5D questionnaire will need to be completed by patients from 18 years onwards. For patients between 2 and 18 years, a PEDsQL and PEDsQL Stem cell transplant module questionnaire needs to be completed. Per timepoint, this will take approximately 20 minutes to complete. For this main study, there will in total be an extra time investment of maximum 3 hours for the completion of these questionnaires (maximum 9 timepoints).

Blood samples (optional)

In addition to routine laboratory tests, study specific blood samples will be collected for ATG level analysis. Anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG), a polyclonal antibody, is given in the standard of care before a hematopoïtic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) to prevent that donor cells attack the recipient's healthy tissues (called graft-vs.-host-disease (GvHD)). For the study, the concentration of the active ATG will be measured in the blood at different timepoints to see how different patients metabolize this in their bodies. A blood sample of 4ml will be taken at the following timepoints:

- day 1 of ATG administration (before and after administration)

- day 2 of ATG administration (before and after administration)

- day 3 of ATG administration (before and after administration)

- At stem cell transplantation

- day 6 after HSCT

- day 15 after HSCT

- day 30 after HSCT

- day 35 after HSCT

Blood samples will be combined with blood samples that are taken within the standard of care (no additional blood punctures or study visits needed). It is possible that an additional puncture is needed for the ATG sample that is taken after the ATG administration.

All biological samples collected will be coded. This means your name and personal identifiers will be replaced with a unique code known only to the study team. Samples will be sent to the coordinating promotor of the study (Bambino Gesù Pediatric Hospital in Rome-Italy).

Genetic analyses can be performed on your samples. These genetic analyses provide essential information for the study and the future treatment of your disease.

These blood samples are optional. If you do not wish that these samples are collected, you can indicate this at the end of the informed consent.

This trial will take place in approximately 26 countries and will include more than 1000 participants worldwide, including about 4-5 patients per year in Belgium. Overall, your participation in this part of the trial will be until 5 years following your stem cell transplant during which information will be collected from your standard of care treatment.

Standard of care procedures or examinations for your condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security). If you need more details or if you are not affiliated with a mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security), please contact the trial staff.

Will I benefit from the trial? 

There is no direct medical benefit for you when you participate in this main study of the platform trial. However, your data obtained during your standard of care treatment may contribute to improving care and outcomes for future patients with ALL undergoing stem cell transplantation.

If you will be included in one of the sub-studies in the future, potential risks and benefits will be explained in a separate patient information and ICF.

What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?

1.  What are the possible side effects?

In this main study of the platform trial, you will receive the standard of care treatment. Clinical data from your standard of care treatment will be collected. Therefore, participation does not carry any direct medical risk compared to what is part of your routine care.

The risks you may face by participating in this study are the general risks associated with a stem cell transplantation procedure. Your treating physician will explain to you in detail which side effects you may experience during your standard treatment.

All medicinal products, even during standard of care treatment, can have side effects. It is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to the investigator. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with the investigator beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

2.  What are the possible risks or discomforts of the examinations during the trial?

All examinations are performed within the standard of care. The taking of blood for the study specific blood samples will be combined with routine blood punctures as much as possible. Taking of blood may cause pain, bleeding, bruising or infection localised around the injection site. Similarly, some participants may feel dizzy or even faint during the procedure. The trial staff who take the blood will do all they can to minimize these discomforts.

3.  Can I take other medicines during the trial?

Do not hesitate to ask your investigator for more explanation about the use of other medicines during your standard of care treatment.

4.  Will my participation to the trial have an impact on my daily activities?

We want to ensure that the trial runs smoothly. That is why we will make the following agreements with you:

• You will make sure you take all your drugs of your standard of care treatment
in the way needed

• During this trial, you won’t participate in another medical scientific trial,
unless sponsor approval was obtained prior to participation

• Please contact the investigator or research nurse in the following situations:

  o You will start to take other drugs. This includes homeopathic drugs,
  natural remedies, vitamins or over-the-counter drugs.

  o You need to be admitted to the hospital or receive treatment there.

  o You suddenly develop problems with your health.

  o You no longer want to take part in the trial.

  o Your phone number, address or e-mail address has changed.

What If something goes wrong within the trial?

Even if there is no fault, the sponsor is liable for harm caused to you whether directly or indirectly related to your participation in the trial. The sponsor has taken an appropriate insurance (a so called “No Fault insurance”) for this liability. A copy of the insurance certificate can be obtained from the investigator or trial staff.

If you (or in the event of death, your rightful claimants) seek compensation for a harm to your health as a direct or indirect result of participating in the trial, you must inform your investigator or trial staff promptly.

If the investigator believes that a link between the new or worsened health problem(s) and the trial is possible, he/she will inform the trial sponsor. The sponsor will then immediately initiate the declaration procedure to its insurance company. If the company considers it necessary, it will appoint an expert to assess whether there is a link between your reported health problem(s) and the trial. The insurance does not cover the natural progression of your disease/condition or the known side effects of the treatment you would have received without taking part to the trial (that is your standard treatment).

Whenever you feel it is appropriate or if you or your rightful claimants disagree either with the investigator or with the expert appointed by the insurance company, you may contact the insurance company or proceedings may be brought against the insurance company. You will find the contact details on the front page of this form.

What if other treatment options or new information become available during the course of the trial?

During the course of the trial, important new information might become available. For example, other standard of care treatments may become available. It is the duty of the investigator to discuss this new information with you. In this case, data of that new standard of care treatment will be collected. You always have the opportunity to withdraw your participation in the trial.

If you decide to stop taking part in the trial or if you are no longer able to participate, your investigator will see to it that you continue to receive the best possible medical care.

Can my participation in the trial end prematurely?

As explained in detail below, your trial participation may end prematurely when

- you decide to withdraw your consent or

- the investigator decides to end your trial participation, or

- other entities interrupt or end the trial.

In any case, if your trial participation ends prematurely, the investigator will discuss your future medical care with you. Data collected before you terminate your participation will continue to be used as described in this form. This is to avoid skewing / biasing results of the trial (as described in chapter I. § 12.3.).

Depending on your situation, the investigator will discuss with you whether follow-up visits or procedures are needed.

1.  You decide to withdraw your consent

You are entitled to withdraw your consent for any reason, at any time, without having to justify your decision. However, for your safety, you should inform the investigator of your decision. Although it is not mandatory, it may be useful for the investigator and for the sponsor to know the reason of this decision.

If you withdraw your consent, this means you decide that data from your standard of care treatment cannot be collected anymore. No new data will be collected and sent to the sponsor.

2.  The investigator decides to end your trial participation

The investigator may end your trial participation because

- he/she thinks it is better for you

- he/she determines that you are not following the instructions given to participants, or

- any other reason that will be explained,

- the entire study should be discontinued for all participants (see next paragraph).

If that happens, the trial staff will explain why and ensure appropriate follow-up.

1.  Other entities may interrupt or end the trial

The sponsor and the competent Belgian health authorities may interrupt or end the trial because

- the information gathered shows that the study is not effective (does not deliver a sufficient level of improvement in the health of the trial participants),

- there are more (serious) side effects than anticipated, or

- any other reason that will be duly motivated by such party.

Which treatment will I get after my participation in the trial? 

If you stop the study, the investigator will assess your health. He/she will prescribe you the best standard treatment available.

Will my participation in the trial involve extra costs for me?

As described in section 4, all visits and examinations are standard of care. The standard procedures or examinations for your condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security).

The study does not involve any additional costs. You will not receive any financial compensation for participating in this trial.

Which data are collected about me during the trial and what will happen with them?

1.  Which data are collected and processed during the trial?

The collected and processed personal data concern information about your health and medical condition. This includes your medical history, some of your background information (for example your age and sex) and the results of examinations.

Ethnic origin data are collected to ensure that research data are processed in the broadest possible population.

2.  How will the investigator treat my personal data?

The investigator (and the trial staff) is bound by professional secrecy or by a confidentiality agreement about the data collected.

This means that he/she will never reveal your identity, even in a scientific publication or lecture and that he/she will encode your data (Ref. [1]) (that is replacing your identity by an identification code in the trial) before sending them to the sponsor. This code will not contain any personal identifier or combination of identifiers such as name, first name, initials, full or partial date of birth, file number, etc.

The investigator and the trial staff under the responsibility of the investigator, will be the only ones able to establish a link between your identity and the data transmitted during the trial, with the exceptions listed under section 12.5.

The data transmitted to the sponsor will not allow the sponsor to identify you.

3.  How will my data be handled?

Your trial data will be processed in accordance with the current European and Belgian data protection legislation.

The reason that your personal data may be processed for the purposes of the study is that it is processed for the conduct of scientific research and for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

4.  Do I have access to my data collected and processed during the trial and can I rectify them?

You are entitled to ask the investigator what data are being collected about you and how those data will be used in connection with the trial.

You have the right

- to inspect and access these data

- to receive the personal data that are collected

- to ask for correction if they are incorrect

- to restrict the processing of your data

- to withdraw your consent for the processing of personal data. However personal data collected before withdrawal will be kept to avoid skewing of results in the trial.

It is not possible to have all your data erased to avoid skewing of results in the trials.

1.  Who, other than the Investigator and his trial staff, has access to my personal data?

To verify the quality of the trial, it is possible that your personal uncoded data or information in your medical records relevant for the trial, will be examined by people outside the trial staff but under the responsibility of the investigator. These persons must be subject to professional secrecy or a confidentiality agreement. The following might be considered:

- the personnel designated by the sponsor of the trial (monitors and auditors), and people or organisations providing services for, or collaborating with,

- the sponsor. They will however never transfer your name and contact details to the sponsor.

- inspectors of competent health authorities worldwide

- an independent audit group

- people designated by the Ethics Committee

For the needs of the trial, the encoded trial data may be sent to other EU and non-EU countries and may be reviewed by

- personnel (other than the inspectors) of competent health authorities of Belgium (Federal agency for medicines and health products, FAMHP) and other EU and non-EU countries,

- the evaluating Belgian Ethics Committee(s),

- external researchers,

- the sponsor of the trial, personnel designated by the sponsor, and people or organisations providing services for or collaborating with the sponsor, and/or

- group companies of the sponsor in Belgium, and in other EU and non-EU countries.

The European regulation and the Belgian legislation on data protection have requirements for transferring data to non-EU countries. The sponsor must ensure equivalent guarantees regarding personal data protection standards before transferring the encoded data to non-EU countries. If for this purpose, there is a data protection agreement, a copy of this agreement may be obtained via the investigator. You can always contact your investigator to obtain more information about any such transfers.

1.  What will happen to the results of the trial?

After trial closure, a description and the results of this clinical trial will be published in specialised medical journals. A copy of the scientific publication or if available a summary for laypersons can be obtained from the investigator or the trial staff.

A description of the trial will also be available on https://euclinicaltrials.eu/search-for-clinical-trials/?lang=en and/or https://www.Clinicaltrials.gov. You can search these websites at any time using the information on the front page of the informed consent form. Within 1 year after the study's completion, the website will contain a summary of the results in accordance with the European Commission's guidelines.

These websites or publications will not include information that can identify you.

2.  Will my data be used for other purposes than for the trial in which I take part?

The results of the trial will be used to answer questions to improve the treatment of ALL patients who need an HSCT. In addition, the sponsor would like to use your data obtained from this trial, in connection with other research and development activities (and the associated scientific publications). This future research can only concern research about the same disease or medication.

Any additional research outside of the trial and therefore not described in the above points, must be approved by a recognized Ethics Committee.

At the end of this form, you agree or disagree to the use of your trial data for other purposes by ticking the appropriate checkbox in Chapter II.

3.  How long will my data be kept?

After the end of the trial your encoded data will be retained for at least 25 years in accordance with the European clinical trial regulation to ensure the validity of the research. This will also be the case if you stopped trial participation prematurely.

 Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial and what will happen with them? 

1.  Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial?

Biological samples are samples of human body material. In this study, the following biological sample(s) will be taken: blood samples - see Chapter I, section 4.

The procedure to encode your biological samples is the same as that used for your personal data in accordance with Belgian legislation on the use of human body material (see I § 12.2). Samples sent to the sponsor or to organisations working in collaboration with the sponsor, will only be labelled with your trial identification code. The sponsor ensures that the location of your biological samples is always tracked. The use of your biological samples is linked to the processing of the associated coded personal data.

Your biological samples are deemed to be a “donation”. You will not receive any financial benefit associated with the development of new therapies derived from the use of your biological samples, and which may have commercial value.

2.  What will happen to the collected biological samples?

These biological samples will be analysed for the objectives of the trial. Study-specific blood samples will be sent to the promotor of the study (Bambino Gesù Pediatric Hospital in Rome-Italy).

Genetic analyses will also be performed on your biological samples. Genetic analyses are studies of DNA. For example, DNA determines the colour of our hair and eyes. DNA can also explain why some people respond to certain drugs and others do not. It can also explain why some people get certain diseases and others do not.

These genetic analyses will deliver essential information for the trial. At the end of this form, you agree or disagree to the genetic analysis of your samples by ticking the appropriate checkbox in Chapter II.

Since scientific progress in this area is constant, the sponsor would like to, with your consent retain the remainders of your biological samples for 25 years. The sponsor will use them for additional research that remains within the context of the current clinical trial and thus within the same disease or the same treatment/drug as in this study.

These samples may be used in the future for research related to this study, such as:

• Understanding aGvHD biology

• Improving methods to diagnose, monitor, or treat the disease

• Comparing data with other clinical studies or developing new clinical endpoints
