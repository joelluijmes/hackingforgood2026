CHAPTER I – DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN your child is PARTICIPATING

Why is this sub-study being conducted?

This sub-study is part of the larger FORUM2 platform trial, which aims to optimize the way stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is performed for children and young people with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

The purpose of this sub-study is to evaluate if the outcome can be improved for children below 2 years of age that are affected by CD19+ B-cell ALL by testing whether using blinatumomab after HSCT leads to a reduction of leukemia relapse.

The current standard of care treatment for children below 2 years of age with an indication for HSCT is the transplant itself, preceded by a intensive conditioning regimen which is chemotherapy-based.

The previous FORUM study has shown that in children younger than 2 years:

- the rate of relapse after HSCT in children with B-ALL below the age of 2 years is particularly high and

- they cannot benefit from the relapse-reducing effect of TBI (Total body irradiation) as part of the conditioning regimen because this is associated with an excessive risk of severe long-term toxicities, in particular on growth and neurocognitive development.

Therefore, we want to administer blinatumomab in this sub-study in order to try to reduce the risk of relapse. Blinatumomab is a kind of immunotherapy authorized to treat some high-risk CD19+ B-cell ALL and will be given as prophylaxis (prevention) of relapse after HSCT. Blinatumomab, a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) targeting CD19 will stimulate T cells to eliminate CD19-positive cells, including leukemia cells, with a favourable toxicity profile when compared to traditional chemotherapy. The BiTE has demonstrated effectiveness both in pediatric and adult populations. In infants with ALL, blinatumomab was recently shown to be safe and effective, with an excellent safety profile.

Generally, well tolerated, particularly in the setting of low-burden disease, blinatumomab represents an ideal drug to be used in the post-HSCT setting. Consolidation strategies after allogeneic HSCT to prevent relapse in patients with CD19-positive B-ALL are under investigation, and blinatumomab has been shown to be feasible.

This sub-study aims to find out whether treatment with blinatumomab up to 4 cycles after HSCT can improve the outcome of children < 2 years of age with CD19+ B-ALL.

The main goals of the sub-study are to:

• Reduce the incidence of relapse

• Improve survival after HSCT

• Evaluate the tolerability of Blinatumomab after HSCT

• Evaluate the effect of blinatumomab on post-transplant reconstitution of the
immune system.

This sub-study is expected to take place in approximately 30 centers across 20 European countries and 40 children below 2 years of age will be included. Recruitment will take maximum 5 years and we expect to include a maximum of 3 Belgian patients.

Why is my child being asked to take part?

Your child has been diagnosed with CD19+ B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) and is between aged 3 months to 2 years of age. Your child was transplanted following a chemotherapy-based conditioning regimen.

We are asking you to give your consent to participate in this sub-study because we want to verify if the use of blinatumomab after HSCT in children <2 years of age is safe and effective in reducing the incidence of leukemia relapse.

The investigator or trial staff will discuss with you the requirements to be allowed to enter the sub-study.

Does my child have to take part in a trial?

Your child’s participation in this trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion. This means that you/your child have the right not to take part in the trial or to withdraw at any time without giving a reason, even if you previously agreed to take part. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the investigator or your child’s treating physician nor will it affect the quality of the future medical care of your child.

If other treatments are available for HSCT for ALL, the investigator or his/her delegate will discuss these treatments with you.

What will happen during the sub-study? 

Before your child’s participation in this sub-study, the treating physician will make sure your child is eligible to take part and to ensure that treatment begins as quickly and safely as possible.

During this screening period, the study team will carry out a number of medical assessments, including:

• Review of your child’s medical history and current health status

• Physical examination

• Blood tests

• Bone marrow aspirate

The screening period will begin once you have signed the informed consent, up to 14 days before the start of the treatment and will last no more than 14 days. All examinations are performed within the standard of care because your child will still be in close follow-up after the stem cell transplantation.

Once your child is confirmed as eligible, study treatment will begin promptly to reduce the risk of disease relapse and avoid unnecessary delays.

Treatment will consist of up to four 28-days cycles of blinatumomab infused intravenously, separated by a 2-month break (see image below). In patients with signs of possible disease recurrence (i.e., positive minimal residual disease) at any time-point after HSCT, this interval period can be reduced to 1 month. Blinatumomab treatment will begin on day 60 +/- 10, after HSCT.

Blinatumomab will be given at the dose of 15 mcg/m2/day as a 24-hour continuous i.v. infusion over a 28 days course. The drug will be administered though specific injectors that will be provided to you/your child. Patients will stay in the hospital the first 3 days of a Blinatumomab course. The remaining treatment can continue in an outpatient setting. The hospitalization time depends on investigator’s judgment, as well as safety and tolerability of blinatumomab. The drug administration should not be interrupted, if possible. In case of infusion interruption due to technical or logistical interruption (e.g., diagnostic measurement), the interruption should be as short as possible and the infusion continued at the earliest time possible. If the interruption is longer than 4 hours, re-start of the infusion should be performed in the hospital, under the supervision of the investigator.

The planned treatment period in this sub-study is 10 months. Your child’s treatment will begin on Study Day 1 and continue according to the scheme unless one of the following occurs earlier:

• A leukaemia relapse occurs

• Serious side effects occur and do not resolve after reduction/interruption of the
treatment

• Your child withdraws from the study or passes away

During the treatment period, your child’s care will be adjusted based on how well they respond to the therapy. The response (i.e., maintenance of remission) will be formally assessed through bone marrow at the end of the first cycle and before each subsequent cycle.

In order to understand if blinatumomab is effective as compared to standard of care (i.e., no additional treatments), the results of treated patients will be compared to those of patients enrolled in the previous FORUM 1 study (historical cohort).

If you agree to allow your child to take part in this sub-study, s/he will need to be hospitalized for 3-7 days at treatment initiation. Afterwards you will come to the hospital regularly and your child will have a close follow-up as usual after a stem cell transplantation. .

About 30 days after your child takes their last dose of Blinatumomab, the study doctor will contact you (by phone or to schedule a visit) to carry out a safety follow-up assessment to check on your child’s overall health.

After the treatment period, you and your child will be asked to return to the hospital for long-term follow-up visits every 6 months up to 2 years after HSCT. These visits are important for continued health monitoring and are also done within the standard of care (will also be done if your child does not participate in this study).

Blinatumomab will be provided free of charge and the associated hospitalisation will not be charged to you. All other visits and all examinations are performed within the standard of care treatment (no study specific examinations).

If your child will not participate in this sub-study with blinatumomab, your child will not receive blinatumomab and will receive the regular intensive follow-up after a stem cell transplantation.

Standard of care procedures or examinations for your child’s condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security). If you need more details or if you are not affiliated with a mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security), please contact the trial staff.

Will my child benefit from the sub-study? 

Blinatumomab may or may not be beneficial in treating your child’s ALL or relieving the symptoms. Blinatumomab may not be more effective than the current standard of care treatment (no additional treatment after stem cell transplantation. Even if it is beneficial to your child, a potential return or worsening of symptoms, illness or disease is still possible.

The information obtained during a trial may contribute to a better understanding of the use of the investigational medicinal product Blinatumomab and may improve care and outcomes of your child and future patients with ALL undergoing a stem cell transplantation.

What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?

1.  What are the possible side effects

Participation in a trial involves some risk. Participation in this sub-study may involve risks related to the study drug (blinatumomab). Even if previous studies have shown that Blinatumomab was normally well tolerated, you may still experience the following side effects. Side effects may vary from person to person. This document includes both common side effects and rare but serious side effects known to be associated with blinatumomab. We recommend discussing any doubts with the study physician, including possible side effects not listed here. Many side effects resolve shortly after drug discontinuation or modification. However, some side effects may be serious, long-lasting or permanent and, in rare cases, could be life-threatening.

Side effects of blinatumomab

In patients treated for lymphoid malignancies, outside the setting of HSCT,
very common side effects (occurring in more than 1 in 10 people) include:

• Infections (viral or bacterial)

• Low white blood cell count (neutropenia), which may increase the risk of
infection

• Low platelet count (thrombocytopenia), which may lead to easy bruising or
bleeding

• Febrile neutropenia (fever and very low neutrophils (type white blood cells) in
blood)

• Cytokine release syndrome (inflammatory response with release of cytokines in
the blood stream)

• Insomnia, headache, tremors

• Tachycardia (fast heart rate), hypertension (high blood pressure), hypotension
(low blood pressure)

• Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain

• Back pain, pain in extremity

• Hepatic enzyme increased, decreased immunoglobulins

In the vast majority of cases (not including infections), these side effects are reversible and can be managed by temporarily reducing or stopping blinatumomab. Your child will be monitored regularly through clinical examination, vital sign measurements and blood tests, and if clinical conditions do not improve, the drug dose may be reduced or even temporary stopped and later restarted at a lower dose.

Common side effects (occurring in 1 to 10 in 100 patients) included:

• Severe infections (including sepsis and pneumonia)

• Tumor lysis syndrome (rapid breakdown of a large number of cancer cells,
typically following chemotherapy which is not expected in this setting due to the
complete remission state required for enrolment)

• Confusional state, disorientation, encephalopathy (brain disease, damage, or
malfunction), paraesthesia (tingling), seizure,
dizziness, somnolence, cranial nerve disorder, ataxia

• Dyspnoea, wheezing

In rare cases (less than 1 in 100), capillary leak syndrome, acute respiratory failure, and pancreatitis have been reported. Symptoms may include weight gain, severe cough and dyspnea, high-grade fever, severe abdominal pain. Inform the study doctor immediately if your child shows any of these symptoms.

Clinical experience of blinatumomab in pediatric patients with B-ALL

In 2 recent clinical trials, children with high-risk B-ALL were treated with one or two courses of blinatumomab before HSCT (and the results compared to that of standard chemotherapy, since these were randomized controlled trials):

• All patients experienced at least one adverse event (AE, side effect)

• Treatment-related AEs, were most commonly Cytokine release syndrome
(22%)and encephalopathy
(11%)

• In the 2 studies, 57% and 76% experienced a severe AE (grade ≥3):
Thrombocytopenia (18.5%), stomatitis (18.5%), neutropenia (16.7%), anemia
(14.8%), fever 5.6%

• Serious AEs occurred in 24.1% of patients

• Only 3.7% of patients had an AE resulting into discontinuation of blinatumomab

In these studies, treatment with blinatumomab was associated with improved outcome (including survival after HSCT) and less side effects as compared to standard chemotherapy.

Therefore, it is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems of your child immediately to the investigator, regardless of whether or not you think it has to do with the sub-study, and even when it is already described in this document. If your child needs to use other medication, discuss this with the investigator beforehand. If, for any reason, your child consults another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that your child is taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving her / him the correct treatment if needed.

What If something goes wrong within the trial?

Even if there is no fault, the sponsor is liable for harm caused to your child whether directly or indirectly related to your participation in the trial. The sponsor has taken an appropriate insurance (a so called “NO FAULT INSURANCE*”) for this liability. A copy of the insurance certificate can be obtained from the investigator or trial staff.

If you (or in the event of death, your rightful claimants) seek compensation for a harm to your child’s health as a direct or indirect result of participating in the trial, you must inform your child’s investigator or trial staff promptly.

If the investigator believes that a link between the new or worsened health problem(s) and the trial is possible, he/she will inform the trial sponsor. The sponsor will then immediately initiate the declaration procedure to its insurance company. If the company considers it necessary, it will appoint an expert to assess whether there is a link between your child’s reported health problem(s) and the trial. The insurance does not cover the natural progression of your child’s disease or the known side effects of the treatment your child would have received without taking part to the trial (that is your child’s standard treatment).

Whenever you/your child feel it is appropriate or if you or your child’s rightful claimants disagree either with the investigator or with the expert appointed by the insurance company, you may contact the insurance company or proceedings may be brought against the insurance company. You will find the contact details on the front page of this form.

What if other treatment options or new information become available during the course of the trial?

During the course of the trial, important new information might become available, possibly affecting your decision for your child to (further) participate. For example, other treatments for your child’s disease or important new information may become available. It is the duty of the investigator to discuss this new information with you to give the opportunity to re-consider your child’s participation in the trial.

If you decide for your child to stop taking part in the trial or if your child is no longer able to participate, your child’s investigator will see to it that your child continues to receive the best possible medical care.

Can my child’s participation in the trial end prematurely?

As explained in detail below, your child’s trial participation may end prematurely when

- you decide for your child to withdraw your consent or,

- the investigator decides to end your child’s trial participation, or

- other entities interrupt or end the trial.

In any case, if your child’s trial participation ends prematurely, the investigator will discuss your child’s future medical care with you. Data collected before you terminate your child’s participation will continue to be used as described in this form. This is to avoid skewing / biasing results of the trial (as described in chapter I. § 12.4.).

Depending on your child’s situation, the investigator will discuss with you whether follow-up visits or procedures are needed.

1.  You decide to withdraw your consent

You are entitled to withdraw your consent for your child for any reason, at any time, without having to justify your decision. However, for your child’s safety, you should inform the investigator of your decision. Although it is not mandatory, it may be useful for the investigator and for the sponsor to know the reason of this decision (for example side effects, frequency of clinical visits,…).

If you withdraw your consent for your child to participate, please discuss with the investigator to evaluate the practical modalities of the withdrawal (in light of your child’s situation). No new data will be collected and sent to the sponsor.

2.  The investigator decides to end your child’s trial participation

The investigator may end your child’s trial participation because

- he/she thinks it is better for your child

- he/she determines that your child is not following the instructions given to participants, or

- any other reason that will be explained,

- the entire study should be discontinued for all participants (see next paragraph).

If that happens, the trial staff will explain why and ensure appropriate follow-up.

1.  Other entities may interrupt or end the trial

The sponsor and the competent Belgian health authorities may interrupt or end the trial because

- the information gathered shows that the trial is not effective (does not deliver a sufficient level of improvement in the health of the trial participants),

- there are more (serious) side effects than anticipated, or

- any other reason that will be duly motivated by such party.

Which treatment will my child get after my child’s participation in the trial? 

If your child stopped the study, the investigator will assess your child’s health. If necessary, he/she will prescribe your child the best standard treatment available.

Will my child’s participation in the trial involve extra costs for me?

As described in section 4, all examinations are standard of care. Blinatumomab will be provided free of charge and the associated hospitalisations to start the blinatumomab treatment will not be charged to you. The standard procedures, examinations and other visits for your child’s condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security).

The study does not involve any additional costs. You/your child will not receive any financial compensation for participating in this trial.

Which data are collected about my child during the trial and what will happen with them?

1.  Which data are collected and processed during the trial?

The collected and processed personal data concern information about your child’s health and medical condition. This includes your child’s medical history, some of your child’s background information (for example your child’s age and sex) and the results of examinations required by the trial.

Ethnic origin data may be collected to ensure that research data are processed in the broadest possible population.

2.  How will the investigator treat my child’s personal data?

The investigator (and the trial staff) is bound by professional secrecy or by a confidentiality agreement about the data collected.

This means that he/she will never reveal your child’s identity, including in a scientific publication or a lecture and that he/she will encode your child’s data (Ref. [1]) (that is by replacing your child’s identity by an identification code in the trial) before sending them to the sponsor. In other words, this code will not contain any personal identifier or combination of identifiers such as name, first name, initials, full or partial date of birth, file number, etc.

Therefore, the investigator and the trial staff under the responsibility of the investigator, will be the only ones able to establish a link between your child’s identity and the data transmitted during the trial, with the exceptions listed under section 12.5.

The data transmitted to the sponsor will not allow the sponsor to identify your child.

3.  How will my child’s data be handled?

Your child’s trial data will be processed in accordance with the current European and Belgian data protection legislation.

The reason that your child’s personal data may be processed for the purposes of the study is that it is processed for the conduct of scientific research and for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

4.  Do I have access to my child’s data collected and processed during the trial and can I rectify them?
