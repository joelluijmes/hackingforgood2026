 CHAPTER I – DESCRIPTION OF THE TRIAL AND YOUR RIGHTS WHEN PARTICIPATING

Why is this sub-study being conducted?

This sub-study is part of the FORUM2 platform trial, which aims to optimize the way stem cell transplantation is performed for children and young people with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

For patients with a diagnosis of an ALL, an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT) is one of the recommended treatments. This means healthy stem cells from a donor are received. This approach is part of routine care for this kind of ALL and has been shown to improve long-term survival.

It is possible that stem cells are received from a mismatched related donor (haplo-HSCT), this means from someone who is related, but does not completely match.

For this type of transplant, T cell depletion is required to reduce the risk of Graft-versus-Host Disease (GvHD), a complication after a stem cell transplant where the donated "graft" (the new tissue) attacks the patient's "host" body as if it were foreign. T cells, or T lymphocytes, are a type of white blood cells that play a critical role in the immune system. T cells from a donor graft can cause graft-versus-host disease by attacking the recipient's healthy tissues, but they are also crucial for fighting the recipient's cancer cells.

Preventing this attack is a major challenge in allogeneic stem cell transplantation. Research is ongoing to find ways to selectively eliminate the T cells that cause GVHD while preserving those that fight cancer.

The two most used methods for T-cell depletion are:

- the administration of Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy)

- removal of a specific subtype of T lymphocyte (αβ T-cells) and B lymphocyte from the donor cells before the transplant.

Retrospective studies have shown that both approaches are effective and safe to treat childhood ALL. To date, there are no comparative studies to clarify which is the best strategy of the aforementioned two. In the Belgian hospitals, the method with the administration of a medication Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy) to remove T-cells from the donor cells is used in the regular care.

Sub-study S1 focuses specifically on the question:

Are there any differences in survival or severe complications rates between patients who received a haplo-HSCT based on the administration of cyclophosphamide after the transplant, or on the depletion of αβ/CD19+ cells from the donor cells before the transplant?

This sub-study is expected to take place in approximately 133 centres in 26 countries and 150 patients will be included.

Why am I being asked to take part?

You have been diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL). Although treatment so far has led to complete remission (meaning that no leukaemia cells are currently detectable), there remains a significant risk that the leukaemia could come back based on the characteristics of your disease.

To give you the best chance of a long-term cure, you will receive an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT), in which you will receive healthy stem cells from a suitable donor. You will receive stem cells from a mismatched related donor, this means someone who is related to you, but does not completely match (haplo-HSCT). In this type of transplant, you will need T cell depletion (removal of T-cells) to reduce the risk of Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD).

With this sub-study we want to investigate if there are some differences in terms of outcomes between the two most used T-cell depletion methods.

The investigator or trial staff will discuss with you the requirements to be allowed to enter the trial.

Do I have to take part in a trial?

Your participation in a trial is voluntary and must remain free of any coercion. This means that you have the right not to take part in the trial or to withdraw at any time without giving a reason, even if you previously agreed to take part. Your decision will not affect your relationship with the investigator or your treating physician nor will it affect the quality of your future medical care.

If other treatments are available for HSCT for ALL, the investigator or his/her delegate will discuss these treatments with you.

If you agree to participate, your investigator will inform your treating physicians. You then cannot participate in another interventional clinical trial without informing the investigator or trial staff. You also need to communicate all relevant information related to the state of your health, other medication taken or the symptoms experienced.

What will happen during the sub-study? 

If you decide to participate in the sub-study, you will receive your stem cell transplantation with a mismatched related donor (haplo-HSCT) and the method of T-cell depletion as usual. At the Belgian hospitals, the method to remove T-cells from the donor cells (T-cell depletion) will be the administration of Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy) as usual. Your doctor will explain to you in detail the standard of care treatment. For this sub-study, only data from your standard of care treatment will be collected (lab results of standard of care examinations, details of the standard of care treatment and how you are feeling).

After the stem cell transplantation you will be followed up frequently according to the regular care in your hospital. This will include a general physical examination, routine blood analyis, microbiological analysis, radiological exams, bone marrow examination, lumbar puncture,urine analysis,...). The duration of the follow-up will take until 5 years after transplantation.

All examination and hospital visits will be done according to the standard of care. No study specific examinations or visits will be done. The study will collect data of all the routine analysis done and how you feel. The sub-study will take 5 years and will collect data until then.

Standard of care procedures or examinations for your condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security). If you need more details or if you are not affiliated with a mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security), please contact the trial staff.

Will I benefit from the sub-study? 

You will not have an immediate benefit, but your participation to the study will help the clinicians to discriminate whether there are differences in terms of outcome and side effects between the two standard of care methods of T- cell depletion.

The information obtained during a trial may contribute to improve care and outcomes of yourself and future patients with ALL undergoing a stem cell transplantation.

What are the possible risks and discomforts of taking part?

1.  What are the possible side effects?

In this sub-study, you will receive the standard of care T-cell depletion method and stem cell transplantation. Clinical data from your standard of care treatment will be collected. Therefore, participation does not carry any direct medical risk compared to what is part of your routine care.

The risks you may face by participating in this sub-study are the general risks associated with an allogeneic haploidentical donor transplant procedure including a T-cell depletion procedure. Your treating physician will explain in detail which side effects you can experience during your standard of care treatment.

All medicinal products, even during standard of care treatment, can have side effects.

Therefore, it is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to the investigator. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with the investigator beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

2.  What are the possible risks or discomforts of the examinations during the trial?

There are no trial specific examinations during this sub-study. Only examinations within the standard of care will be performed. There will be no additional risks or discomforts compared to the standard of care.

3.  Can I take other medicines during the trial?

Do not hesitate to ask your investigator for more explanation about the use of medicines or and food supplements (including aromatherapy, phytotherapy, homeopathy..).

4.  Will my participation to the trial have an impact on my daily activities?

We want to ensure that the trial runs smoothly. That is why we will make the following agreements with you:

• You will make sure you take all your drugs in the way needed

• During this trial, you won’t participate in another medical scientific trial,
unless sponsor approval was obtained prior to participation

• Please contact the investigator or research nurse in the following situations:

  o You will start to take other drugs. This includes homeopathic drugs,
  natural remedies, vitamins or over-the-counter drugs.

  o You need to be admitted to the hospital or receive treatment there.

  o You suddenly develop problems with your health.

  o You no longer want to take part in the trial.

  o Your phone number, address or e-mail address has changed.

What If something goes wrong within the trial?

Even if there is no fault, the sponsor is liable for harm caused to you whether directly or indirectly related to your participation in the trial. The sponsor has taken an appropriate insurance (a so called “No Fault insurance”) for this liability. A copy of the insurance certificate can be obtained from the investigator or trial staff.

If you (or in the event of death, your rightful claimants) seek compensation for a harm to your health as a direct or indirect result of participating in the trial, you must inform your investigator or trial staff promptly.

If the investigator believes that a link between the new or worsened health problem(s) and the trial is possible, he/she will inform the trial sponsor. The sponsor will then immediately initiate the declaration procedure to its insurance company. If the company considers it necessary, it will appoint an expert to assess whether there is a link between your reported health problem(s) and the trial. The insurance does not cover the natural progression of your disease/condition or the known side effects of the treatment you would have received without taking part to the trial (that is your standard treatment).

Whenever you feel it is appropriate or if you or your rightful claimants disagree either with the investigator or with the expert appointed by the insurance company, you may contact the insurance company or proceedings may be brought against the insurance company. You will find the contact details on the front page of this form.

What if other treatment options or new information become available during the course of the trial?

During the course of the trial, important new information might become available, possibly affecting your decision to (further) participate. For example, other treatments or important new information may become available. It is the duty of the investigator to discuss this new information with you and to give you the opportunity to re-consider your participation in the trial.

If you decide to stop taking part in the trial or if you are no longer able to participate, your investigator will see to it that you continue to receive the best possible medical care.

Can my participation in the trial end prematurely?

As explained in detail below, your trial participation may end prematurely when

- you decide to withdraw your consent or,

- the investigator decides to end your trial participation, or

- other entities interrupt or end the trial.

In any case, if your trial participation ends prematurely, the investigator will discuss your future medical care with you. Data collected before you terminate your participation will continue to be used as described in this form. This is to avoid skewing / biasing results of the trial (as described in chapter I. § 12.4.).

Depending on your situation, the investigator will discuss with you whether follow-up visits or procedures are needed.

1.  You decide to withdraw your consent

You are entitled to withdraw your consent for any reason, at any time, without having to justify your decision. However, for your safety, you should inform the investigator of your decision. Although it is not mandatory, it may be useful for the investigator and for the sponsor to know the reason of this decision (for example side effects, frequency of clinical visits,…).

If you withdraw your consent, please discuss with the investigator to evaluate the practical modalities of the withdrawal (in light of your situation). No new data will be collected and sent to the sponsor.

2.  The investigator decides to end your trial participation

The investigator may end your trial participation because

- he/she thinks it is better for you

- he/she determines that you are not following the instructions given to participants, or

- any other reason that will be explained,

- the entire study should be discontinued for all participants (see next paragraph).

If that happens, the trial staff will explain why and ensure appropriate follow-up.

1.  Other entities may interrupt or end the trial

The sponsor and the competent Belgian health authorities may interrupt or end the trial because

- the information gathered shows that the sub-study is not effective (does not deliver a sufficient level of improvement in the health of the trial participants),

- there are more (serious) side effects than anticipated, or

- any other reason that will be duly motivated by such party.

Which treatment will I get after my participation in the trial? 

If you stop the study, the investigator will assess your health. If necessary, he/she will prescribe you the best standard treatment available.

Will my participation in the trial involve extra costs for me?

As described in section 4, all visits, examinations and the treatment are standard of care. The standard procedures or examinations for your condition (i.e. standard of care) will be charged to you or reimbursed by your mutual insurance fund (Belgian social security).

The study does not involve any additional costs. You will not receive any financial compensation for participating in this trial.

Which data are collected about me during the trial and what will happen with them?

1.  Which data are collected and processed during the trial?

The collected and processed personal data concern information about your health and medical condition. This includes your medical history, some of your background information (for example your age, sex, and ethnic origin) and the results of examinations performed within the standard of care.

Ethnic origin data maybe collected to ensure that research data are processed in the broadest possible population.

2.  How will the investigator treat my personal data?

The investigator (and the trial staff) is bound by professional secrecy or by a confidentiality agreement about the data collected.

This means that he/she will never reveal your identity, including in a scientific publication or a lecture and that he/she will encode your data (Ref. [1]) (that is by replacing your identity by an identification code in the trial) before sending them to the sponsor. In other words, this code will not contain any personal identifier or combination of identifiers such as name, first name, initials, full or partial date of birth, file number, etc.

Therefore, the investigator and the trial staff under the responsibility of the investigator, will be the only ones able to establish a link between your identity and the data transmitted during the trial, with the exceptions listed under section 12.5.

The data transmitted to the sponsor will not allow the sponsor to identify you.

3.  How will my data be handled?

Your trial data will be processed in accordance with the current European and Belgian data protection legislation.

The reason that your personal data may be processed for the purposes of the study is that it is processed for the conduct of scientific research and for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

4.  Do I have access to my data collected and processed during the trial and can I rectify them?

You are entitled to ask the investigator what data are being collected about you and how those data will be used in connection with the trial.

You have the right

- to inspect and access these data

- to receive the personal data that are collected

- to ask for correction if they are incorrect,

- to restrict the processing of your data.

- to withdraw your consent for the processing of personal data. However personal data collected before withdrawal will be kept to avoid skewing of results in the trial.

It is not possible to have all your data erased to avoid skewing of results in the trial.

1.  Who, other than the Investigator and his trial staff, has access to my personal data?

To verify the quality of the trial, it is possible that your personal uncoded data or information in your medical records relevant for the trial, will be examined by people outside the trial staff but under the responsibility of the investigator. These persons must be subject to professional secrecy or a confidentiality agreement. The following might be considered:

- the personnel designated by the sponsor of the trial (monitors and auditors), and people or organisations providing services for or collaborating with the sponsor. They will however never transfer your name and contact details to the sponsor.

- inspectors of competent health authorities worldwide

- an independent audit group

- people designated by the Ethics Committee

For the needs of the trial, the encoded trial data may be sent to other EU and non-EU countries and may be reviewed by

- personnel (other than the inspectors) of competent health authorities of Belgium (Federal agency for medicines and health products, FAMHP) and other EU and non-EU countries,

- the evaluating Belgian Ethics Committee(s),

- external researchers,

- the sponsor of the trial, personnel designated by the sponsor, and people or organisations providing services for or collaborating with the sponsor, and/or

- group companies of the sponsor in Belgium, and in other EU and non-EU countries.

The European regulation and the Belgian legislation on data protection have requirements for transferring data to non-EU countries. The sponsor must ensure equivalent guarantees regarding personal data protection standards before transferring the encoded data to non-EU countries. If for this purpose, there is a data protection agreement, a copy of this agreement may be obtained via the investigator. You can always contact your investigator to obtain more information about any such transfers.

1.  What will happen to the results of the trial?

After trial closure, a description and the results of this clinical trial will be published in specialised medical journals. A copy of the scientific publication or a summary for laypersons can be obtained from the investigator or the trial staff.

A description of the trial will also be available on https://euclinicaltrials.eu/search-for-clinical-trials/?lang=en and/or https://www.Clinicaltrials.gov. You can search these websites at any time using the information on the front page of the informed consent form. Within 1 year after the study's completion, the website will contain a summary of the results in accordance with the European Commission's guidelines.

These websites or publications will not include information that can identify you.

2.  Will my data be used for other purposes than for the trial in which I take part?

The results of the trial will be used to answer the scientific questions of the trial. In addition, the sponsor would like to use your data obtained from this trial, in connection with other research and development activities (and the associated scientific publications). These activities can only concern research about the same disease or medication.

Any additional research outside of the trial and therefore not described in the above points, must be approved by a recognized Ethics Committee.

You already agreed or disagreed , to the use of your trial data for other purposes in the main ICF of the FORUM2 study at the beginning.

3.  How long will my data be kept?

After the end of the trial your encoded data will be retained for at least 25 years in accordance with the European clinical trial regulation to ensure the validity of the research. This will also be the case if you stopped trial participation prematurely.

 Which biological samples are collected from me during the trial and what will happen with them? 

Biological samples are samples of human body material. In this sub-study, no trial specific biological samples will be collected.

Who has reviewed and approved the trial documents?

The documents of the trial have been reviewed by

- The Belgian competent health authorities (FAMHP) or, if applicable, by the competent national health authorities of other EU members states and

- An independent Belgian Ethics Committee

It is the task of the competent health authorities and the Ethics Committees to protect people who take part in a trial. The health authorities will ensure that the trial is conducted in accordance with the applicable legislation.

You should not under any circumstances take their approval as an incentive to take part in the trial.
