 Information form for Participants aged 12-17 years

Forum2 – Sub-Study S1

Comparing between In-Vivo PTCy and Ex-Vivo αβ T-Cell Depletion in Mismatched Donor Transplantation for Pediatric and Young Adult ALL: A Prospective Stratified Cohort Study

EU number: 2025-522052-13-00

Official title of the trial: FORUM2 – Framework for Optimizing, Refining, and Unifying Management of HSCT in Pediatric ALL

Dear,

You are invited to take part in a part of the FORUM 2 study. This S1 sub-study is a clinical study that is done to find out what the effect of a treatment will be on your disease. We ask you to participate because you have been diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and will need a hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT).

This sub-study, called Sub-Study 1 (S1), is part of the larger international FORUM2 study, which aims to improve the way stem cell transplantation is performed for children and young people with ALL.

Before you decide if you wish to participate, we would like you to understand why the research is being done and what it would involve for you. This document will inform you clearly and in detail about the nature and purposes of this sub-study and about what taking part will mean for you, including your rights and responsibilities.

A member of the team at your local hospital involved in this study will go through this information sheet with you and answer any question that you may have. Please ask if there is anything that is not clear.

If you agree to take part, you will be asked to sign an assent form. Your parents will be asked to sign a consent form as well. You are free to withdraw at any time, without giving a reason. This will not affect the standard of care you will receive.

You will also be given a copy of this information.

1 What is the purpose of the sub-study? 

You have been diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL). Although treatment so far has led to complete remission (meaning that no leukaemia cells are currently detectable), there remains a significant risk that the leukaemia could come back.

To give you the best chance of a long-term cure, an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT) is recommended, in which you will receive healthy stem cells from someone else (a suitable donor).

You are going to have an allogeneic stem cell transplantation from a mismatched related donor (haplo-HSCT). This means that you will get cells from someone who is related to you, but does not completely match.

In this type of transplant, some adaptations of the cells of the donor are needed (cells called T-cells need to be removed) to reduce the risk of Graft-versus-Host Disease (GvHD). GVHD is a disease in which the donor cells attack the recipient's healthy tissues.

In the standard of care treatment, there are two frequently used methods to remove T-cells from the cells for transplantation:

- One method is based on the administration of a medication Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy)

- The other method is based on the removal of specific cells from the donor cells before transplant (αβ T-Cell depl).

Studies have already shown that both methods are effective and safe but more data are needed. In the Belgian hospitals, the method with the administration of Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy) is used in the regular care.

In this sub-study we want to investigate in a large population if there are some differences in results and side effects between the two different methods to remove T-cells from the donor cells for transplantation.

2 WHAT WILL HAPPEN IF YOU TAKE PART?

If you decide to participate in the sub-study, you will receive your stem cell transplantation with a mismatched related donor (haplo-HSCT). At the Belgian hospitals, the method to remove T-cells from the donor cells (T-cell depletion) will be the administration of Cyclophosphamide after transplant (PTCy) as usual. Your doctor will explain in detail what will happen to you in the regular care.

After the stem cell transplantation you will be followed up frequently according to the regular care in your hospital. This will include a general physical examination, routine blood analyis, microbiological analysis, radiological exams, bone marrow examination, lumbar puncture,urine analysis,...).

The duration of the follow-up will take until 5 years after transplantation.

All examinations and hospital visits will be done according to the normal care. No study specific examinations or visits will be done. The study will collect data of all the routine analysis done and how you feel.

This sub-study will take 5 years and will collect data until then.

3 HOW many participants will be included in this sub-study?

This sub-study is expected to take place in approximately 133 centres in 26 countries and 150 patients will be included.

4 WHAT IS THE DURATION OF THIS sub-STUDY?

Overall, your participation in this sub-study will take until 5 years after transplantation.

5 What is expected from you?

For the study to run smoothly, it is important that you fully cooperate with the doctor and that you follow his/her instructions carefully.

If you decide to take part in this trial, you will be asked to:

- Keep all scheduled appointments

- Tell the trial doctor about any medications you take, even if it is medicine you buy without a prescription or is a natural or herbal remedy

- Tell the trial doctor if you plan to have a vaccination. Some vaccines may not be suitable during chemotherapy.

- Tell the doctor about any side effect, injury, symptom, or complaint you experience, including any unplanned hospital admissions

- Not to participate in another clinical trial without informing the investigator or trial staff

- Make sure that, if you are sexually active, you use a method of highly effective birth control both during treatment and for one year afterwards.

6 WHAT ARE THE POSSIBLE SIDE EFFECTS OF THE TREATMENT?

In this sub-study, you will receive the standard of care T-cell depletion method and the standard of care stem cell transplantation. Clinical data from your standard of care treatment will be collected. Therefore, participation does not carry any direct medical risk compared to what is part of your routine care.

It is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to your doctor, regardless of whether or not you think it has to do with the sub-study, and even when it is already described in this document. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with your doctor beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

7 WHAT ARE YOUR RIGHTS and what happens if you do not participate in this sub-study?

Your decision to participate in this sub-study is entirely voluntary. If for any reason and at any time you decide not to participate, you can tell your doctor. Your doctor and the nurses won't mind if you don't want to participate. The doctor will then discuss with you how your disease can be further treated. If you decide not to participate anymore, you will be asked to allow us to collect follow-up data at your normal hospital appointments. However, you are also free to withdraw consent for all data collection if you wish. Any information we have already collected on you may still be used.

If, during the course of the sub-study, your doctor feels that this sub-study is not the best treatment, the doctor may remove you from the sub-study and suggest a possibly better treatment.

Also, in rare cases, the study may be stopped early if there is new evidence that one treatment is clearly superior or inferior, or if unexpected safety issues arise. You will be informed immediately should this happen.

The doctor conducting this study will inform your general practitioner and/or pediatrician about your participation in this study.

This study was evaluated by an Ethics Committee in Belgium. The study is thus carried out in accordance with national laws. Your data will be treated confidentially and given a code (pseudonymized) in order to respect your privacy.

This means that other people cannot directly see that it belongs to you. Only the investigator will know which code belongs to which person. Other people will only see the code, so they won’t know your name. This coded data can then be shared with other researchers for future studies in the context of the same or similar disease or treatment. These studies may take place in Belgium or abroad. Such new studies must always first be approved by the ethics committee. You already agreed or disagreed to the use of your data for future studies in the assent form of the general part of the FORUM2 study. If you do not want your data to be used for future research anymore, you can let your parents or the doctors know.

The sponsor will also ensure that insurance (protection against unexpected costs) is taken out to protect and/or treat you in the event of damage and/or injury resulting from your participation in this study.

You can ask questions about the study at any time.
