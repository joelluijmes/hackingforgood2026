1. What is the FORUM2 S1 sub-study?

You have an illness in your blood called leukemia.

To help you get better, the doctors want to give you healthy cells from another person,. These cells help your body to make new, healthy blood cells.

The person giving you the cells is a family member but the cells are not complete the same as yours. This means that the cells you will receive need to be adapted. There are two ways to do that

1.  [A blue circle with a drop of liquid AI-generated content may be incorrect.]Administration of some medicines after you received the cells.

2.  Some bad cells will be removed in the lab before you get the cells.

The doctors want to compare and check if there any differences between the two methods. Studies performed so far have shown that both methods works safely in children with your disease but more studies are needed to be sure there are no differences between the 2 methods. In Belgium, the first method is used in all hospitals within the regular care. The doctors will further explain this to you and your parents in detail.

2.  What will happen if I participate in the sub-study?

If you decide to participate in this sub-study, you will receive your cells from family as usual . You will receive medicines after you received the cells (see method 1 above). The same method will be used if you don’t participate in the study. Your doctor will explain in detail what will happen to you in the regular care.

There are no procedures specifically for this part of the study and only data will be collected on how you feel. You may develop itching, skin rash, diarrhea or vomiting. These symptoms may be related to the effect of the transplant on your body. Therefore, it is important to tell your parents and the doctor if you’re not feeling well. The doctors will help you to get better.

At regular intervals and until 5 years after the stem cell transplantion, you will undergo examinations and check-ups during which the doctor will ask you how you feel. You will have the same examinations and check-ups if you don’t participate in this part of the study.

3.  Who knows that I am participating in this sub-study?

The only people who know you are participating are the doctors, the researchers and your primary care physician. All information about you is shared only with people from the study. 4.  Has anyone checked that this sub-study is okay?

Before a study is conducted, it is checked by a group of people who look very carefully at the study and decide if everything is okay. We call this an Ethics Committee. Only when this Committee gives approval may the study begin.
