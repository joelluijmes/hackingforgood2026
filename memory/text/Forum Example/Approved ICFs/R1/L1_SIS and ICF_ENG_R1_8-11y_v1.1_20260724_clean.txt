Hello,

You are invited to take part in a part of the FORUM 2 clinical study. This R1 sub-study is a clinical study that is done to find out what the effect of a treatment will be on your disease.

You and your parents or guardian can decide together if you want to participate in this study. You can decide to stop taking part in the research at any time. If you do not understand something or want to know more about the study, you can always ask your doctor or other persons of your choice.

1.  What is the FORUM2 R1 sub-study?

You have a disease in your blood called leukemia. To help you get better, the doctors want to give you healthy cells from another person, a so called stem cell transplantation. To make some space in your body for the new healthy cells, the sick cells must be removed with x-rays (called radiation) and strong medicines.

In this sub-study, we want to see whether a smaller amount of x-rays can help you stay healthy for a long time.

Since the x-rays are also harmful to your body, we hope that less x-rays are better for you and that you will stay healthy for a long time even with less x-rays.

2.  What will happen if I participate in the FORUM2 R1 SUB-study?

First of all, you will be assigned to a group:

Group 1 ‘less radiation than usual during treatment’

or

Group 2 ‘as much radiation as usual during treatment’

If you are in group 1, you will receive less x-rays than you would normally receive during radiation therapy.

If you are you in group 2, you will receive as much x-rays as you would normally receive during radiation therapy.

A computer will decide which group you will be in. Neither you, nor your parents, nor your doctor can choose the treatment. At the end of this study, the results of the two groups will be compared.

[Afbeelding met schermopname, tekenfilm, ontwerp Door AI gegenereerde inhoud is mogelijk onjuist.]

You will be followed-up very closely by your doctor with examinations and blood tests. These tests will also be done if you don’t participate in this study. You will regularly visit your doctor. Information about your treatment, your exams and how you feel will be collected.

Your information we collect for the study will receive a code without mentioning your name. The data we collect about you may also be used for later research on your disease. No extra procedures are needed for this sub-study.

[]

Data will be collected on how you feel until 5 years after transplantation, which is the normal time doctors keep checking on patients after a stem cell transplantation. The doctors will always check how you’re doing, just like they do now. If you ever feel something is wrong, like a headache, tummy ache, or feel sick, tell your parents right away.

The doctors will take care of you and help you feel better.

3.  Who knows that I am participating in this study?

The only people who know you are participating are the doctors, the researchers and nurses looking after you, and your family doctor. All information about you is shared only with people from the study.

4.  Has anyone checked that this study is okay?

Before a study is allowed to happen, it has to be checked by a group of people who look very carefully at the study and decide if everything is okay. We call this an Ethics Committee. Only when this Committee gives an approval the study can start.

[]

5.  Do I have to participate in the SUB-study?

It is important to know that you are not obliged to participate. You and your parents can decide this.

If you do not want to participate, this will have no effect on how the doctors will follow you up and you will receive your treatment as usual. Even if you have already given your permission, you can decide at any time not to participate anymore without having to say why. We do however ask you to inform us as soon as possible.

If you want to participate, please tell your parents and your doctor. In that case, we ask you to enter your name, first name and date below.
