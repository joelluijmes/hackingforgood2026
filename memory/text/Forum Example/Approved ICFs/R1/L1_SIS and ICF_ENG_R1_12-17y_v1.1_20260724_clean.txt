Dear,

You are invited to take part in a part of the FORUM 2 study. This R1 sub-study is a clinical study that is done to find out what the effect of a treatment will be on your disease. We ask you to participate because you have been diagnosed with acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL).

Before you decide if you wish to participate, we would like you to understand why the research is being done and what it would involve for you. This document will inform you clearly and in detail about the nature and purposes of the trial, and about what taking part will mean for you, including your rights and responsibilities. A member of the team at your local hospital involved in this study will go through this information sheet with you and answer any questions that you may have. Please ask if there is anything that is not clear.

If you agree to take part, you will be asked to sign an assent form. Your parents will be asked to sign a consent form as well. You are free to withdraw at any time, without giving a reason. This will not affect the standard of care you will receive.

You will also be given a copy of this information.

What is the purpose of the SUB-study?

Although treatment so far has led to complete remission (meaning that no leukaemia cells are currently detectable, there remains a high risk that the leukaemia could come back. To give you the best chance of a long-term cure, an allogeneic stem cell transplant (HSCT) is recommended, in which you will receive healthy stem cells from a suitable donor. This approach is part of normal care f and we know this improves the long-term survival (living longer without the disease coming back).

Before the transplant, a so-called conditioning treatment (a kind of preparation treatment) will been given. This prepares the body by eliminating remaining leukaemia cells and suppressing the immune system (your body’s defence system that recognises and fights off viruses, bacteria and other pathogens to keep you healthy), to allow donor cells to grow.

Conditioning usually involves a combination of total body irradiation (TBI) and chemotherapy.

The current standard approach is 12 Gray (Gy) irradiation in combination with a chemotherapy called Etoposide (12 Gy TBI plus Etoposide), which has shown to have very good results in preventing relapse.

However, radiation is also associated with an increased risk of acute side effects (organ damage, nausea, mucositis, skin toxicity, and infections) and long-term side effects such as growth disorders, hormonal imbalances, infertility and late organ damage.

This part of the clinical trial is being conducted to find out whether a lower radiation dose of 8 Gy (also combined with Etoposide) can work just as well in preventing relapse while causing fewer long-term side effects. Several smaller studies have shown hopeful results while keeping the treatment effective.

This trial, called Sub-study R1, is part of the larger international FORUM2 study, which aims to improve the way stem cell transplantation is performed for children and young people with ALL.

Sub-study R1 focuses specifically on the question: Is 8 Gy TBI as effective as 12 Gy TBI in preventing leukaemia relapse, but with fewer harmful effects?

WHAT WILL HAPPEN IF YOU TAKE PART?

If you chose to participate in this sub-study, you will receive either the standard dose (12 Gy) or the reduced dose (8 Gy) of TBI, depending on the random assignment. A computer will assign you randomly to the group of the standard dose (12Gy) or the reduced dose (8Gy).

All other aspects of the transplantation — including the chemotherapy drug (Etoposide), the transplantation itself, supportive care during the hospital stay, and follow-up after discharge — will be identical to the treatment you would receive outside the trial.

Both groups will receive a preparation treatment (conditioning) before the transplantation, which includes chemotherapy called Etoposide (VP16).

This drug will be given intravenously (in a blood vein) like usual.

The difference between the two groups is the dose of total body irradiation (TBI) applied along with etoposide:

- One group will receive the standard dose of 12 Gray (Gy) TBI, which is the current treatment used in clinical practice.

- The other group will receive a reduced dose of 8 Gy, being tested in this trial.

TBI is given through external radiation in multiple times(fractions) over 2 to 3 days. , The aim is to compare how well the two different TBI doses work in preventing relapse and to examine whether the lower dose can reduce side effects without compromising effectiveness.

Importantly, participation in this sub-study will not result in any additional examinations, procedures, or follow-up visits beyond a standard of care stem cell transplantation. T You will be monitored in the same way as any other patient undergoing a stem cell transplantation outside this trial.

Possible benefits of participating include the chance that you will receive a lower radiation dose that may reduce the risk of long-term complications — assuming the 8 Gy dose proves to be as effective. However, this cannot be guaranteed, and there may be no direct benefit for you. The broader benefit is contributing to better and safer treatment for future patients.

If you decide not to take part, you will receive stem cell transplantation using the current standard of care conditioning regime (12 Gy). Y

Once the treatment is complete, there will be a standard follow-up period until 5 years after the transplantation.

HOW many participants will be included in this sub-study?

This sub-study is expected to enroll around 1,000 children and adolescents aged 2 to 25 years, in multiple centres across Europe and beyond, including about 4-5 participants each year in Belgium. All participating centres are experienced transplant centres following strict international protocols.

WHAT IS THE DURATION OF THIS sub-STUDY?

Clinical data will be collected on how you feel and which side effects you experience until 5 years after transplantation.

What is expected from you?

For the study to run smoothly, it is important that you fully cooperate with the doctor and that you follow his/her instructions carefully.

If you decide to take part in this trial, you will be asked to:

- Keep all scheduled appointments

- Tell the trial doctor about any medications you take, even if it is medicine you buy without a prescription or is a natural or herbal remedy

- Tell the trial doctor if you plan to have a vaccination. Some vaccines may not be suitable during chemotherapy.

- Tell the doctor about any side effect, injury, symptom, or complaint you experience, including any unplanned hospital admissions

- Not to participate in another interventional clinical trial without informing the investigator or trial staff

- Make sure that, if you are sexually active, you use a method of highly effective birth control both during treatment and for one year afterwards.

WHAT ARE THE POSSIBLE SIDE EFFECTS OF THE TREATMENT?

TBI is used to prepare the body to accept donor stem cells by suppressing the immune system and eliminating remaining leukemia cells. Radiation can also affecthealthy tissues, which may lead to complications.

- Short-term (acute) side effects: Fatigue, nausea, vomiting, decreased appetite, irritation of the mucous membranes, and skin redness or dryness.

- Long-term side effects: These may develop months to years after treatment and include:

  - Endocrine disorders such as hypothyroidism or growth hormone deficiency.

  - Delayed or impaired growth and physical development, particularly in younger children.

  - Infertility or reduced fertility due to damage to the reproductive organs.

  - Cataracts (clouding of the lens in the eye) that may require surgery.

  - Lung or kidney dysfunction, usually mild but sometimes persistent.

  - Secondary malignancies (new cancers) later in life — these are rare but serious.

The risk and severity of acute and long-term effects are generally higher with 12 Gy TBI (standard of care treatment) compared to 8 Gy. The goal of the sub-study is to determine whether the lower dose is equally effective while causing fewer of these complications. However, since 8 Gy has not been tested in a trial yet, the lower dose of irradiation might be associated with an increased risk of relapse. If you will not participate in the R1 sub-study, you will receive the higher dose of TBI (12Gy) with the same side effects as described above.

It is very important that you report any new or worsened health problems immediately to your doctor, regardless of whether or not you think it has to do with the sub-study, and even when it is already described in this document. If you need to use other medication, discuss this with your doctor beforehand. If, for any reason, you consult another treating physician during the trial you must inform him/her that you are taking part in a trial. This could be important in determining a diagnosis and giving you the correct treatment if needed.

Pregnancy

The conditioning regimen, particularly TBI, can damage reproductive organs and can cause significant harm to an unborn child.

If you are a women, you must not become pregnant during the trial and for at least 12 months after completing treatment. In addition, you must not have egg cells stored for your own use or donation after the start of the treatment and for at least 12 months after the last treatment. You must not breastfeed during treatment.

If you are a men, you must not cause a pregnancy or donate sperm during the trial and for at least 6 months after completing treatment.

Your doctor will speak to you about a reliable way to prevent a pregnancy.

WHAT ARE YOUR RIGHTS and what happens if you do not participate in this SUB-study?

Your decision to participate in this sub-study is entirely voluntary. If for any reason and at any time you decide not to participate, you can tell your doctor. Your doctor and the nurses won't mind if you don't want to participate. The doctor will then discuss with you how your disease can be further treated. If you decide not to participate anymore, you will be asked to allow us to collect follow-up data at your normal hospital appointments. However, you are also free to withdraw consent for all data collection if you wish. Any information we have already collected on you may still be used.

If, during the course of the sub-study, your doctor feels that this sub-study is not the best treatment, the doctor may remove you from the sub-study and suggest a possibly better treatment.

Also, in rare cases, the study may be stopped early if there is new evidence that one treatment is clearly superior or inferior, or if unexpected safety issues arise. You will be informed immediately should this happen.

The doctor conducting this study will inform your general practitioner and/or pediatrician about your participation in this study.

This study was evaluated by an Ethics Committee in Belgium. The study is thus carried out in accordance with national laws. Your data will be treated confidentially and given a code (pseudonymized) in order to respect your privacy.

This means that other people cannot directly see that it belongs to you. Only the investigator will know which code belongs to which person. Other people will only see the code, so they won’t know your name. This coded data can then be shared with other researchers for future studies in the context of the same or similar disease or treatment. These studies may take place in Belgium or abroad. Such new studies must always first be approved by the ethics committee. You already agreed or disagreed to the use of your data for future studies in the assent form of the general part of the FORUM2 study. If you do not want your data to be used for future research anymore, you can let your parents or the doctors know.

The sponsor will also ensure that insurance (protection against unexpected costs) is taken out to protect and/or treat you in the event of damage and/or injury resulting from your participation in this study.

You can ask questions about the study at any time.
