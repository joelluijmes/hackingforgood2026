Dear,

A clinical study is a research study that helps us to find new treatment options. You
and your parents or guardians may decide together if you want to participate in this
study. You don't have to decide right away, you get time to think about it and to ask
questions. This letter contains more information about the study and can help you
make a decision. It is important that you understand everything in this letter. Your
parents/guardians have received a similar information letter and we will also ask their
permission for you to participate in the study.


1   WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF THIS STUDY?

The purpose of this study is to learn more about the origin and behaviour of rare
tumours in the soft tissues in children and young adults. We want to use this to
improve the treatment of patients with these tumours in the future.

A tumour has been found in your soft tissues or there is a suspicion of a soft-tissue
tumour. Soft tissues are, for example, your muscles, connective tissue, fatty tissue or
the walls of blood vessels. These tumours can appear anywhere in the body. This
study covers different kinds of rare tumours in soft tissues. Little is known about the
origin of these tumours. We also do not know what causes these tumours to grow
and how they behave.



2   WHO IS ORGANIZING THE STUDY?

The study is coordinated at international level by the Princess Máxima Center
(hereinafter referred to as “the sponsor”). (Utrecht, Netherlands). The study will be
carried out by physicians in various hospitals in Europe and in several countries
outside Europe. In Belgium, the study will be carried out in 7 hospitals. The national
coordinating center will be Cliniques Universitaires Saint-Luc and Dr. de Ville will act
as national coordinating investigator.

3   WHAT DOES PARTICIPATION IN THE STUDY INCLUDES FOR YOU?

The surgeon will remove the entire tumour or a small piece of tumour tissue (biopsy)
from you under general anaesthesia (as standard of care procedure). This may
already have taken place. In that case we shall request a piece of your tumour
biopsy from the biobank, if it is stored there. A biobank is a facility where human
body material (such as blood, urine, tissue samples…) is stored together with
additional data related to this material. The tissue will be processed, placed under
the microscope and evaluated by a pathologist. This results in a standard diagnosis.
This biopsy or operation is standard, it would also be done if you don’t give consent
to take part in this study. Once the diagnosis is made, you will be treated in the
standard manner for the disease. The treating doctor will discuss the treatment plan
with you. If you agree to this study, we will perform additional test on the same
biopsy for study purpose.

Along with the use of your bodily material sample (biopsy + blood), we register the
data about the diagnosis, treatment and result of your treatment. We do this from the
start of participation in this study, and we continue registering how you are doing for
5 years afterwards.

The intention is to precise the diagnosis of soft-tissue tumours through international
re-evaluation under the microscope. In addition, we shall collect the molecular
profiles (unique characteristics of your tumour (biomarkers). These biomarkers can
be genes or other molecules that signal if a disease is present) of all soft-tissue
tumours from this study so we can rapidly learn more about the molecular profiles of
these types of tumours in children.

The European investigators discuss together the outcomes of all the examinations
done with your tumour tissue and blood. If there are results that could lead to a
better treatment for you, we shall inform your treating doctor of this. The doctor is
likely to adjust the treatment appropriately and discuss the findings with you.
It is also possible that this study will not produce any results that would be useful for
adjusting your treatment. In that case, you will not benefit from participating in his
study. But your participation in this study will contribute to our knowledge about soft-
tissue tumours in children and young adults.


4   HOW MANY PARTICIPANTS WILL BE INCLUDED IN THIS STUDY?

A total of 250 children/young adults will participate in this study. 20 participants will
be included in Belgium.


5   WHAT IS THE DURATION OF THIS STUDY?

The study will last four years, with the majority of the time consisting of registering
data and follow-up treatments. We call this the follow-up phase. In this phase you do
not have to come to the hospital for additional visits, you come to the hospital for a
check-up as usual once every three or six months.

6     WHICH PROCEDURES WILL BE PERFORMED IN THE CONTEXT OF THE
      STUDY?


6.1   Procedures:

In this study we perform research on a piece of your tumour tissue. This tissue has
already been taken or will be taken to establish the diagnosis. The research consists
of several parts. Participation in part B is required for participation in the study. For
the other parts in the study, you can choose whether you want to participate.

Part A: in this part we perform additional research on the genetic material in your
tumour (the molecular profile). This can help establish the diagnosis and helps us to
learn more about how soft tissue tumours develop.

Part B: In the laboratory of the hospital in Lyon (other) research will be performed on
the genetic material of your tumour. In addition, international experts will look under
a microscope at the cells of your tumour. These international experts will then
discuss if they agree with the diagnosis that was made in your country.

Part C: Part C of the study is the part in which Belgium does not participate.

Part D: In this part, we want to see if we can measure certain pieces of the genetic
material from your tumour in your blood. If you participate in this part, we will take an
extra blood sample during your treatment and after your treatment has been
completed. These blood withdrawals will be combined with regular blood withdrawals
as much as possible.

Part E: In this part, we examine your tumour after you have received treatment. We
will examine the molecular profile of your tumour, too see if it has changed during
your treatment.

You will be asked to give separate consent for each part of this study. Part B is
mandatory to be able to participate in the study. In addition, you can consent to
Parts A, D and E individually.

All these studies serve to improve our familiarity with and understanding of the
tumour and its behaviour during treatment. In addition, the molecular examinations
may sometimes produce starting points for new medicines that may act on the
tumour. In this way, we are trying to improve the chance of curing children and
young adults with a soft-tissue tumour in the future.

As described above, we want to examine a piece of tumour tissue for this study. The
tumour tissue will be sent to a number of laboratories in Europe for further
examination. these laboratories are the hospital laboratory in Centre Léon Bérard in
Lyon (France), the Princess Máxima Centre in Utrecht (the Netherlands) and in
some cases also the Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù in Rome (Italy).

Extra blood sample collections for scientific research (Part D)

At a number of time points, we would like to collect extra blood samples for Part D of
this scientific research.
For this part of the study, we want to collect about 5-10 ml more blood each time:
        • At diagnosis
        • During the treatment, when the scans are done. Usually for children, we do
        this 2 to 4 times during the treatment
        • At the end of the treatment
        • At the moment that the disease recurs, if it does

We will combine the extra blood sample collections for the study with regular blood
sample collections where possible. With most patients, we use the central line (an
infusion placed in a large vein) or the Port-A-Cath (PAC = a device used to draw
blood and give treatments, including intravenous fluids, blood transfusions, or drugs
such as chemotherapy and antibiotics) for access; that’s a catheter positioned in a
major vein under your skin. Occasionally, you may need to be pricked again for
these collections.

We ask you to give separate consent for the blood sampling collections. If you do not
consent to participate in this part of the study, you can still participate in the other
parts of the study.

In addition, this study requires a blood sample to look at the genetic material (DNA)
of the healthy cells.

As already mentioned, genetic tests will be performed on your samples for this study
to find out what the genes in your cells look like (these genes form the 'code' in the
cells that makes the cells know what to do and how to do it. This code is called a
cell's 'DNA' or 'genetic material' and is uniquely constructed in each person).
Possible genetic changes that can be found are very important to help us determine
your treatment and thus provide essential information for the study. If you don’t want
these analyses to be carried out, you cannot take part in the study.


Incidental findings

It may happen by chance and in addition to the trial objectives, that the results of the
analysis of your child’s biological samples reveal information that may be important
to your child’s health or the health of your child’s blood relatives. These data are
called ”incidental findings”. The sponsor will inform the investigator. With your
consent the investigator will notify you and your child’s treating physician about your
child’s results and potential consequences. If necessary, the investigator and/or the
treating physician will advise you on the next steps.

Where can you find more information about the study?

You can find more information about the study on the following website(s):
www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN12831761 . Once the study has been completed, this
website can publish a summary of the results of this study. You will find the study by
searching for number ‘ISRCTN12831761’.

What will happen after the study?

The investigator will let you know if new information about the study becomes
available that could be important for you to know. The investigator will then ask you if
you want you to continue taking part. You will stop participating in the following
situations:
    • The entire study has reached an end.
    • You want to stop taking part in the study. This is possible at any time. Please
      tell the investigator if you decide this. You do not need to say why you are
      stopping.
    • One of the following authorities decides that the study must stop:
            o the sponsor Princess Máxima Center,
            o the government, or
            o the medical ethics committee that evaluates the study.

The entire study has been completed once all the participants have finished, all tests
have been done by the laboratory, and all data collected.

After participation in the study, you will be checked according to standard of care.
Participation in the study does not influence the treatment and check-ups after the
end of the treatment.

Once all the data have been processed, the treating doctor will inform you about the
most important results of the entire study. Would you prefer not to know them?
Please tell your treating doctor about this choice. Then they will not inform you.

May we use your data and bodily materials for other research?

Your data and any leftover bodily materials can still be useful for other scientific
research into the field of soft-tissue tumours after this study has been completed.
Therefore, the data and bodily material samples will be stored in the hospital and the
involved laboratories for 25 years after the end of the study.
Any further research with data or (leftover) bodily material is always first proposed to
a committee. This scientific committee evaluates whether the data and/or bodily
material may be used. The committee will evaluate whether the research is designed
well and whether the objective fits the MyKids research. We will collaborate only with
others after careful consideration and after agreements are defined within a contract.

You can indicate on the assent form whether you agree to this. You don't agree with
this? Then you can still take part in this study.

7   WHAT IS EXPECTED FROM YOU?

If you decide to participate in this study, we ask you to cooperate with the researcher
and that you follow his/her instructions carefully. In particular, we ask you to respect
the planned study appointments.


8   WHAT ARE YOUR RIGHTS AND WHAT HAPPENS IF YOU DO NOT
    PARTICIPATE IN THIS STUDY?

Your participation in this study is entirely voluntary; there can be no obligation of any
kind. You can refuse to participate in the study and you can withdraw from the study
at any time without having to give a reason and without this affecting in any way your
treatment or future relationship with the investigator or treating doctor. This will also
not have a negative impact on the quality of care and your future follow-up.

Your participation in this study will be terminated if the doctor believes that this is in
your interest. You may also be withdrawn prematurely from the study by the
investigator if you don’t follow the procedures described in this information sheet
properly or if you don’t respect the items described.

If your participation in the study is terminated, the pseudonymized data already
collected before your withdrawal will be retained. Pseudonymisation is a process in
which information that relates to you, is changed to a number that has no meaning
so that it is impossible to see who the information relates to. This ensures the validity
of the study. No new data will be passed on to the sponsor.

This study was evaluated by the Ethics Committee of the Cliniques Universitaires
Saint Luc, which issued a favorable opinion after consulting the Ethics Committees
of each Belgian center where the study will be conducted. This study is therefore
being conducted in accordance with national legislation. Your data will be treated
confidentially and you will receive a code to protect your privacy. These
pseudonymized data may then be shared with other researchers for future studies
related to a disease/treatment similar or identical to yours.

These studies may be conducted in Belgium or elsewhere. A new study of this type
must always be approved by an ethics committee. If you do not want your data to be
used for future research, you can inform your parents or the research team.

Your doctor will also ensure that you are covered by insurance to protect you in the
event of damage and/or injury resulting from your participation in this study (Amlin
Corporate Insurance, Policy No. LXX002596, broker details: (MS Amlin Insurance
SE, Boulevard du Roi Albert II, 37 - 1030 Brussels, Tel +32 (0)2 894 70 00,
www.msamlin.com). You are welcome to ask questions about the study at any time.

9   WHAT ARE THE RISKS AND EXPECTED BENEFITS OF PARTICIPATING IN
    THIS STUDY?

Your participation in the study can serve to better help patients in the future.

When participating in this study, you will be treated in the standard manner.

You may have to undergo extra blood sample collections (for which a separate
consent must be given). With most patients, we use the central line or the PAC for
blood sampling. We will also combine blood sample collections for the study with
regular blood sample collections when possible.


Potential advantages of the study participation:

•      Your participation in the study can contribute to increasing our knowledge
about soft-tissue tumours in children. The data and results of this study can improve
the treatment of children and young adults in the future.
•      Because many additional examinations are done and international experts are
monitoring the results, we may come to learn more about your disease and refine the
diagnosis. In individual cases we may find out something that enables the treating
doctor to provide a better therapy for you.


It is also possible that other risks and side effects occur that are currently unknown.
It is therefore of great importance to report every new health complaint you may
have to the doctor-investigator as soon as possible, regardless of whether you think
the complaint relates to the study or not.

Potential disadvantages of the study participation:

•      You may have to undergo extra blood sample collections. They are not
mandatory, and separate consent will be requested for this. With most patients, we
use the central line or the PAC for blood sampling. We will also combine blood
sample collections for the study with regular blood sample collections where
possible. Very occasionally, it is not possible to combine the collection with another
blood sampling (for example, because you did not have to be pricked for a long
time), so then you will have to be pricked again for those collections.

You have the right to ask questions at any time about the possible and/or known
risks of this study. If information comes to light during the course of the study, that
could affect your willingness to continue your participation in this study, you will be
notified. If you do experience any disadvantage as a result of your participation in the
study, you will receive an appropriate treatment.
