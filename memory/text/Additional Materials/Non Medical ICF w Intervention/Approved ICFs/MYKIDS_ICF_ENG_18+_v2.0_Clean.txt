Information sheet for the adult participants in a clinical study

Title of the study: MYKIDS

Official title: MyKids: Molecular identification and characterisation of non-rhabdomyosarcoma soft-tissue sarcomas in children, adolescents and young adults: an EpSSG NRSTS study.

Version 2.0 Dated 25 March 2025

Dear,

With this information letter, we want to ask you whether you are willing to take part in medical scientific research. 

Before you decide to participate in this study, take sufficient time to read this information sheet carefully and discuss this with the investigator, medical team or other people of your choice. Please take time to ask questions if there are any uncertainties or if you require additional information. This process is called "informed consent" for participating in a clinical study. Once you have decided to participate in the study, you will be asked to sign the consent form at the end of this information sheet.

Participation is voluntary. You are receiving this letter because you were diagnosed with a soft-tissue tumour. 
This letter provides more information about the study, what this means for you and what the advantages and disadvantages are. This is a lot of information. Could you read the information and decide whether you want to take part? If you want to take part, you can complete the informed consent form found at the bottom of this information letter.

WHAT IS THE PURPOSE OF THIS STUDY?

You have been diagnosed with a soft-tissue tumour or there is a suspicion of a soft-tissue tumour. Soft tissues are, for example, the muscles, connective tissue, fatty tissue or the walls of blood vessels. Soft-tissue tumours can appear anywhere in the body. About half of the tumours in the soft tissues in children and young adults is a ‘rhabdomyosarcoma’. The other half consists of different, other, rare soft-tissue tumours. This study concerns this other half of soft-tissue tumours, the so-called 'non-rhabdomyosarcoma soft-tissue sarcomas (NRSTS)'.

Little is known about the origin of soft-tissue tumours, especially about this group with different types of rare soft-tissue tumours. 
The origin of many forms of cancer (including paediatric) lies in the tumour’s genetic material. This is also called its molecular profile (unique characteristics of your tumour (biomarkers). These biomarkers can be genes or other molecules that signal if a disease is present). For these reasons, more and more medicines are being developed that specifically target the particular aberrations in the genetic material (DNA) of tumour cells or in the material that the tumour cell needs to produce proteins (RNA).
The purpose of this study is to learn more about the origin and behaviour of rare soft-tissue tumours in children and young adults. We want to use this to improve the treatment of patients with a soft-tissue tumour in the future.

WHO ORGANIZES THE STUDY

This study is  set up as part of the ‘European Paediatric Soft tissue sarcoma Study Group’, abbreviated to ‘EpSSG’. This is a European collaboration of doctors and investigators who are actively working towards an improved treatment of children with a soft-tissue tumour. The study is  internationally coordinated by the Princess Máxima Center (hereafter referred to as the Sponsor). (Utrecht, Netherlands). The study will be performed by doctors in different hospitals in Europe and a number of countries outside Europe. In Belgium, the study will be performed in 7 hospitals. The national coordinating center will be Cliniques Universitaires Saint-Luc and Dr. de Ville will act as national coordinating investigator.

WHAT DOES PARTICIPATION IN THE STUDY INCLUDES FOR YOU?

The surgeon will remove the entire tumour or a small piece of tumour tissue (biopsy) from you under general anaesthesia (as standard of care procedure). This may already have taken place. In that case we shall request a piece of your tumour biopsy from the biobank, if it is stored there. A biobank is a facility where human body material (such as blood, urine, tissue samples…) is stored together with additional data related to this material. The tissue will be processed, placed under the microscope and evaluated by a pathologist. This results in a standard diagnosis. This biopsy or operation is standard, it would also be done if you don’t give consent to take part in this study. Once the diagnosis is made, you will be treated in the standard manner for the disease. The treating doctor will discuss the treatment plan with you. If you agree to this study, we will perform additional test on the same biopsy for study purpose. 

Along with the use of your bodily material sample (biopsy + blood), we register the data about the diagnosis, treatment and result of your treatment. We do this from the start of participation in this study, and we continue registering how you are doing for 5 years afterwards. 

The intention is to precise the diagnosis of soft-tissue tumours through international re-evaluation under the microscope. In addition, we shall collect the molecular profiles of all soft-tissue tumours from this study so we can rapidly learn more about the molecular profiles of these types of tumours in children and young adults.

The European investigators discuss together the outcomes of all the examinations done with your tumour tissue and blood. If there are results that could lead to a better treatment for you, we shall inform your treating doctor of this. The doctor is likely to adjust the treatment appropriately and discuss the findings with you. It is also possible that this study will not produce any results that would be useful for adjusting your treatment. In that case, you will not benefit from participating in this study. But your participation in this study will contribute to our knowledge about soft-tissue tumours in children and young adults.

HOW MANY PARTICIPANTS WILL BE INCLUDED IN THIS STUDY?

A total of 250 children/young adults will participate in this study. 20 participants will be included in Belgium.

WHAT IS THE DURATION OF THIS STUDY?

The study will last four years, with the majority of the time consisting of registering data and follow-up treatments. We call this the follow-up phase. In this phase you do not have to come to the hospital for additional visits, you come to the hospital for a check-up as usual once every three or six months. 
Your treating doctor will check you according to standard of care, no extra examinations or measurements will be done. We use the data from these check-up visits for the study. The entire study will take about eight years in total.

WHICH PROCEDURES WILL BE PERFORMED IN THE CONTEXT OF THE STUDY?

Procedures:

In this study we want to examine these rare tumours thoroughly in the laboratory. Various components will be examined:

Part A.	The genetic material in your tumour cells will be mapped fully. We call this the ‘molecular profile’. This information can help with confirming the diagnosis and understanding how this kind of soft-tissue tumour develop.

Part B.	To diagnose a soft-tissue tumour, the pathologist examines a piece of tissue under a microscope. This can be very complicated for this kind of rare tumour. Within this study European pathology experts will repeat this examination (under the microscope) to confirm the diagnosis that was made. Factors that predict whether the tumour will grow rapidly or slowly will also be sought. That is still a question for research. Results may thus not yet be included when determining the best treatment for you.

Part C.	Part C of the study is the part in which Belgium does not participate.

Part D.	In blood samples, we want to look for pieces of the tumour’s genetic material (tumour markers). We call this Liquid Biopsy. In the future we want to use this technique to detect tumours easily in the blood, for example in the phase after concluding the treatment if children undergo regular check-ups in the follow-up phase. 

Part E.	If the tumour is first pre-treated with chemotherapy, an operation is done later to remove the tumour. We would like to examine the molecular profile of tissue from the removed tumour to see if anything has changed during the treatment. 

You will be asked to give separate consent for each part of this study. Part B is mandatory  to be able to participate in the study. In addition you can  consent  to Parts A, D and E individually. 

All these studies serve to improve our familiarity with and understanding of the tumour and its behaviour during treatment. In addition, the molecular examinations may sometimes produce starting points for new medicines that may act on the tumour. In this way, we are trying to improve the chance of curing children and young adults with a soft-tissue tumour in the future.

Collection of biological samples:

As described above, we want to examine a piece of tumour tissue for this study. The tumour tissue will be sent to a number of laboratories in Europe for further examination. These laboratories are the hospital laboratory in Centre Léon Bérard in Lyon (France), the Princess Máxima Center in Utrecht (the Netherlands) and in some cases also the Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù in Rome (Italy). 

Extra blood sample collections for scientific research(Part D)

At a number of time points, we would like to collect extra blood samples for scientific research. With new techniques we can measure tiny amounts of the tumour’s genetic material in the blood. We want to investigate whether this technique could be used for rare soft-tissue tumours as well. We measure this during and after the treatment. We investigate whether this says anything about the result of the treatment and the chance of a cure. 

For this part of the study, we want to collect about 5-10 ml more blood each time:
• At diagnosis
• 	During the treatment, when the scans are done. Usually, we do this 2 to 4 times during the treatment
• At the end of the treatment
• At the moment that the disease recurs, if it does

We will combine the extra blood sample collections for the study with regular blood sample collections where possible. With most patients, we use the central line (an infusion placed in a large vein) or the Port-A-Cath (PAC= a device used to draw blood and give treatments, including intravenous fluids, blood transfusions, or drugs such as chemotherapy and antibiotics) for access; that’s a catheter positioned in a major vein under your skin. Occasionally, you may need to be pricked again for these collections. 

Participation in this part of the study is not mandatory. You have to consent separately for it. If you do not consent to your participation in this part of the study, you can still participate in the other parts of the study. 

Hereditary diseases 

Within this research it is standard practice to examine the genetic material of healthy cells as well. This allows the tumour tissue to be compared with the healthy cells.  
For this we also need a small amount of extra blood, about 5-10 ml. This blood sampling can usually be combined with a blood sample done for standard of care procedures.

Information about hereditary conditions can be burdensome for you and your family. If you find it difficult to decide whether you would want to know such information, you can talk at this stage with a clinical geneticist to help you come to a decision. Your treating doctor will refer you. Any finding that concerns a particular hereditary condition should always be confirmed by a laboratory specialising in hereditary conditions. The clinical geneticist will supervise this process. This genetic examination within the study is definitely not a replacement for genetic testing by a clinical geneticist.

Incidental findings
It may happen by chance and in addition to the trial objectives, that the results of the analysis of your child’s biological samples reveal information that may be important to your child’s health or the health of your child’s blood relatives. These data are called ”incidental findings”. The sponsor will inform the investigator. With your consent the investigator will notify you and your child’s treating physician about your child’s results and potential consequences. If necessary, the investigator and/or the treating physician will advise you on the next steps.

The entire study has reached an end.

The entire study has been completed once all the participants have finished, all tests have been done by the laboratory, and all data collected.

After participation in the study, you will be checked according to standard of care. Participation in the study does not influence the treatment and check-ups after the end of the treatment. 

Will you receive the results of the study?

If there are results that could lead to a better treatment for you, we shall inform your treating doctor of this. The doctor is likely to adjust the treatment appropriately and discuss the findings with you. 

Once all the data have been processed, the treating doctor will inform you about the most important results of the entire study. Would you prefer not to know them? Please tell your treating doctor about this choice. Then they will not inform you.

May we use your data and bodily materials for other research?

Your data and any leftover bodily materials can still be useful for other scientific research into the field of soft-tissue tumours after this study has been completed. Therefore, the data and bodily material samples will be stored in the hospital and the involved laboratories for 25 years after the end of the study. 
Any further research with data or (leftover) bodily material is always first proposed to a committee. This scientific committee evaluates whether the data and/or bodily material may be used. The committee will evaluate whether the research is designed well and whether the objective fits the MyKids research. We will collaborate only with others after careful consideration and after agreements are defined within a contract.

You can indicate on the informed consent form whether you agree to this. You don't agree with this? Then you can still take part in this study.
WHAT IS EXPECTED FROM YOU?

If you decide to participate in this study, we ask you to cooperate with the researcher and that you follow his/her instructions carefully. In particular, we ask you to respect the planned study appointments.

WHAT ARE YOUR RIGHTS WHEN PARTICIPATING IN THIS STUDY?
Rights when participating in the study

Your participation in this study is entirely voluntary; there can be no obligation of any kind. You can refuse to participate in the study and you can withdraw from the study at any time without having to give a reason and without this affecting in any way your treatment or future relationship with the investigator or treating doctor. This will also not have a negative impact on the quality of care and your future follow-up.

Your participation in this study will be terminated if the doctor believes that this is in your interest. You may also be withdrawn prematurely from the study by the investigator if you don’t follow the procedures described in this information sheet properly or if you don’t respect the items described.

If your participation in the study is terminated, the pseudonymized data  already collected before your withdrawal will be retained. Pseudonymisation is a process in which information that relates to you, is changed to a number that has no meaning so that it is impossible to see who the information relates to. This ensures the validity of the study. No new data will be passed on to the sponsor.

This study was evaluated by the Ethics Committee of Cliniques Universitaires Saint Luc which has issued a favorable opinion after consulting the Ethics Committees of each Belgian center where the study will be conducted. Ethics committees are tasked with protecting those who participate in clinical trials. They check whether your rights as a patient and as a participant in a study are respected, whether - based on current knowledge - the balance of risks and benefits is favorable for participants, whether the study is scientifically relevant and ethical. In this regard, the Ethics Committees issue an opinion in accordance with the Belgian law of May 7, 2004. Under no circumstances should you consider the positive opinion of the Ethics Committees as an incentive to participate in this study.

In accordance with the Belgian law of August 22, 2002, relating to the rights of the patient, the General Data Protection Regulation (or GDPR) (EU) 2016/679 of April 27, 2016 and the Belgian law of July 30 2018, on the protection of individuals related to the processing of personal data and on the free movement of such data your privacy will be respected and you can have access to the collected data about you if you want. Princes Maxima Center, Utrecht, The Netherlands, as sponsor, even in the absence of fault, is liable for any damages incurred by you as a participant - or in the event of death your beneficiaries - which are directly or indirectly related to his/her participation in this study.

He or she can provide you with additional information about possible treatments. WHAT ARE THE RISKS AND EXPECTED BENEFITS OF PARTICIPATING IN THIS STUDY?

Your participation in the study can serve to better help patients in the future.

When participating in this study, you will be treated in the standard manner.



You may have to undergo extra blood sample collections (for which a separate consent must be given). With most patients, we use the central line or the PAC for blood sampling. We will also combine blood sample collections for the study with regular blood sample collections when possible.

Potential advantages of the study participation:

•	Your participation in the study can contribute to increasing our knowledge about soft-tissue tumours in children. The data and results of this study can improve the treatment of children and young adults in the future.
•	Because many additional examinations are done and international experts are monitoring the results, we may come to learn more about your disease and refine the diagnosis. In individual cases we may find out something that enables the treating doctor to provide a better therapy for you.

It is also possible that other risks and side effects occur that are currently unknown. It is therefore of great importance to report every new health complaint you may have to the doctor-investigator as soon as possible, regardless of whether you think the complaint relates to the study or not.

Potential disadvantages of the study participation:

•	You may have to undergo extra blood sample collections. They are not mandatory, and separate consent will be requested for this. With most patients, we use the central line or the PAC for blood sampling. We will also combine blood sample collections for the study with regular blood sample collections where possible. Very occasionally, it is not possible to combine the collection with another blood sampling (for example, because you did not have to be pricked for a long time), so then you will have to be pricked again for those collections.

You have the right to ask questions at any time about the possible and/or known risks of this study. If information comes to light during the course of the study, that could affect your willingness to continue your participation in this study, you will be notified. If you do experience any disadvantage as a result of your participation in the study, you will receive an appropriate treatment.

ARE COSTS ASSOCIATED WITH YOUR PARTICIPATION IN THIS STUDY?

If you decide to participate in this study, there will be no additional costs for you or your insurance company. The visits and procedures belonging to this study, which are mentioned in the description of the conduct of the study in section 6  will be paid by the sponsor. Only costs related to usual medical services in your clinical situation may be charged to you.

IS THERE A REIMBURSEMENT FOR PARTICIPATION IN THIS STUDY?

You will not receive any financial compensation or reimbursement of travel costs for your participation in this study.
