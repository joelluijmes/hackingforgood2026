About the study

This study is being conducted in several European countries and in some countries outside Europe. A total of roughly 250 children will participate in this study. Approximately <number> children will take part in <country> . The study has been checked and approved. The name of the committee that evaluated the study is the <name local MREC / IRB> .

Why this study?

The goal of this study is to learn more about the origin and behaviour of rare tumours in the soft tissues in children and young adults. We want to use this to improve the treatment of patients with these tumours in the future.

Background

A tumour has been found in your soft tissues or there is a suspicion of a soft-tissue tumour. Soft tissues are, for example, your muscles, connective tissue, fatty tissue or the walls of blood vessels. These tumours can appear anywhere in the body. This study covers different kinds of rare tumours in soft tissues. Little is known about the origin of these tumours. We also do not know what causes these tumours to grow and how they behave.

The surgeon will remove a small piece of tumour tissue (biopsy) from you under general anaesthetic or remove the tumour. This may already have happened. In that case we shall request a piece of the tumour biopsy from the biobank, if it is stored there. The tissue will be processed, placed under the microscope and evaluated by a pathologist. This results in the diagnosis. This biopsy or operation is standard, it would also be done if you don't take part in this study. After the diagnosis has been made, the doctor will discuss a treatment plan with you and your parents.

How does participation work?

In this study we perform research on a piece of your tumour tissue. This tissue has already been taken or will be taken to establish the diagnosis. The research consists of several parts. Participation in part B is required for participation in the study. For the other parts in the study, you can choose whether you want to participate.

Part A: in this part we perform additional research on the genetic material in your tumour (the molecular profile). This can help establish the diagnosis and helps us to learn more about how soft tissue tumours develop.

Part B: In the laboratory of the hospital in Lyon (other) research will be performed on the genetic material of your tumour. In addition, international experts will look under a microscope at the cells of your tumour. These international experts will then discuss if they agree with the diagnosis that was made in your country.

Part C: in this part we investigate whether tumour cells can grow outside the body (as a mini-tumour, or 'organoid'). This allows us to perform extra research to learn more about the origin, growth and behaviour of soft tissue tumours.

Part D: In this part, we want to see if we can measure certain pieces of the genetic material from your tumour in your blood. If you participate in this part, we will take an extra blood sample during your treatment and after your treatment has been completed. These blood withdrawals will be combined with regular blood withdrawals as much as possible.

Part E: In this part, we examine your tumour after you have received treatment. We will examine the molecular profile of your tumour, too see if it has changed during your treatment.

Along with the bodily material, we store the data about the diagnosis, treatment and result of the treatment. We do this during the treatment and for 4 years after the start of your participation in this study.

The European investigators discuss together the outcomes of all the examinations done with the tumour tissue and blood. If there are results that could lead to a better treatment for you, we shall inform your doctor of this. Your doctor will likely want to adjust your treatment appropriately and discuss this with you and your parents. It is also possible that this study will not produce any results that would be useful for adjusting your treatment or changing your diagnosis. In that case, you will not benefit from participating in this study. Your participation in this study does contribute to our knowledge about these rare tumours in children and young adults.

Other investigators may request information from this study for further research. This data will only be shared with other investigators after approval from the MyKids study investigators.

Extra blood sample collections for scientific research:

At a number of time points, we would like to collect extra blood samples for part D of this scientific research. For this part of the study, we want to collect about 5-10 ml more blood each time:

- At diagnosis

- During the treatment, when the scans are done. Usually for children, we do this 2 to 4 times during the treatment

- At the end of the treatment

- At the moment that the disease recurs, if it does

We will combine the extra blood sample collections for the study with regular blood sample collections where possible. With most children, we use the central line or the Port-A-Cath (PAC) for access, that’s a catheter positioned in a major vein under the skin. Very occasionally, you may need to be pricked again for these collections.

We ask you to give separate consent for the blood sampling collections. If you do not consent to participate in this part of the study, you can still participate in the other parts of the study.

In addition, this study requires a blood sample to look at the genetic material (DNA) of the healthy cells.

Hereditary diseases

When examining the DNA of the tumour, it is standard practice to examine the DNA of healthy cells as well. This allows your tumour tissue to be compared with your healthy cells. In very rare cases we find a predisposition for a disease in your healthy cells. We refer to this as genetic information.

If a hereditary condition is found that has no direct influence on your treatment, you can indicate separately on the consent form if you want to know this.

If an hereditary condition is found that does affect your medical care, your treating doctor will discuss this with you and your parents. If you wish, your treating doctor will make an appointment for you with a doctor specialising in hereditary diseases for further explanation and guidance.

Advantages and disadvantages:

It is important that you carefully consider the potential advantages and disadvantages before deciding to take part in this study.

Advantages:

- Your participation in the study produces more knowledge about soft-tissue tumours. The data and results of this study can improve the treatment of children in the future.

- Because many additional examinations are done and international experts are reviewing the results, we may come to learn more about your tumour. In individual cases we may find out something that enables the doctor to treat you better.

Disadvantages:

- If you consent to it, we shall collect a small amount of extra blood several times (part D). It is up to you to decide about these extra blood samples. You will be asked to consent separately for this. Usually, we can collect the blood during a standard blood sampling, so you would not have to be pricked again in that case. Very occasionally, it is not possible to combine the extra blood sample collection with another blood sampling (because you did not have to be pricked for a long time), so then you will have to be pricked again for those collections. Then the additional visit to the hospital for this will take more of your time.

Payment

You will not receive payment for taking part.

Your rights

Do you have to take part?

No, you decide whether you want to take part. If you don't want to take part, then you don't need to, even if your parents would like you to take part. If you want to take part, please sign the form.

Even after you have signed the form, you can always decide to stop if you don’t want to continue. Please tell the doctor about this. You don't need to say why you are stopping.

Withdrawing consent

Please tell the doctor or the study nurse if you want to stop taking part. This is called: withdrawing your consent. We will still use the information that has already been collected for the study.
